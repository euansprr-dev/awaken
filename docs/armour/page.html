The Forged Armour

Armour you forge yourself at your Forge, in 29 sets across the ranks, each with its own shape, its own way to fight and a look that wakes as you master it. Below is the first set, Lordscale, already drawn in the real game.

What you asked for

"It should make really awesome armor... Looking really cool is the biggest one: really epic, not the boring flat vanilla look."

  • The look first. Every set is sculpted in 3D with its own silhouette, metal that shines, seams that glow, a cape and cloth that swing. It looks the same on you, on a stand and on other players.
  • Made at your Forge, as a game. Heat the metal, strike it on the beat, quench it, fit it on the stand. Skill makes it better, never makes it fail.
  • Powers, never cheats. Abilities change how you fight and move. Nothing shows creatures, ores or blocks through walls.
  • On par with weapons. Four sets at E, five at every rank from D to S.

Why today's armour feels flat

  • Vanilla armour is a picture wrapped on a box half a pixel off the body. Painting it better can't add depth. The horns and crowns on Lordscale and Sovereign Weave today are plain boxes stuck on.
  • One set a rank is a stat ladder. There is nothing to choose.
  • Armour comes from a crafting grid. The Forge only tempers.
  • Most set bonuses are invisible numbers, and the one you can feel, Hunter's Sense, shows creatures through walls.

The weapons had the same problem and were fixed by sculpted meshes and their own shader. The armour gets that path, with its own kit.

The look, proven in the game

One hero set, Lordscale (the A set), made in the new armour kit and drawn in the real game: on a stand beside vanilla netherite, from behind, from the side, by night, worn by a player, walking, and the gauntlet in first person. 1,024 triangles for the whole set. No mixin; nothing about the renderer changed.

It took four rounds in the game to get here. The first drew nothing but a flat hat, because the game only hands its own kind of armour to the body; that's fixed. The second worked but looked squat and toy-like next to netherite: the helm was twice the size of the head, the shoulders drooped and gold rings lined up across the waist. So the helm now sits close to the head and gets its height from the horns, the shoulders rise, and the gold is kept for edges that lead the eye. The last round gave the forearm, the part you see most, its gold corners and molten line.

Powers you feel, never cheats

The rule: armour may change your body, your blows and your movement, and what everyone in front of you can see. It never shows you anything hidden. No outlines, no glowing creatures, no ores through stone.

  • Two pieces of a set give its way's smaller bonus. Four give its power plus the set's own twist. Sets still mix.
  • Each set has a twist. Wildhide's Pounce (a long leap from sneaking that lands hard) replaces Hunter's Sense. Bellguard rings like a bell when you guard perfectly and staggers those close. Rootwarden roots you while you cast.
  • Mastery I to V. A set grows as you fight in it. II: its trims brighten. III: its seams glow, and pulse with your heartbeat when you're hurt. IV: it grows (longer horns, a longer cape, an extra crest). V: its own aura, like embers rising off Lordscale. Others see it.

The Forge Song: making armour

From D up you make armour at your Forge. The Emberkits keep the fire hot and work the bellows; the hammer is yours. At E, before the Forge, you make it by hand at the works table.

LayPick the piece. The materials leave your pack and land on the bench.
HeatThe bar glows dull red, orange, yellow. Let go when it's in the gold band. Too early, your strikes count for less. Too late, a scale flaw shows on the finished piece.
ShapeThe piece's outline glows on the bar. Strike the marks on the beat of the set's own rhythm, 6 to 12 strikes. The metal takes the shape strike by strike, and every clean strike plays a note of the set's melody.
QuenchPlunge it in the trough on the cue. Steam and a hiss.
FitIt goes on the Fitting Stand. The last piece of a set completes the suit, and the set wakes: the seams light, the cape unfurls, the camera circles once.

  • Each rank adds a move: C inlay a crystal (picks the accent colour), B tune Deepglass by its ring, A fold netherite with paired strikes, S starfire's fast fading marks.
  • Quality: Plain, Fine, Masterwork, Flawless. It sets the piece's starting temper, and the Forge tempers on from there as today.
  • Still fun the 20th time: a piece takes 20 to 40 seconds, each set has its own rhythm to learn, and a piece you've made at Masterwork can be forged from memory in 6 seconds. A friend at your anvil can take the off-beats.
  • Cinematic camera by default (wide, over the shoulder, close on the metal, low for the quench, around the stand), first person in settings.
  • Never lost: leave or log out mid-piece and the work stays on the anvil.
