The Armoury

The weapons are built. The way to them is invisible. This plan gives every hunter a first weapon at the High Gate, tells them where every part comes from, and gives each of the 16 styles a person or a place that teaches it. Nothing is built until you approve it.

What we found

The Fang

Nothing says what it is

The tooltip reads "E-rank Hunter gear · Wildhide". It never says it is a blade, that every forged weapon starts from one, or what makes it. Blueprints and the Forge have no tooltip at all.

The Blueprint

It hides behind iron

You draw one by sneaking at a Forge with an empty hand. The Forge costs 7 iron, and a Blueprint needs lapis from a cave. Early gear is meant to need no iron, and nothing tells you any of this.

Essence

It was never the problem

Creatures already drop Essence Shards: about 0.6 a kill from a night hunter, 0.25 from a day animal. The whole first kit is about 7. The game just never says so, and our own guide page leaves creatures out.

One correction to the brief: I checked the loot tables and 182 of 199 creatures drop essence. The first kit is a night of hunting, not a gate.

The first hour

  1. 0:15

    Hale stops you at the High Gate. His rack holds three weapons. You take one: Daggers (fast), Axe (wide cuts) or Maul (slow and heavy). You get the finished E weapon, its Blueprint and a spare Fang, taken from his hand.

    "Hold on. You're not going out there with nothing in your hands. The Association gives every new hunter one weapon. Take whichever one you like. They're all fine."

  2. 0:20

    Ilse gives you the Ember, as today. If you skipped Hale, she hands you the same issue in his name.

  3. first night

    The first Essence Shard drops, and a card says what it is. Night hunters drop about one for every two kills. A rule adds a shard on the fifth kill in a row that gave none, so bad luck never stalls you.

  4. 0:30

    Level 3 opens the armour recipes, and the toast now says what to bring: hide from creatures, a bone or flint, a string or feather, and one shard for each piece. The Armoury appears in your band, with a Track button for the parts you still need.

  5. ~1 h

    The full E set takes about 15 hide and 7 shards. You are ready for the first gate, and your first Marked weapon can drop from an elite.

"How would I ever know that?"

Fair question, and the first version of this plan had no answer for the nine places. The rule now: you are told at a place you will already be, never in a menu you have to go and find.

At a gate

The gate card says it

Gates are what hunters do anyway. Once the card shows the theme, it adds a line for a style you don't know: "The first elite here carries a pattern: Daggers." Nothing shows for a style you already have.

At a camp

The camp says it

A riftborn camp's name carries the same line when you come near it.

From a person

Their offer says it

"If you help me find the ten sheep, I'll show you how to use a spear. It keeps wolves off." The job is the door.

At Hale

The plaque, and a question

Each pattern on his rack reads "4 essence, or free: the first elite in a Hive gate". Ask him "Anything on patterns?" and he names the nearest home you have met, with a direction. At each new rank he says what he added.

A home you have met but can't use yet is filed under "What waits" in your band. Homes you haven't met are never listed. And you never have to find any of it: Hale sells every pattern, so a player who never learns that places teach only pays a few essence.

Every item says where it comes from

An item has to answer three things: what is it, what is it for, where do I get more. A test fails the build if any ingredient of any gear or weapon recipe has no "where" line.

Plain words, one set

Hide is the body of armour. Part is the hard heart: bone, flint, a horn, a beast's trophy. Binding is what holds it: string, feather, moss, later a metal ingot. Tooltips, JEI and the Armoury all use them.

JEI also gets a source line on every ingredient, a Blueprint source, an Armoury category and a fixed Essence page. The recipe book stops unlocking all 16 weapons from one bone.

The ladder, tier by tier

Each tier asks for one thing from each of three places: the hunt, the deep and the gates. A new tier is announced in three beats. First you see it locked, with what it waits for. Then it opens with a toast that names the job. Then Hale's board posts the job for its signature part, in plain words, naming the creature and the place.

No grind wall: a tier never needs more than one trophy, one metal run and essence you already earn. Every part slot takes several things, so one missed drop blocks nothing.

Finding and making both matter

Make it

Forged

The weapon of your tier in the style you choose. Reliable, and you can attune and temper it.

Try something new

Marked

From elites, Wardens and camp hearts: a forged weapon of your tier with a quirk you did not pick. Your first one is guaranteed. A pity rule means no long dry spell.

A keepsake

Named

A rumour tells you it exists. A place gives it to you. It has a name, a Mark or two and one plain line of history.

A trophy

Lord-forged

As today: the Lord's weapon and its signature move as the weapon's Art. 35% a Lord, certain by the third.

The link between them

Raise

At a Forge, lift a found weapon to your next tier for that tier's parts. It keeps its Marks, its Art, its attunement and its name. A Maul from the Bell Warden can stay yours from E to S, and a crafted weapon is what you reach for when you want a style at your tier right now. Neither is a strict upgrade of the other.

How hard it is

The combat sim says gear is the floor and skill is the lever. Against the Drowned Archivist at D-rank, a player who guards well but is not great wins 6% of the time. An expert with the same weapon wins 99%. A diamond sword only moves the first number to 12%. So the first gear can be generous, and nothing in this plan changes how hard a fight is.

One risk: the sim finds only 3 of 15 Lords inside their targets even for an expert, so kits from C up could slip if the Lords stay as they are. That waits for your three-Lord session.

Twelve decisions

Each has my recommendation. "Approve all as recommended" is a fine answer.

1

Fix the guidance first, on its own Recommended

Tooltips, plain words, a "where from" line on every ingredient with a test behind it, JEI, and the recipe-book fix. It answers "where do I get a Fang" without changing a number.

Instead: wait and ship everything together.

2

Hale hands over a first weapon Recommended

Pick Daggers, Axe or Maul at the High Gate. You get the finished weapon, its Blueprint and a spare Fang. Ilse covers it if you miss him. A free first weapon with a real choice is how Monster Hunter and Hades start.

Instead: hand nothing over, so everyone crafts their first.

3

The Armoury is the one door for Blueprints Recommended

Hale's rack sells them for essence only. Your Forge draws any pattern you know, with a list to choose from instead of a random cycle. Hale's yard gets a real rack of weapons. This also removes the iron wall from the first hour.

Instead: a menu at Hale with no rack, or the Forge only.

4

Every style has a home Recommended

Six people teach (Hettie, Pell, Marta, Gus, Wren, Oren) and nine places teach, when you beat the first elite of their theme. Each home tells you where you already are: the gate card, the camp, the person's offer, Hale's plaques and his "Anything on patterns?". A style you know pays its price in essence.

Instead: Hale's rack only. Cheaper, and no discovery.

5

Keep the blade names, add a plain type line Recommended

Fang, Cleaver, Sabre, Edge, Warblade and Oathblade are good names. They just need "Hunter blade" under them. Hide, Part and Binding become the words everywhere.

Instead: rename all six to "<Tier> Blade".

6

An Armoury page in the band, with Track Recommended

K, then Powers. It lists the styles you know, the tier cards with have and need, and the finds. Track puts the missing parts on your screen, and the Now line points at known places.

Instead: tooltips and JEI only.

7

Say where essence comes from, and never let it dry up Recommended

A card on the first shard. The fifth kill in a row with none drops one, at E and D rank. Ilse's Moth Lights and Marta's Wheel pay a few. It already drops from creatures, so this only fixes bad luck.

Instead: leave the rates as they are.

8

Tiers announce themselves Recommended

See it locked, then open it with a toast naming the job, then a job on Hale's board for the signature part. Plain speech that names the creature and the place.

Instead: keep the one generic toast.

9

Marked and named weapons Recommended

Keep the 4% and 10% rates. Add camp hearts at 10%, a pity (certain by the 25th), and a guaranteed first one. Named weapons are told by rumour and found at places, about 30 to start, built after the rest.

Instead: change the rates now. I would set them from a sim row first.

10

Raise found weapons at the Forge Recommended

A Marked or Lord-forged weapon can be lifted to your tier for that tier's parts, keeping what makes it special. This is how finding and making both stay worth it.

Instead: found weapons keep their tier and fall behind in about a rank.

11

Difficulty stance Recommended: confirm

Tiers stay one point of damage apart. No item-level multiplier. Nothing here touches a Lord's numbers. Those wait for your three-Lord session.

Instead: tell me which way you want it harder or easier.

12

Order Recommended

A and B first, then C with D beside it, then E and F. Named weapons wait. You play the first half hour after B.

Instead: tell me which part to hold or drop.
