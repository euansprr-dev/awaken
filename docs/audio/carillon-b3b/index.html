Carillon: the city's sound

AWAKEN · Carillon M4 B3b · 21 scenes (seven districts at morning, noon and night), 28 sounds alone

About 13 minutes. Each district has three moments. Play after: what you'd hear standing there with this build. Before is the same spot before it: only the Choir striking the hour, otherwise silence. Mark each moment keep, remake or cut, and say why in the note when it isn't a keep.

In the game every sound comes from a place: a quiet bed for the district (it follows how many people are outdoors there now, and turns into the night bed after dark), with fountains, stalls, forges, boats, birds, the Watch's handbell and the carillon at their own places and hours. Rendered here offline from the shipped files, so three things differ from the game: no Sound Physics echo down the lanes, plain stereo panning, and the animals' vanilla voices (cats, dogs, hooves) are left out because they can't be copied into the public repo. Headphones help.

The city's own sounds are synthesised; nature comes from AmbientSounds when you have it (below), and those recordings are heard in the game, not here. The weak spot is the crowd.

Your choices stay in this browser. When you're done, Export and send me the file (or the text).

Where to start, and my read

If you have ten minutes: the Market at noon (the city at its busiest), Garden Ward at 06:00 (the dawn chorus under the carillon), Lantern Quay at 22:00 (water, a tavern, boats at night). Then any district you mean to cut.

Read this first: these mixes were made in the cloud, where nothing can play sound. I haven't heard them. What follows comes from the measurements and the code, and your ears overrule all of it.

Recommendations

  1. Keep the structure: one quiet bed per district that thickens with the crowd and becomes the night bed after dark, with every other sound at its own place and hour. Each bed sits 6 to 12 dB under a stall or a fountain heard close, so the places carry the detail and the bed only holds the gaps. Measured mixes: about -35 dBFS when a district is quiet, up to -28 when the Market is packed.
  2. The crowd is the weak link. Its murmur is made of synthetic formant voices. Far off it reads as people; close up it may read as an imitation. AmbientSounds has no crowd, so this stays ours: mark the Market "remake" if it doesn't hold up and say what's wrong.
  3. Nature is now recorded where you have AmbientSounds (below): birds, crickets, owls, gulls, doves, wind and rain. Our synthetic birds and crickets step aside so nothing doubles.
  4. If the Market at noon sounds thin, say "louder" in its note. The packed level can rise about 3 dB and still pass the beds' test.
  5. Night, without AmbientSounds: our crickets' chirps fall on a regular pattern in the spectrogram. Over the 48 s loop that may be heard as a loop. If you hear it, mark the night "remake" and I'll scatter them.
  6. 06:00 is quiet on purpose. The forges start at 06:45 and the stalls at 07:00, so dawn is the bed, the birds and the carillon. Mark it "remake" if it feels empty rather than early.
  7. Rain: the bed drops a level, and AmbientSounds plays rain outdoors and mutes the rest under it. No awnings drip or gutters run yet; that's the next thing to add if you want rain to change the city itself.

Fixed while making this page

  • The carillon was a stereo file, so it played unplaced and equally loud anywhere within 420 blocks. It is now mono and comes from the spire, fading over 320 blocks. At the Wallring it is faint.
  • At 06:00, noon and 18:00 the Choir struck the hour at the same moment its carillon began, and the two played over each other (noon is twelve strokes over 28 s). Now the tune plays first and the hour is struck after it, as a clock tower does.

Not like the plan

Decision 28 put vanilla's sounds first. The game has almost nothing for a city, so this build synthesises the city's own sounds and borrows the rest by id: cats, dogs and hooves are vanilla's, and nature is AmbientSounds'. Neither is heard on this page. If you'd rather hear vanilla's water and fire under the fountains and forges, say so on those rows.
