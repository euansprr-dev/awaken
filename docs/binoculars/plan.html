Binoculars

Always on you, one key away. The hands lift a pair of binoculars to your eyes, the world pulls in with weight, the scroll wheel clicks through the magnification, and the glass tells you how far away the thing you're looking at is.

Plan · waiting for your OK · nothing built28 Oct 2026branch claude/binoculars-plan-6cb428SPEC §24 Hands, §20 Far Sight, §21.1 plates, §16.8 Atlas

In short

  • The zoom mods players love all agree on a few things: zoom that eases instead of snapping, scroll to set how far, the mouse slowing down in step with the zoom, and the hand and HUD out of the way. What they complain about is OptiFine's "cinematic" wobble, a spyglass you have to carry, and a fixed zoom you can't change.
  • I'll build all of that, then go further than any of them: real first-person hands raising real binoculars, our own lens edge, clicky magnification steps with a little settle, a short focus pull, gentle breathing sway that crouching steadies, and a range and compass readout in the glass.
  • Others see you lift the binoculars in third person, so it works in multiplayer. Nothing is saved on the server. No mixins.
  • Two cheap extras use systems you already have: right-click while looking to make that spot your Atlas destination, and see a creature's plate (name and level) out to about 160 blocks.
  • One honest limit: past your render distance, what you see is Far Sight's far land, and at 8× its shapes look 8× coarser. I've kept "Far focus" (Far Sight sharpening where you look) as a later choice, after you've seen it.

Try the feel

This is a stand-in for the game, drawn in the browser, to show the timing and behaviour. It isn't the final art. Press Raise or the I key, scroll (or use − and +) to step the zoom, drag to look around. Switch to Vanilla spyglass to compare.

1 · What you asked for

Your words: "research all of the best zoom mods and what people really like about them… create a custom zoom mod, but with binoculars… you always have it, and when you click a certain button, it uses the binoculars to zoom in. It should be really one of the best zoom mods in the world."

How I read it

  • The job: look far across AWAKEN's big world (ridges, ruins, gate pillars, a herd, the city) and decide where to go next, without opening the map.
  • The pain today: there's no zoom at all unless you craft and hold a vanilla spyglass, which gives one fixed 10× view, a hard black ring, a mouse slowed by a fixed 8 times, and no hands.
  • The experience you want: it feels like holding real binoculars, it's always there, and it's as good as the best zoom mods or better.
  • Your idea, kept as is: no item, one key, a binoculars animation. "Always have it" means it can't be lost, dropped or forgotten.
  • Things to check rather than guess: a free key, how far zoom can usefully reach with your render distance and Far Sight, and whether Lens, Camera Overhaul and Shoulder Surfing get along with a narrow view.

What good looks like

ResultWhat you'd notice
StrongYou reach for it without thinking. The raise feels like a body moving, the zoom lands with a click, aiming stays as easy as unzoomed, and you spot a ruin, read its distance and mark it in one go.
WeakA plain FOV zoom with a vignette. It works, but it reads as a mod bolted on, which fails your visual quality bar.
UnacceptableIt wobbles or lags behind the mouse (OptiFine's complaint), it eats the hotbar scroll, it leaves you zoomed in after a screen opens or you die, it catches you defenceless in a fight, or it costs frame rate when it's not in use.

Not in scope: a binoculars item to craft, scopes on weapons, photo mode, or seeing creatures beyond the range the server sends them (that would need new server traffic; see section 6).

4 · Three ways to build it

A. A good zoom key

FOV zoom on a key, scroll steps, zoom-matched mouse speed, a vignette, HUD hidden. About 2 hours.

Loses: no binoculars, no hands, no feel. It would be a copy of the mods, and it reads as an add-on.

B. Binoculars as a body action · recommended

Everything in A, done carefully, plus the hands raising real binoculars, our own lens edge and sound, detents with a settle, a focus pull, breathing sway, a readout, others seeing it, and two cheap extras.

Fits Hands, plates and the Atlas as they are. About 6 to 9 hours across stages 0 to 2.

C. A real optical render

A second camera pass for a true picture-in-picture lens with depth focus and distortion.

Loses: draws the world twice, which costs frame rate on your Mac, for something B gets close to with one cheap pass. Not worth it.

B wins because the binoculars are what make it AWAKEN's and not another zoom mod, and the pieces it needs already exist. What would change my mind: if the raise feels slow in play, the timing is data and comes down in one edit. If you'd rather have no hands at all, A is a subset of B.

5 · How it behaves

The flow

Press ITap to keep them up. Hold to peek; they drop when you let go.
Raise ~330 ms
What you hold goes away, both hands lift the binoculars to your eyes.
Eyecups
The binoculars fill the frame and the lens edge takes over. A soft leather-and-glass sound.
Pull in ~300 ms
The view eases to your last magnification and comes into focus.
Scroll
Each notch clicks one step, with a tiny settle and a short refocus at high steps.
Lower ~220 ms
Tap I again, attack, use, sprint, open a screen or get hit.

Settings and accessibility

  • Key mode: tap and hold (default), hold only, toggle only.
  • Look speed while zoomed: 100% means matched to the zoom; 50 to 150%.
  • Sway: on (off with reduced motion). Lens edge: twin, single, or off. Readout: on or off.
  • Reduced motion also halves the raise and lower times, removes the settle and the focus pull, and keeps the binoculars still.
  • The FOV slider and the FOV effects slider still work: zoom divides whatever FOV you'd have, so a speed boost stays a speed boost. Zoom is never scaled down by the FOV effects slider, because it's an action you chose, not an effect.

6 · How far you can see

Two things set the limit: which land is real blocks, and which creatures your game knows about.

StepView (vertical)A block 384 m awayFar Sight cell at 1 km
1× (your normal 70°)70°3 px10 px
2×39°7 px21 px
4×20°13 px41 px
8×10°27 px82 px
12×6.7°40 px123 px

Worked for your MacBook's 1,800-pixel-high picture. 384 m is render distance 24 at its edge. A Far Sight cell is 8 blocks at 1 km.

  • Real land (out to your render distance, 384 blocks at 24): crisp at any step. A block at the edge is 27 pixels at 8×. This is most of what you'll look at.
  • Far land (Far Sight, out to 16 km): its shapes are built so a cell is about a pixel at your normal view. At 8× each cell covers about 8× more, so ridges show straight-edged facets, softened by the haze. This is why the steps stop at 12×.
  • Creatures: the server only sends creatures within its tracking range: about 160 blocks for animals, 128 for monsters, and players to your view distance. At render distance 20 or more, vanilla already draws them all the way to that range, so no change is needed. Beyond it, nothing is there to draw. Showing creatures farther away would mean new server traffic; I don't propose it.
  • Far focus (optional, later): while you hold still at 6× or more, Far Sight works out a small extra patch of finer land around where you look, about 1 to 2 seconds of work on two background threads, so the far land sharpens like a focus wheel. It changes Far Sight's code, so I suggest deciding after you've seen 8× in play.

7 · The extras

Mark a spot · recommended

Right-click while looking makes the point at the centre your Atlas destination, named like the Atlas names spots, with its existing words: "That's your destination now." Uses Destinations with the kind spot, which the server already checks. No new saved data.

About 45 minutes. Out past loaded land it uses Far Sight's ground height, and in multiplayer (no Far Sight) it marks only within loaded land.

Read creatures at range · recommended

Hold the centre on a creature for half a second and its plate appears (name, level chip in rank colour, its health bar), out to about 160 blocks. Only the one you hold on, and only while the binoculars are up. Like Red Dead's "Study", with nothing to press.

About 45 minutes. Uses only what your game already knows about the creature, so nothing new comes from the server.

Real focus by depth · later

The focus pull becomes real: things nearer or farther than the centre blur a little, reading Lens's depth. About 0.3 to 0.5 ms while up, nothing otherwise.

2 to 3 hours. Best judged once the core is in your hands.

Far focus · later

Section 6. Far Sight sharpens the land where you hold your look.

3 to 5 hours, in Far Sight's module.

8 · Under the hood

Pieces

  • New module binoculars (dev.awaken.binoculars): BinocularsModule (the relay payload), client.Glass (the state: down, raising, up, lowering), client.GlassView (FOV, look speed, sway), client.GlassHands (a Hands stance and the raise and lower clips), client.GlassOverlay (lens edge, readout, focus pull), client.GlassOthers (the pose others see), client.Rangefinder.
  • Feel data in assets/awaken/binoculars/feel.json (schemaVersion 1): times, curve, steps, sway, settle. A broken file keeps the built-in numbers.
  • Art from tools/art/specs (model texture, lens edge, sounds). Clips in fp_moves.py, previewed with its preview command.
  • A display-only item awaken:binoculars so the hands and other players' models can draw it like any held thing. Not in any loot or recipe.
  • One public addition to SPEC §12: CameraHooks.zoom() (the current magnification, 1 when none) for Far Sight's panorama and anyone else who needs to know.
  • No mixins. Events used, each checked in the 26.3 sources: ViewportEvent.ComputeFov, CalculatePlayerTurnEvent, InputEvent.MouseScrollingEvent (cancelled while up), RenderGuiLayerEvent.Pre, MovementInputUpdateEvent, RegisterKeyMappingsEvent, and Hands' own FirstPerson API.

State and network

  • The binoculars' state is client-side. Your last step and settings live in a client config. Nothing is saved on the server and nothing changes in any save.
  • For others to see you: your client sends "up" or "down" when it changes (at most 4 a second). The server passes it to players tracking you and clears it on death, logout and dimension change. A server without it just doesn't pass it on; your zoom still works.
  • The server never trusts a client for anything that matters. Marking a spot goes through the destination request the server already checks.

Cost

  • Not in use: one key check a tick. No per-frame work.
  • In use: a few numbers a frame, the overlay (one textured quad and a line of text), and the focus pass only while it's clearing. Range finding: one ray through loaded blocks every 4 frames. The cost to watch is Far Sight drawing live (above).
  • No per-frame allocation in any of it.

10 · Before and after

Spotting a ruin from a ridge (the ordinary case)

Today

No zoom. Open the Atlas, look for unexplored land, guess, close it, walk.

With binoculars

Tap I, scroll to 8×, see a broken tower: 8× · 610 m · NE. Right-click. The Now line points the way. One key, one scroll, one click, and the map never opened.

Something moving in the valley (the case zoom mods do badly)

With a spyglass

Fixed 10×, the mouse a fixed 8× slower, a hard black ring, and no idea what or how dangerous it is.

With binoculars

4× to find it, 8× to hold it, half a second on it and its plate reads "Ashback boar · Lv 23" in the rank's colour. Aim feels the same at every step.

Today

Far Sight draws it beautifully at your normal view.

With binoculars

11 · Tests, stages and estimate

Tests (GameTests, prefix binoculars_)

  • binoculars_state: tap, hold, toggle, and every cause of lowering.
  • binoculars_zoom: steps, memory, trackpad build-up, FOV from 30 to 110 with any speed effect.
  • binoculars_sense: the on-screen look speed stays within 2% of unzoomed at every step and mouse setting.
  • binoculars_feel: the file reads; a broken one keeps the defaults.
  • binoculars_relay: only trackers hear it, rate limit, cleared on death, logout, dimension change.
  • binoculars_mark: a spot becomes the destination through the server's own checks; sky and out-of-world refused.
  • The look and feel can't be tested headless. That's your play: the checklist below.

Your checklist in game

  1. Raise and lower ten times quickly. It never sticks, and the sword comes back.
  2. Step 2× to 12× and back. Each step clicks and settles. Nothing wobbles.
  3. Track a running animal at 8×. It feels like aiming unzoomed.
  4. Crouch at 12×. The view calms.
  5. Look at Carillon from far away, then at a far range on Lens Ultra. Fine, or ask for Far focus.
  6. Get hit while looking. They come down at once.
  7. Have someone watch you in third person (or /awaken binoculars peek to see yourself).

12 · Decisions for you

  1. Build the binoculars as planned, stages 0 to 2 (the core, mark a spot, read creatures at range). Recommended: yes
  2. Default key. I is the only free letter. B would be easy to remember but it's Bind Echo. A mouse side button is also possible. All are rebindable. Recommended: I
  3. Tap to toggle, hold to peek, both on one key. Recommended: yes
  4. Lens edge. Twin (two soft merged circles, reads as binoculars at a glance, the film look) or single (one wide round field, closer to what real binoculars show). Both are in the mock-up. Recommended: twin
  5. Steps of 2×, 3×, 4×, 6×, 8×, 12×, first raise at 4×, then remembered. Recommended: yes
  6. Walking with them up at a slow walk, rather than standing still. Recommended: yes
  7. Later choices, after you've played stage 2: real focus by depth, and Far focus in Far Sight. Recommended: decide then
