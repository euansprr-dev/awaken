The Whole Boss Fight

You fought the Ringer today and named four things: the sword broke, the ring can't be trusted, a perfect parry feels small, and the win has no moment. I read your log, your save and the code. All four have a plain cause, and the same causes sit on all 47 bosses.

What I propose, in five lines
  • Gear that lasts. An E weapon holds 900 blows, where it holds 220. Nothing you forged is ever destroyed: at zero it is worn out until you mend it.
  • A ring you can read. It runs on the same clock as the boss's body, closes at one speed, and closes when the blow reaches you. It turns white for exactly as long as a parry will work.
  • A parry that feels great. A clash in three sizes, the ring bursting outward, a sound you choose by ear. No pitched-up ding.
  • A final blow worth remembering. A boss can only die to a finishing blow. At zero it kneels, you finish it, and four seconds of camera, slowed time and its own ending follow.
  • Every boss, no easier. No window, health, damage or stance number changes. Three pilots first, as before.

2

The ring moves in four jumps, trembles between them, and is gone before the blow lands.

The ring reads a countdown the server sends. The server only sends it on every fourth tick. The ring then subtracts your own frame clock from that stale number, so in each tick it creeps in and snaps back. For the Ringer's Swing it sits at four sizes for a fifth of a second each, shaking, and jumps between them.

The countdown is cleared on the last tick of the wind-up. The bell lands one to four ticks after that. So the ring disappears a quarter open, and the right moment to raise your guard is just after it has vanished. The last ring you can see is the wrong moment: a guard raised there is a block, not a parry.

The white glint on the striking hand, which was meant to be the cue for a light blow, is set to show when the countdown reads five. It reads 16, 12, 8, 4. It never shows. All of this is in the shared code, so it is true of every boss and every creature.

Some of the parries that felt perfect were probably blocks, because the ring gave you no way to tell. A block has a thud and no ring.

This bench is a model I wrote from the game's code, with the game's numbers. It is here so you can judge the idea. It is no proof that the built ring will feel right, which only your play can say. The clash you see after a parry uses today's numbers on today's ring and the planned ones on the planned ring. There is no sound on this page.

One clock
The ring reads the same clock that moves the boss's body. Ring and bell can't disagree, because they are one number. No countdown from the server is read for time again.
One speed
Every ring, on every boss and creature, takes 0.6 seconds to close. A longer wind-up shows its flare and glow first and the ring later. A held blow's ring just comes later. You learn one rhythm for the whole game.
Closes on you
It closes when the blow reaches where you stand, worked out from the same shapes the server strikes with. A Whirl that reaches you late closes late. A blow that will miss you draws its ring dim.
The band
The lock ring grows a band as wide as your weapon's parry window. While the ring is inside it, both burn white. That is now, and it lasts as long as a parry will work: wider for a rapier, narrower for a maul. Red and violet blows use the same ring with your dodge's window.
After
Parried: the rings burst outward. Guard up too soon: Too early under the ring for half a second. Struck: Too late. You can switch the words off.
No lock needed
In a boss fight the ring shows on the boss whenever it is on screen.
Also fixed
The white glint on the striking hand fires as the band lights, so the world and the ring say the same thing.

4 · The design · part four

A final blow worth remembering

The rule: a boss can only die to a finishing blow. When its health is gone it does not fall. It stops, its helpers drop, anything it threw fades, and it goes to its knee. The plate turns gold and says It's beaten. Hold Left Button to finish it. It can't hurt you and nothing but you can end it. If you walk away for twenty seconds it dies the plain way, so a fight can never get stuck.

Then you hold attack, and this plays:

  1. In. Sound ducks, black bars slide in, the HUD goes. The view leaves your eyes for a low shot of you and the kneeling boss.
  2. Your weapon's finishing move. Your body plays it, in your armour, with your weapon. Each of the sixteen styles has its own.
  3. The blow. The last swing slows to a third. On contact: a quarter-second freeze, one white frame, and the sound of what the boss is made of.
  4. Its own ending. Each boss falls its own way and has a last word. The Ringer's bells drop and toll once. A forge goes dark. Glass breaks along its whole length.
  5. Back to you. The view returns to your eyes. The reward follows as it does today.
Your arm
The camera films your whole body from outside, so the first-person arm is never asked to do more than it does today. Your rule about the straight vanilla arm holds.
Own eyes
A setting: Finishing blows: cinematic camera, through your own eyes, or off. Through your own eyes plays the same beats with no camera move, using the Execution your arm already has. It is also what plays if there is no room for the camera.
By weapon
Longsword: a rising cut, then both hands overhead. Maul: one full wind and the crush. Daggers: in close, a flurry, both blades out together. Twin Blades: a crossing pair, then both points down. Katana: one drawn cut past it, and it falls behind you. The other eleven follow the same way.
By boss
Six kinds of ending for six kinds of body (two-legged, beast, serpent, flier, machine, growth), each in the boss's own material and sound. The six Wardens, the eight Sovereigns and the Signal's Shadow get hand-made endings after the pilots.
Skipping
The first time you see a boss's ending it plays whole. After that, holding Sneak ends it at once.
With others
Only the one who struck gets the camera. Everyone else sees your body make the move and the boss fall, in real time. You can't be hurt or pushed while it plays.

How it stays smooth

  • One side decides. The server commits the finishing blow once: who, which weapon, which tick. From that tick the boss is dead by rule and the reward's timers run. Your game plays a fixed four seconds. No answer from the server can change it part-way.
  • A known start. The boss is kneeling and the server stands you on a clear spot in front of it. With no clear spot, it plays through your own eyes.
  • A camera that checks. Three shots are tried, each tested for a clear path and a clear view on every frame. If none is clear, it plays through your own eyes.
  • One way back. A pause, a disconnect, a change of world, or anything else taking the camera all run the same clean return. The opening's camera already works this way.
  • Read every frame. Each weapon and each kind of ending is filmed on CI from both views before you see it.
5 · Difficulty

How it stays as hard as it is

  • Nothing a fight is judged by changes: the parry and dodge windows, health, damage, stances, the bosses' minds and strings.
  • The ring shows the real window. It does not widen it. You will parry more, because you can finally see when. That moves you toward the player these fights were tuned for, who parries six blows in ten and wins the Ringer most of the time. Someone who only blocks still loses nine fights in ten.
  • The finishing blow does no damage. It starts only when the health is already gone.
  • A worn-out weapon makes a fight slower, never easier.
  • The check: the simulator's table for all 15 Lords and 6 Wardens must come out identical before and after. If your own log later shows you parrying more than three blows in four, the answer is the shorter tells already planned for later Wardens. The ring stays honest.

7 · Checked from four sides

A structured review, not a playtest

  • Someone new. One ring, one speed, one colour change to learn. The existing one-time stance hint gains a line: Raise your guard when the ring turns white.
  • Someone expert. Nothing is slower. The band shows your weapon's true window, so a rapier's wider window and a maul's narrower one are visible for the first time. The finishing blow can be skipped after the first time.
  • Comfort and access. The ring's "now" is a change of brightness and thickness as well as colour. The words can replace or join it. Shake, freeze and camera each have a switch. No sound is the only cue for anything.
  • The engineer. The riskiest piece is the camera's shot test in tight rooms, so own-eyes mode is the fallback everywhere. The ring's rule for when a blow reaches you is one shared function that both sides call, tested against all 47 move tables. Every part has its own switch, and the old ring and the plain death stay in the code until you've played the pilots.
