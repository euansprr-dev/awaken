## The premise

Carillon is a walled city of bells, built on top of the Choir, a machine the Precursors left under it. Gates tear open
across the world and things come through them; the wall is why the city is still standing, and the bells, ringing every
hour, keep the gates calm near it. The people who fight gates are hunters: the few who died inside one, kept thinking,
and woke up Awakened. You are not one of them. You are a Runner, a courier who carries the city's letters over its roofs.
One morning at dawn the great bell rings out of time, a light falls into Choir Square, and something only you can see
says "[ERROR] … candidate found." You run to it. A gate has opened inside the walls, under the bells, which isn't
supposed to be possible. It takes you down into the Choir's hall, where the Seated One judges you and kills you. You keep
thinking. You wake in the Hunter Association's infirmary, an E-rank hunter with a band on your wrist, a city that has
started to say your name, and no idea why the bell chose you.

## The world, in the player's terms

- **Gates** are tears where the Deep leaks into the world. Inside: floors of rooms, the things that live there, and a
  Lord at the bottom. Clear one and it closes. Leave one too long and it breaks, and what was inside walks out. They
  open outside the walls. Never inside. Until this morning.
- **The Choir** is the machine under Carillon. Its bells are on every tower in the city. Only the archive (Teodor) knows
  what it really is, and he isn't sure.
- **The Hunter Association**, on the High Terrace, finds the Awakened, assesses them with the Lens (a shard of the
  Choir), ranks them E to S, pays them to clear gates, and patches them up in its infirmary. Its people are tired, kind and
  very dry.
- **The Runners' Guild** carries the city's messages over the roofs, because the streets are slow and crowded. Runners
  train from childhood on the low roofs. Their house is the Roost, on the north-west wall, and its Roostmaster is
  **Kestrel**. That's why your body can do all this before any System touches you.
- **The Signal** is the System's voice. Only the Awakened see it, and at first it can barely speak: "[ERROR]".

## Who you are, and why the bell rings for you

You are a Runner on the dawn shift: good legs, no rank, nobody's idea of a hero. In multiplayer your friends are
Runners of the same shift, and the bell rings for all of you, which the Association finds even stranger.

The Choir chooses. When it finds a mind it can wake, its own bell rings once out of time and its light shows the way to
its own gate, the Bell Crypt, under the square. Inside, the Seated One judges you, and the judgement is death: the Choir
only wakes a mind that keeps thinking after the body stops. Ordinary hunters pass the same test by accident, dying in
ordinary gates; most stay dead. You were called. Nobody in the first hour knows why, and nobody pretends to. (The truth,
found at the Seed, per STORY.md: the Signal is you, from a loop that already reached the bottom. It called you because it
has called you before. That's why its jokes land a little too well.)

**What fixes the contradiction.** Today the Void says "You died inside a gate and kept thinking. Few do." and the
infirmary says "You walked out of a gate nobody walks out of." Both can't be true. The Void is right, and stays exactly as
it is. The infirmary now says what the city saw: the gate shut with you inside, and at the next bell it opened and put
you back on the stones. Breathing. And the officer knows what that means: you died in there, and most people stay that
way.

## What the first hour is about

**Being chosen and not knowing why.** The bell rings for you; the city stops to watch; a gate opens where none can; it
kills you and gives you back. Then the plain, warm, slightly comic business of becoming a hunter: a walk with a tired
officer, a Lens that finds you unremarkable, a band on your wrist, a map, a first power, a Directive, and a view of the
gate you'll leave by. Teaching is part of the fiction: on the run the Runners' marks show you each move with your own
keys (you're a courier; of course the roofs have marks), and after the awakening the same tips speak in the System's
glass, then fall quiet once you know (onboarding.md). The mystery (a gate inside the walls, a bell that hasn't rung out of time in living memory) is left
open on purpose: the city's story spine (the lean plan's "The city's story spine": bell metal stolen at C, the Choir
waking at A) picks it up. One thread is personal and funny: Kestrel sent you for word from the square, and you never
brought it back. At E-rank the Roost asks for its word, and for one last delivery.

Tone: bright, funny and sincere (Kestrel, Sun, Bram, the clerk), then suddenly brutal (the hall, the hand, "most people
stay that way"), then warm again.
