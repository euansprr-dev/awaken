## What changed

**The climbing grid, as rules in `CityGrammar` (v2), on all 779 plots.** In every climbing face's outside plane:
row 3 a hood over each ground opening (a canopy across shopfronts), row 5 the shop cornice (on a Bellwatch jetty:
corbels and beam ends 2 out), then per storey row 2 a box under every window and row 4 a floor course; the top course is
the eave. Even rows hold only shutters (no collision). Courses run across party walls (same rows on every plot of a
street), so a row of houses is one shimmy rail and every window stack a ladder; outside corners turn the course.
Climb lines (lead drainpipe; ivy on a trellis in the Garden Ward and on some Bellwatch fronts; the Quay's hoist rope)
sit within 5 of each end of every street-face run (never more than 12 apart, 11 across a party wall), between window
stacks, clear of trees and of a neighbour's jetty. Running faces: alleys, slots under 3 wide between houses, and the
Quay's warehouse flanks keep only flush windows (kit windows are full blocks, so a run passes them); party walls stay
blind. Faces class themselves by the ground in front (street / back / running / none), so nothing is drawn into a
neighbour's wall, an inside corner where two fronts meet, or a neighbour's jetty.

**Facades per district** (look bible §5). Window vocabulary as kit windows (closed houses; enterable plots keep glass):
Bellwatch leaded casements, one per timber bay, wandering by at most one between storeys, small ground windows under
stone hoods, painted shutters (blue, sage, oxblood), jetties once at the first floor (lanes 6+ both sides, 4-5 one side
so the lane never closes at the lips), crow-stepped stone gables on a third of the tall merchant houses; Market sash
windows in single or paired rhythms, shopfronts (shop window, canopy, dark fascia, sandstone cornice), bronze courses,
iron balconettes, a three-bay balcony on a third of the houses, calcite quoins and pilasters, first-floor oriels on some
fronts; Quay brick and mud-brick warehouses (boarded ones timber-framed) with a loading-door stack over the street door,
sill boards, barred ground windows, crow-stepped gables and, where the gable meets the street, a hoist beam and rope;
Terrace symmetric fronts, arched windows, stone sill boxes, quartz pilasters and cornices, balustrades open at every
climb line; Works paired iron windows and blackstone bands; Garden cottage windows, green shutters, ivy, verdigris
hoods; Wallring leaded windows and natural shutters. Plaster in eight colours, timber, ashlar and city bricks replace
the vanilla wall blocks.

**Roofs.** Every ridge capped in a pale stone (odd and even spans), hips turn their eave corners, dormers on Bellwatch's
steep roofs over an eave of 9+ and all round the Market's mansards (verdigris cheeks), crow-stepped gables, chimney
crowns (kit) with the pot the citizens' smoke finder looks for, mansard decks with a verdigris curb, attic windows in
gables, mossy patches on old-town slopes (seeded). Bellwatch's mix: kit slate 40%, deepslate tiles 25, cobbled deepslate
15, tuff 10, blackstone 5, polished deepslate 5. Market: clay tiles 40%, copper 20, resin 15, bronze 15, granite 10.

## Decisions taken (SPEC §16 proposals for the Carillon session)

1. **Jetties**: once, at the first floor, over lanes 6+ wide on both sides, 4–5 wide on the south or east face only, so a
   lane never closes at the lips (bible: 4+). Wallring houses have no jetties.
2. **Face classes**: a face climbs only with 3+ of open ground in front; narrower slots, alleys and the Quay's warehouse
   flanks are running faces. An inside corner where two fronts meet, and any cell under a neighbour's jetty, take
   nothing from either face. Corners turn only where free-standing.
3. **Kit windows** (opaque, flush) replace glass panes on closed houses (solid cores kept; enterable plots keep glass).
   Running faces keep them: a flush window is no opening for a wall-run (bible §3.2's "openings ≤ 1" is moot there).
4. **Pilasters stay in the wall plane** (Market calcite on 5+ storeys, Terrace quartz pillars), not proud as bible §5.3
   says: a proud pilaster stops every shimmy along the courses.
5. **Balconies** in the Market are a three-bay iron balconette (a full-cell hold), not a floor and a rail; **oriels**
   on ~30% of Market and Garden fronts (first floor, 3 wide, glazed all round, roofed by the floor course).
6. **Climb lines**: within 5 of each end of every street-face run, so ≤ 12 apart and ≤ 11 across a party wall; ivy
   trellis for 70% of the Garden Ward and 30% of Bellwatch's unjettied faces; never on a window stack, a tree or a
   neighbour's jetty.
7. **Roof mixes** (decision 27 shifts them to the kit): Bellwatch kit slate 40 / deepslate tiles 25 / cobbled deepslate
   15 / tuff 10 / blackstone 5 / polished deepslate 5 (bible: kit slate 10); Market clay tiles 40 / copper 20 / resin 15 /
   bronze 15 / granite 10 (the bible's mix had no clay). Crow-stepped gables on a third of Bellwatch's 4+ storey gable
   houses (stone) and on every brick or mud-brick Quay warehouse.
8. **Chimney crown** is `awaken:chimney_bricks`, so M3's smoke finder (a pot on "bricks") keeps working unchanged.
9. **Avenue tree crowns fill only air** (they cut courses and pipes before).
10. Shop canopies at row 3 are dark-oak top slabs until B3's awnings; the fascia at row 4 is dark planks.

## For B2 (relief and heights)
- Every grid row is relative to the plot's lift, so even lifts keep the grid (bible §3.10). `CityGrammar.put` refuses
  any outside-plane cell at or below the ground's own lift there, so a plot facing higher ground loses its lower lips by
  design (a retaining wall is not its face).
- Face classes read only the plan's kinds; new retaining walls, stairs and stepped lanes need no grammar change, but a
  street face whose ground steps along its length keeps one row set (the plot's): stepped lanes should step at party
  walls, as the bible says (2 per plot).
- The plan hash is unchanged; you own the next re-pin. `city_climb_lines` / `city_grid_rows` check whatever you build.
- Dormers and crow steps are grammar, not plan: the roof graph's `top` is unchanged (a crow-step gable stands at most 1.5
  over it).

## For B3 (street life)
- Awnings go where the dark-oak canopies are (row 3, continuous across shopfronts; the door's column too). Sign
  brackets belong on the row 5 cornice and reach 2–3 out: beyond a plot's 2-block margin, so make them props pieces.
- Hang lines at 7/9/11 meet the courses (rows 9, 13…) and boxes (7, 11…) on both sides of a lane; M3's laundry still
  hangs at row 4 (even, no collision) and can meet a drainpipe in the outside plane (it skips that cell).
- `awaken:lit_window[lit=true]` has the glow ready; everything is placed unlit. Smoke: M3's finder still finds pots on
  `chimney_bricks`.
- `runner_lines` is empty and `runner_soft` holds only hay: fill them with the lines, bunting and awnings.

## For B4 (landmarks)
- The mill (`CityLandmarks.mill`) builds its body with `CityGrammar.building(.., millStyle(), ..)`; landmarks can reuse
  the grammar the same way. Corner turrets (bible §5.1, 1 Bellwatch corner house in 12) and scaffolding on plots under
  repair (1 in 30) were not built.

## The honest read: "every facade looks built to be there, and you just know you can climb it"

Mostly yes, from the street. Every front now has depth in every row: the hood, the cornice, a box under each window, a
course on every floor, pipes and ivy between the stacks. The ladder reads at a glance in the Market and the Garden
Ward, where the boxes, shutters and courses are contrasting and lit (`b1_facade_market`, `b1_facade_garden`), and the
Runner's own hang code agrees: 99% of the holds catch. Bellwatch reads as a timber old town at last, and from the sky
it is no longer one dark mass: blue-grey slate with pale ridges, dormers, crowns, crow steps and coloured plaster
(`pair_sky_nw_low`, `b1_roofs_bellwatch`). Where it falls short:
- **Repetition.** A house repeats one floor's rhythm all the way up; the grid is honest but can read as shelving on
  tall fronts. The course variety fixed after the tour helps across a street, not up one front.
- **Ground storeys are plain** (stone, a small window, a hood): they wait for B3's awnings, signs, crates and people.
- **Bellwatch lanes are dark** where jetties and courses close over a 4-wide lane (`b1_bellwatch`); good for mood,
  heavy at noon. The jetty rule could go to 5+ if Euan finds them oppressive.
- **Drainpipes are chunky** for their job (one every ~10 blocks of street) and a pipe at a party wall sometimes doubles
  with the neighbour's.
- **Not verified in game after the four post-tour changes**, and no shot of lit windows (B3 lights them).
- Some framings are cramped (`b1_facade_bellwatch` is too close to the bell tower).
