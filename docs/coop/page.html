Together, All the Way

Co-op from the first bell to the last, for two people who always know where the other is, are never split up by a rule, and fight the same fight at the difficulty each of them enjoys.

Changed after your answer. You said no to the support job: she shouldn't become a healer, she keeps her weapons and powers, and it's really two people on different difficulty. This version does that. The thread powers, the light and the thread's rank are gone.

  • The rules for sharing are already in the game and mostly sound. The experience of being two people isn't: you can't see your partner, point at anything or share a home without typing a command, and a death or a Reassessment splits you up.
  • Her own difficulty. Each player has a switch, My fights: Full or Gentle. On Gentle she fights the same Lord beside you with her own weapon and powers at full strength. What the fight throws at her is plainer, slower and softer. Nothing aimed at you changes.
  • No tether, no card at the door, no penalties: the same XP, loot and rank. It's hers to switch any time out of a fight.
  • Around that: fixes for two fighters, and the things a couple needs outside the fights. Alone, the game plays exactly as it does today.

The two players in the drawings are Euan and "Ana", an example name. The drawings are mockups I made for this page, not screenshots.

1 · What you asked for, and how we'll know it worked

My reading of your brief

  • Everything from A to Z works with two. The story, the first boss scene, every gate, the Reassessments, levels, discovery, the end.
  • Two ways to play a gate and a Reassessment. Both at full difficulty, or one of you on an easier one: two people having a different difficulty mode, simpler but still obviously action-based and fun, with their weapons, their powers, and revertible. It doesn't need to be so fair for her.
  • Whatever is missing that would make co-op 20 times better for a couple or friends, extremely smooth.

Wrong or missing with two players

1

You can't see your partner or point at anything. No health, no direction, no dot on the Atlas, no ping. The Atlas shares waypoints, and that's all.

No party HUD or ping class exists anywhere in the code; the Atlas draws no other players.

2

Your party is a side effect. It's whoever shares your Hearth, your gate or your thread. Sharing a Hearth is /awaken hearth invite. Two people walking the wilds on the first day share nothing.

gates/GateParty.java, hearth/HearthCommands.java:42-52

3

A death in a gate sends one of you home. The one who falls loses the Satchel, takes a Fracture, leaves the run and wakes at the Hearth, perhaps kilometres away, while the other is still inside.

gates/run/Runs.java:651-704

4

A Reassessment leaves your partner at the bell. It takes only fighters at the level, and skips a tied pair without a word. The rider, or a friend a few levels short, watches you vanish into the game's best fight.

hearth/trial/Provings.java:167-171

5

The warning ring closes for the wrong person. A boss's tell doesn't say who the blow is for, and the ring is drawn for whoever is locked on. With the new boss plan's "Too early / Too late", your partner would be marked on a blow that was aimed at you.

FightRunner.java:864,935, aim/client/Reticle.java:157-177

6

Bosses flick between two fighters. They turn to whoever hit them last, on every hit, and their read of you starts over each time. "Fights That Read You" ends up reading nobody.

AwakenMob.java:201; SPEC §48.1. Read in the code, not seen in a game.

7

The fights have one difficulty. The world's difficulty setting only decides what a death costs. Two people who want different fights can't have them. Riding Along's answer was to make her safe by making her weak and poor, on a tether.

core/AwakenConfig.java:9-16; SPEC §55. At half XP she'd be about level 40 when you reach 100 (docs/coop/harness/rider_pace.py), so you could never rank up the same day.

8

A friend who joins later is alone for half an hour, and can never earn a monument you've already opened. Levels far apart have no rule except the thread.

OpeningDirector, §67.5, finds/Reward.java:39-50

9

Nothing has been seen with two real players. All 544 tour scripts are one client. The thread, the fold and the party doors are unseen.

One more to measure before believing it: a party raises a boss's health but not its stance, so two fighters may break it twice as often (ArmsRules.java:250).

Gears of War has let each co-op player pick their own difficulty since its second game. Theirs changes damage and aim. Ours also changes what the boss chooses to throw, because your bosses already decide blow by blow who they're aiming at.

For being together, one small module owns the party, the strip and the pings, behind the hook every system already asks. The other way was patching six systems one by one, which is how today's gaps happened.

Looked at and left out: split screen, one shared level, chat features, scaling the land to the weaker player (the world-moods plan owns the land's danger), a third step above Full (the setting is data, so one can be added later).

4 · Being together

See each other, point, share a home

  • A home for two. When one of you places the Ember near the other: "Make this your home too?" A home has an owner and may have one second keeper with the same rights, so neither of a couple is a guest.
  • The party strip (top left in the drawings below): your partner's name, health and what they're doing, and an arrow on the screen's edge with the distance when they're out of view. It's hidden when you're alone.
  • The Atlas shows your party as live dots, and "Go to Ana" is a destination that follows her.

6 · Her own difficulty

My fights: Full or Gentle

One switch per player. Full is the game as it is. Gentle is the same fight asked more simply. She keeps her weapon, her powers, her loot and her progress, and nothing ties the two of you together. Everything Gentle changes is decided by who a thing is aimed at, so a blow aimed at you is exactly today's.

  • Your fight stays yours. Her full-strength hits would shorten it, so she counts as half a fighter when a room or a boss is sized: a Lord gains a quarter more health and stance for her (half for a partner on Full), a room a sixth more monsters, and no extra elite.
  • Still a fight for her. The boss does turn to her. What it throws can always be read, and holding guard is always an answer. She breaks stance, lands heavies, casts, and takes the finishing blow if she's the one there.
  • Hers to switch. On the band, any time out of a fight; inside a gate it takes hold at the next room. Your strip shows a small mark by her name. It stays hers when you log off, so the world doesn't turn on her.
  • No thread. No tether, no reach to stay inside, no both-must-agree, no card at a gate's door. The thread's look that Riding Along built stays in the code, unused.
  • The numbers are first guesses. Every value in the table is data. The first evening you two play is the tuning session.

7 · A Reassessment, two ways

She comes too, and it counts

Both on Full stays as it is. One on Gentle is new: today a tied pair is skipped at the bell and Riding Along earned no rank.

  • The same rank. If she's at the level and ready, she takes it, with nothing written on it. She earns the same XP as you, so a pair who play together reach the level together.
  • A friend a few levels short, on either setting, may come along and fight. They're told plainly that this one doesn't count for them yet. Nobody is skipped in silence.
  • Losing is as today: no fail day, you wake at the door, you go again.

10 · How it gets tested

Two players in every check

  • A pair kit for the game's tests. Two real survival test players in a party, with each one's network messages readable (the helper exists today, for one). About forty tests, one per mechanism, and five longer ones that walk the journey as a pair: the opening to the Registry, Ilse to the landing, a whole gate with one on Full and one on Gentle, a Reassessment the same way, a home founded by two.
  • Tours with a partner, on GitHub. One client films while the server plays a second player from a short script (go, look, strike, cast, use, fall). The client is on Full in one tour and on Gentle in the next, so both screens are photographed, on the same runners as today.
  • Two real clients, once. A probe on a Linux runner: a server and two clients for five scripted minutes, then the logs are read for kicks and errors. If the clients won't draw there, the probe is dropped and nothing else changes. Never two clients on your Mac.
  • The wire. Messages per player per second, alone against two, before and after each stage. Budget: under 12 a second in a fight for everything in this plan.
  • Your fight is the same. From the fight recorder: with a Gentle partner, every blow aimed at the player on Full is the same move with the same timings as alone, and at least three phrases in four are theirs.
  • Alone plays the same. One player with every switch on gets the same numbers and the same messages as today. The old-save test stays.
  • The real test is the two of you: the opening, a gate with her on Gentle, and a Reassessment the same way.

What could go wrong

  • Gentle is too soft, or not soft enough. It's the part most likely to need the two of you. Every number is data, and both play logs are read after your first evening.
  • Gentle bleeds into your fight. The recorder check above, on all 47 fight scripts.
  • A boss with nothing plain to throw. Every body has plain blows; a test walks every script to prove a Gentle target always gets one.
  • An easy mode in solo play. Gentle is each player's own choice and Full is the default. Decision 5 asks whether it may be used alone.
  • Three other plans move the same ground. The boss plan's ring, the Far Landing's skiff and the pets. The Far Landing, the pets and the world-moods sessions have answered and nothing clashes: a couple can take one pet each, and sister homes stay the one rule for a second home. The boss plan's session hasn't answered yet. None of their files is touched before they merge.
