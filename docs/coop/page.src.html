Together, All the Way

Co-op from the first bell to the last, for two people who always know where the other is, are never split up by a rule, and each have a full evening of things to do in the same fight, even if only one of them likes fighting.

The short answer.

  • The rules for sharing are already in the game and mostly sound. The experience of being two people isn't: you can't see your partner, point at anything or share a home without typing a command, and a death or a Reassessment splits you up.
  • The tag-along you asked for before exists. It's Riding Along (9 Nov): a thread of light, and the rider can't go down near you. It's merged and has never been seen in a game. It makes your partner safe. It doesn't give them anything to do, and it's refused at Reassessments.
  • This plan gives the rider a real job on that thread (eight things to do, earned across the ranks), lets a Reassessment be ridden, fixes the fights for two fighters, and adds the things a couple needs around the fights.
  • Alone, the game plays exactly as it does today. Every part has a switch.

The two players in the drawings are Euan and "Ana", an example name. The drawings are mockups I made for this page, not screenshots.

1 · What you asked for, and how we'll know it worked

My reading of your brief

  • Everything from A to Z works with two. The story, the first boss scene, every gate, the Reassessments, levels, discovery, the end.
  • Two ways to play a gate and a Reassessment. Both fight, or one tags along: can't die as much as you, earns less (isn't as good as fighting), and has a ton of things to do during the fight.
  • Whatever is missing that would make co-op 20 times better for a couple or friends, extremely smooth.

Wrong or missing with two players

1

You can't see your partner or point at anything. No health, no direction, no dot on the Atlas, no ping. The Atlas shares waypoints, and that's all.

No party HUD or ping class exists anywhere in the code; the Atlas draws no other players.

2

Your party is a side effect. It's whoever shares your Hearth, your gate or your thread. Sharing a Hearth is /awaken hearth invite. Two people walking the wilds on the first day share nothing.

gates/GateParty.java, hearth/HearthCommands.java:42-52

3

A death in a gate sends one of you home. The one who falls loses the Satchel, takes a Fracture, leaves the run and wakes at the Hearth, perhaps kilometres away, while the other is still inside.

gates/run/Runs.java:651-704

4

A Reassessment leaves your partner at the bell. It takes only fighters at the level, and skips a tied pair without a word. The rider, or a friend a few levels short, watches you vanish into the game's best fight.

hearth/trial/Provings.java:167-171

5

The warning ring closes for the wrong person. A boss's tell doesn't say who the blow is for, and the ring is drawn for whoever is locked on. With the new boss plan's "Too early / Too late", your partner would be marked on a blow that was aimed at you.

FightRunner.java:864,935, aim/client/Reticle.java:157-177

6

Bosses flick between two fighters. They turn to whoever hit them last, on every hit, and their read of you starts over each time. "Fights That Read You" ends up reading nobody.

AwakenMob.java:201; SPEC §48.1. Read in the code, not seen in a game.

7

The rider has no job. They keep their weapon and powers at 40%. That's a weak fighter, not a role. Mario Galaxy's first second-player mode was called thin for the same reason.

SPEC §55. And at half XP the rider is about level 40 when you reach level 100 (docs/coop/harness/rider_pace.py).

8

A friend who joins later is alone for half an hour, and can never earn a monument you've already opened. Levels far apart have no rule except the thread.

OpeningDirector, §67.5, finds/Reward.java:39-50

9

Nothing has been seen with two real players. All 544 tour scripts are one client. The thread, the fold and the party doors are unseen.

One more to measure before believing it: a party raises a boss's health but not its stance, so two fighters may break it twice as often (ArmsRules.java:250).

For being together, one small module owns the party, the strip and the pings, behind the hook every system already asks. The other way was patching six systems one by one, which is how today's gaps happened.

4 · Being together

See each other, point, share a home

  • A home for two. When one of you places the Ember near the other: "Make this your home too?" A home has an owner and may have one second keeper with the same rights, so neither of a couple is a guest.
  • The party strip (top left in the drawings below): your partner's name, health and what they're doing, and an arrow on the screen's edge with the distance when they're out of view. It's hidden when you're alone.
  • The Atlas shows your party as live dots, and "Go to Ana" is a destination that follows her.

6 · One rides along

The rider's job

The thread, the catch, the 48-block reach and the refusals stay exactly as you approved them on 9 November. What's new is the job. The fighter makes the fight shed light, and the rider turns that light into help. Everything the rider does is a choice about where to be and whom to help. None of it is a reflex, and none of it needs aim.

  • The keys. Thread powers are a ninth power family in the system you have. The rider puts them in their own five slots, beside or instead of their own powers, which still work at 40%. Same keys, same bar. They cast only while tied.
  • It stays a hard fight. Mend gives a heart a second against blows of four to eight. Ward halves one blow every 15 seconds. Bind never holds a boss. The rider adds no damage of their own. They make your mistakes survivable, and that's all.
  • The rider's own risk. Blasts still hurt. A fold (the four seconds in your shadow) spills their light on the floor. Past 48 blocks the protection is gone, as now.
  • Your say as the fighter. A setting: "My partner may haul me", or "Ask first". A haul never interrupts a strike or a cast that has begun.

7 · A Reassessment, two ways

The rider comes too

Both fight stays as it is: each fighter at the level takes the rank, and the Warden stands longer for each. One rides is new, and reverses the 9 November rule that a Reassessment can't be ridden.

{{S2}}
  • The thread's task is the rider's own test, the same shape for every Warden: keep the thread whole (out of reach for no more than five seconds in all), go down no more than twice, and do the thread's work for that rank (twelve deeds at D, four more each rank: hearts mended, marks struck, binds, wards that took a blow, hauls). It shows as a row of beads filling. There's no clock.
  • A rank won by the thread is the same letter with a thread through it. The story, the Tasks page, the town, the bells and the next strand treat it as the rank. What makes the world more dangerous (the gates rolled for you, the fair-damage numbers) reads only a rank you fought for, so a thread rank never puts your partner in danger alone. If they reach the level later and fight the same Warden, the thread comes off the letter.
  • A friend a few levels short may come as the rider, or wait. They're told which. Nobody is skipped in silence.
  • Losing is as today: no fail day, you wake at the door, you go again.

10 · How it gets tested

Two players in every check

  • A pair kit for the game's tests. Two real survival test players in a party, with each one's network messages readable (the helper exists today, for one). About forty tests, one per mechanism, and five longer ones that walk the journey as a pair: the opening to the Registry, Ilse to the landing, a whole gate with a fighter and a rider, a Reassessment with a rider, a home founded by two.
  • Tours with a partner, on GitHub. One client films while the server plays a second player from a short script (go, look, strike, cast, use, fall). The client is the fighter in one tour and the rider in the next, so both screens are photographed, on the same runners as today.
  • Two real clients, once. A probe on a Linux runner: a server and two clients for five scripted minutes, then the logs are read for kicks and errors. If the clients won't draw there, the probe is dropped and nothing else changes. Never two clients on your Mac.
  • The wire. Messages per player per second, alone against two, before and after each stage. Budget: under 12 a second in a fight for everything in this plan.
  • Alone plays the same. One player with every switch on gets the same numbers and the same messages as today. The old-save test stays.
  • The real test is the two of you: the opening, a gate each way, and a Reassessment with one riding.

About 30 to 43 builder hours, one builder, in order. Every stage's switch off is exactly today, and every saved field is optional, so a world loads on a build without them.

What could go wrong

  • The rider's job misses. It's the part most likely to need your eye. The first strand is only three verbs, the tours photograph both screens before you play, and every power is data that's cheap to change.
  • Hauls annoy the fighter. The setting, the no-interrupt rule and a 20-second wait.
  • A thread rank feels like being carried. The task, the mark on the letter, and danger still measured by the rank you fought for.
  • Three other plans move the same ground. The boss plan's ring, the Far Landing's skiff and the pets. I've asked each session; none of their files is touched before they merge.
