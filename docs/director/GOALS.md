Written 29 Oct 2026 by the game-director session, after Euan's notes on the first pass (`IDEAS.md`).
Nothing here is approved or built. It's a direction for Euan to push on; each part then gets its own plan through

## What Euan said

- He stopped at hour four because he had no idea what he was supposed to be doing by then, and he thinks everything past
  that mark (the Sovereigns, "basically everything") hasn't been brought up to the level of the first hours.
- The loop past hour four reads as "improve your Hearth, go to gates, level up, pass Reassessments", and the
  Reassessments are "literally just a boring box". Each one should be one great boss in a really epic room, built like
  the bosses being made for the gates but a much bigger, real fight, and beating it should give a reward on top of the
  rank.
- There's no big goal. Greed Island's goal is to collect the 100 cards; Solo Leveling's is S-rank and the elixir. Awaken
  needs a goal like that: truly massive, interesting as a story and as play, and once it's done you can keep doing
  everything you enjoy.
- Most of the twenty ideas felt fluffy and not important. Tide Nights was interesting, but every night is too much: what
  if you want to leave and explore? Something like it now and then, orchestrated well, could work.
- The Hearth can go much further: machines and automation, a lot more custom. You shouldn't have to build everything by
  hand. The Hearth should tell you what it needs ("go and get these materials"), you go and do the fights and find the
  things, you bring them back, and your people build it, unless you want to build yourself. Set up right, that guides
  you to every part of the game. Finishing the Hearth might need something hidden deep in an S-rank gate, so you know
  that's the goal.
- "We basically need to be much smarter about how the game sets goals for the player", in a way that feels AAA and makes
  you experience a ton of different things. The bar stays extremely high.

**Where the first pass went wrong.** It answered "what else could you do?" with twenty features. The real question is
"what am I doing, and why?" at every hour of the game. A feature list makes that worse: more things to do with no reason
to do them. This pass starts from the goal and works down.

## How the best games set the goal

| Game | The goal | What makes it work |
|---|---|---|
| Hunter x Hunter, Greed Island | Collect the 100 designated slot cards, numbered 000 to 099 | A number you can count. Each card is ranked by how hard it is to get (SS down to H) and some exist in only a few copies, so players compete and trade. Collecting 001 to 099 earns 000, the prize. |
| Solo Leveling | Reach S-rank; get the Holy Water of Life for his mother | A personal reason. The way there is a climb with named stops (the Demon Castle's floors, an ingredient from Vulcan on floor 50). His job change is a hidden dungeon whose boss, Igris, becomes his first shadow. |
| Monster Hunter | Raise your Hunter Rank | Each rank cap is lifted by an Urgent Quest against one named monster. Ranking up *is* a boss fight. |
| Elden Ring | Become Elden Lord | Great Runes from the shardbearers. Two open the capital; the rest are optional, and each one restored at its tower gives a power. Partial to progress, all for the full prize. |
| Vault Hunters | Collect the 25 Artifacts | They come from vault crates. Assembled on a wall, they make the frame for the Final Vault. |
| Stardew Valley | Restore the Community Center | Six rooms of bundles, each drawn from a different activity (crafting, crops and animals, fishing, mining). Each room restored unlocks a place or a service. The bundles teach the whole game. |
| Terraria | The Moon Lord | Four pillars, each a small event of its own, drop the fragments for the last gear. After the first win the rematch is quicker. |

What runs through them:
1. **A number you can count**, shown from early on (100 cards, 25 artifacts, two runes).
2. **Every piece has its own story of how you got it.** Nobody remembers a drop; everyone remembers the fight.
3. **The rank-up is a named fight** you know about in advance.
4. **The collection teaches the whole game.** Stardew's rooms send you to every activity; Greed Island's cards to every
   corner of the island.
5. **Part of it to progress, all of it for the real prize.** You can finish without 100%, but 100% is what people chase.
6. **Each piece is worth having on its own**: a card's effect, a rune's power.
7. **You can see or hear your progress**: a restored building, a wall of artifacts.

## The shape: three horizons, always on screen

At any moment the player should be able to answer three questions without thinking:

1. **What am I working toward?** The far goal: the Hundred Bells (part 1). "You have 9 of 100."
2. **What's my next big thing?** The middle goals: the next **Proving** (part 2: "The Ringer waits at level 10. Beat it
   for D-rank, the Great Bell Tenor and the Tether") and the Hearth's next **work** (part 3: "Oren wants a Foundry. It
   needs bell bronze from the Singing Deep and Deepglass from the Descent").
3. **What do I do today?** The near goals: the things those need, routed through the guidance Awaken already has (the Now
   line, Trails, Promises' "What waits", the compass).

**The Road** is the first page of the K menu: three cards, far, middle, near, each with a picture, a count and one plain
line. Everything else in the journal sits behind it. Today the near horizon exists (§27, §33); the middle is a level
number; the far is missing. This document fills the other two.

## 1. The goal: the Hundred Bells

### The story reason
Carillon is a city of bells, and the game begins with "the bell rings wrong". Make that the whole game's thread:

- The Precursors gave the Choir a voice in the Waking world: **the Great Carillon**, a hundred bells, each cast from a
  piece of the world (a stone, a beast, a storm, a dream, a Sovereign's grief). Rung together they were the Choir's song.
- When the Keepers went down to hold the Seed, the bells scattered into the layers. The gates swallowed most of them.
  That's why the city's bell rings wrong: it's ringing alone.
- The Signal sets the goal plainly, the first night after the Registry: "There were a hundred. Find them. Ring them all
  and I can show you the bottom." (It knows: it's you, from a loop that didn't.)
- Vael is collecting them too, and tells you their count every time you meet: "Fourteen. You?"

### What a bell is
- **Numbered 00 to 99**, each with a name, a rank (E to S, how hard it is to get) and a place it comes from.
- **A note.** Hung in your tower, it adds its note to your Hearth's song. With nine bells the song is thin and gappy;
  with sixty it's a melody; at a hundred it's the full peal. You literally hear your progress, every dawn and dusk.
- **A voice.** Each bell does one thing while it hangs, tied to where it came from: the Mossback bell makes your fields
  grow faster, the Storm bell rings before a storm reaches you, the Glass Wyrm bell lets you see through ice, the
  Drowned Archive bell reads Precursor writing aloud. Small, real, and different.
- **Peals.** Particular sets of bells make a peal: a power you play from your tower, or from the handbell on your band
  away from home. *Homeward* (three early bells) takes you home. *Gather* calls your people and Echoes to the yard.
  *Stillness* calms a Big Day at your walls. About twelve peals, each needing bells from different places, so the
  collection has goals inside the goal.

### Where they are (and what each kind teaches)

| Count | From | How you get it | What it sends you to |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | **The Provings** (part 2) | The Great Bells: one from each Warden | The rank ladder |
| 1 | **The Seed** | #00, the Seed Bell, the last | The end of the story |
| 8 | **The Hollow Court** | One in each Sovereign's domain | The end game |
| 16 | **The gate worlds** | One per gate world, from its Lord, at the rank and modifier that world's bell asks for | Every gate theme, harder runs |
| 13 | **The Wilds** | The three legendary beasts, hunts, wonders | The Wild Lands, hunting, exploring |
| 13 | **Villages and Carillon** | A village's founding chime when you're its Friend or end its trouble; Carillon's guild bells (the Registry's handbell on day one, the bell-founders, the Runners' Roost, Choir Square) | People, villages, the city |
| 10 | **The Descent** | One per stratum, found through that stratum's rule | The Deep, stratum by stratum |
| 6 | **The Singing Deep** | Cast at your own Foundry from rare sung metals | Mining, the Foundry |
| 6 | **The Veil** | Spirit bells, heard and taken only in spirit form | The Veil, possession |
| 6 | **The Lucidium** | Dream bells, carried out of lucid dreams | Dreams, lucidity |
| 6 | **The Above** | On the Halo and the sky islands | Flight, the Above |
| 5 | **Big Days** (part 4) | Only from events | Answering the world when it calls |
| 4 | **Your own** | Cast from your Ember, your first Grand room, your first settler's gift, your town's founding | The Hearth itself |

That's every pillar of the game, each with a reason to go there that you can count. The Bell Book shows all hundred
slots from the start: unknown ones show only their rank and a blank; a rumour (from Mira's Map Room, a settler, a spirit,
Teodor's Archive) adds a hint in plain words ("A bell sleeps under the ice where the herd won't cross. B-rank.").

**Pacing.** Two by the end of hour one (the Registry's handbell and your Ember's). About ten by hour four, each from a
different system, so the hours where Euan stopped are the hours where the book starts filling. About thirty at C, fifty-five
at B, seventy-five at A, ninety at S, ninety-nine before the Seed.

### The tower
Your Hearth's bell tower is where they hang. It grows with them: a frame at five, a belfry at twenty, a tower at fifty,
and the Great Belfry (the Hearth's last work, part 3) for the full hundred. It's the monument, seen from far off and heard
for 256 blocks. Visitors hear how far you've come before they see your gate.

### The finish
- **Ring all hundred and you play the full peal.** The Choir's whole song in the Waking world, from your own tower.
- The story can end before that. The six Great Bells and #00 open the Seed, and *Become* and *Break* are open there.
  **Wake**, the ending where the world gets its colour back everywhere at once, needs the full peal. Partial to finish,
  all for the real prize.
- **Afterwards nothing stops.** Your town, your people, your bells' voices and peals stay. Gates, Big Days, the Endless
  and Ascension go on. Ascension (the Become loop) adds *recasting*: rare true-tone castings of bells you own, a long
  tail that is a look and a sound, never power (fits the cosmetic-only money rule).

### With friends, and on a server
- Each player has their own book. A household shares a tower; each member's bells hang in it.
- A server can set **limits**: a few S-rank bells exist in only three copies on the whole server, as Greed Island's rarest
  cards did. That makes a race, and trading, and a reason for guilds.
- A guild can raise a guild belfry from its members' bells.

### Alternatives I weighed
- **The Eight Hearts** (beat the eight Sovereigns, Elden Ring's runes): strong, but it only starts at S. Hours 4 to 80
  stay empty. It survives inside the bells (the Court's eight).
- **Wake the Sleepers** (a hundred dreamers): strong lore, but it lives in one pillar, the Lucidium.
- **Climb the Spire** (a hundred floors, Solo Leveling's castle): simple and clear, but it's one place, so it doesn't send
  you through the rest of the game.

The bells win because they live in every layer, you can hear them, they make your home the monument, and the city of
bells is already who Awaken is (one of the gate worlds is already a Bell Crypt with a Sunken Belfry).

## 2. The Provings: each Reassessment is one great fight

### Today
`hearth/trial/Trials.java` (716 lines): walled arenas in `awaken:trials`. D survives three waves, C waves plus the Path
reveal, B duels a Gate Lord, A five rooms down in four minutes, S a Sovereign's echo. Fail and you try again next
play-day. Euan's longest run ended here: the D trial failed on day 10, and he stopped.

### The rules
- **One Warden, its own hall.** Each Proving is a single boss in a hall built for it alone, at the scale of the best
  rooms in the game (Gate Worlds' look tools, Lens light, 100 to 200 blocks across). The hall changes with the fight's
  phases: water drains, a floor gives way, the roof opens to the sky.
- **Built to the bosses' bar and beyond.** The Living Forge standard is the floor. These are the six fights people
  remember. One Warden first, judged by Euan, the way the bosses started.
- **Known in advance.** From the moment you reach a rank, the Road shows the next Warden's name, its hall and what it
  gives. Halfway to the level you get a glimpse of it (a dream, a vision at the Spire).
- **Fair to retry.** Fall and you wake at the hall's door, ready to go again now, not tomorrow. After three falls the
  Signal says plainly what killed you.
- **Readable.** Telegraphed moves, no swarm of adds, four to eight minutes.
- **With friends.** A party at the level takes it together; it scales.

### The six Wardens (first sketches; the boss plan designs them)

| Rank | Warden | Hall | The fight | The gift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D · level 10 | **The Ringer** | The Drowned Belfry: a flooded nave of hanging bells | A bell-ringer swinging bells on chains; you use the bells as cover, then the water drains and the pit opens | D-rank, the Great Bell *Tenor*, and **the Tether** (its chain) |
| C · level 25 | **The Examiner** | The Hall of Reflections | It fights with your own habits, the powers and moves you use most | C-rank, the Great Bell *Second*, and **your Path** (the Second Awakening) |
| B · level 45 | **The Unmade** | The Broken Span over the void | A colossus you climb (the boss plan's colossus probe) | B-rank, the Great Bell *Third*, and **it rises as your first General** (the Echo bind, done as Solo Leveling's best moment) |
| A · level 70 | **The Sky Warden** | A cathedral breaking apart above the clouds | Fought on the wing | A-rank, the Great Bell *Fourth*, and **Skyborn** |
| S · level 100 | **The Keeper's Shadow** | The Court's threshold | A Sovereign's echo, the Keepers' last test | S-rank, the Great Bell *Fifth*, and **the Court's doors** |
| Sovereign · level 135 | **The Last Loop** | The Cradle's antechamber | You, as the Signal was: every power you've had | Sovereign rank, the Great Bell *Bourdon*, and **the Seed's door** |

Each gift already exists at that rank today (Tether at D, Path at C, Echo binding at B, Skyborn at A, the Court at S).
The Provings route them through one fight, so ranking up gives you something you feel the same minute. Two of them
move: the Tether comes from the Ringer instead of a vault door, and the Sovereign Proving becomes the way into the Seed
instead of coming after it.

Each Warden also leaves its trophy for your Hall: the Ringer's bell, the Examiner's mirror.

## 3. The Hearth: it asks, you fetch, they build

### Today
Tiers ask for rooms and vanilla metals (`hearth/TierRules.java` and `Upgrades.anchorTier`: iron at II, gold and amethyst
at III, diamonds at IV, diamonds, obsidian and a Lord's Heart at V). Settlers come when a Snug bedroom and their trade's
room stand. Oren builds the first cottage over a day. Everything else you build by hand, and after Tier V there's nothing.

### The change
- **Works, not tiers.** The Hearth grows by **works**: a Forge, a Foundry, a Map Room, the Works, the Great Belfry. Each
  work is asked for by the settler it belongs to, in their own words ("If I'm casting bells, I need bronze that sings.
  The miners say it's below the river.").
- **You fetch the rare things.** Each work needs one to three things only adventure gives: a Lord's Heart, sung metal,
  Deepglass, a sky core, a spirit's blessing. Those are what send you into each part of the game, in order, with a reason.
- **They gather the common things.** Timber, stone, iron, food: your people gather and make them over the play-days (the
  Mine head, the fields, the woodlot). You can speed a work by bringing them, never have to. No vanilla grind.
- **They build it.** Scaffolds go up and your people work through a day (Oren's day of building already does this). You
  come home to it finished.
- **Or you build it.** Every work has a plan your people follow in their own style. If you'd rather, build the shell
  yourself: the room reading still reads it, and a Grand room still pays more. Building by hand is a choice, rewarded,
  never a requirement.
- **It gives back to the adventure.** Every work does something you use outside the walls: cast bells and weapons, hear
  rumours, heal, travel, run machines.
- **Your people work for you.** Fields, pens, the Mine head, the Foundry and the Works produce into your home's chests every
  day, and crafting already pulls from them. Machines run off the tower's ring: each register of bells you complete
  powers a bigger tier of the Works.

### The works (first sketch)

| Rank | Work | Asked by | Needs (where it sends you) | Gives back |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E | The Cottage *(exists)* | Oren | Your first Lord's Heart (a gate) | The Anchor; people start to come |
| E | Fields and Pens | Hettie | A herd's young (the Wild Lands), seed from a village you helped | Food every day |
| E–D | The Greenhouse | Ilse | Wild plants (the Wild Lands) | Herbs and tonics |
| D | The Forge | Oren | A Lord's Heart and a pattern from a gate | Tempering and commissions |
| D | The Mine Head | a miner | A sung vein found with the Tuning Fork (the Singing Deep) | Ore every day |
| D | The Map Room | Mira | A Wayfarer's chart and a Lookout | A bell rumour every morning; the Atlas fills |
| C | The Foundry | Oren and a Carillon bell-founder | Bell bronze (the Singing Deep), Deepglass (the Descent's third stratum) | Casts bells and the tower's frame |
| C | The Archive | Teodor | Precursor logs (the Descent), scans (the Halo) | Reads bell hints and peals aloud |
| B | The Sanctuary | Sun | A spirit's blessing (the Veil) | Fracture care; dreams on purpose (the Lucidium) |
| B | The Echo Hall | your first General | The Unmade, bound (the B Proving) | Your legion's home; errands |
| B | The Works | Teodor and a machinist | A sky core and Above plans (the Above) | Machines run off your bells: smelting, sorting, essence |
| A | The Ringing Gate | Mira | A Halo shard (the Halo) | Travel between every place you've belled |
| S | **The Great Belfry** | everyone | **The Heart-clapper, from the deepest room of an S-rank gate**, and a Sovereign's relic (the Court) | Holds all hundred; plays the full peal; your town's monument |

The tier names stay (Homestead to Town) and are earned by works built. The Great Belfry is the Hearth's last work and the
goal's monument at once, and its keystone is exactly what Euan described: something deep in an S-rank gate, so from the
first look at the Hearth's plan you know that's where this ends.

## 4. Big Days: the world calls now and then

Not every night. A few times a week of play, the world does something big, and you choose whether to answer.

**The rules**
- At most one Big Day every four play-days or so, a little more often at higher ranks. Never in a world's first two
  hours, never while you're in a gate.
- **Always warned** at least half a day ahead: what, where, and when, in plain words, with the compass.
- **Your choice.** Skip it and little is lost: a village is hurt and recovers; at your Hearth your people and Echoes hold
  the walls and you get a report at dawn. Answer it and there's something worth having, sometimes one of the five event
  bells.
- **Muffle your tower** before a long trip and the Deep won't come for your bells while you're gone (their voices sleep
  too). You decide when home is in play.

**The kinds**
- **Bell Raid** (at home, from your first Great Bell): your bells are loud now, and the Deep hears them. Waves at your
  walls, a Raid Lord at the end, tide glass at dawn. This is Tide Nights, now and then, with a cause you control.
- **A Break** at a village you know: its gate breaks open; you ride out.
- **A Wandering Lord**: a gate's Lord walks out into the Wilds.
- **A Toll Gate**: Carillon's great bell tolls for a gate twice the size; the city musters; rare, made for groups.
- **The Caravan**: a caravan on your road is ambushed.
- **A Sovereign Stirring** (S and up): a domain reaches the surface for a day and the land around it changes.

## 7. Order and size

At our pace (all of Awaken from the MDK in about 30 hours; the Singing Deep and the villages each one to two days with
several builders). Art and Euan's look rounds come on top.

1. **First, what Euan plays next (hours 0 to 17).** The Road screen; bells as a system (the book, the tower, notes,
   voices, peals) with the first thirty bells; the Ringer and the Examiner; the works up to the Foundry; Big Days' rules
   with Bell Raids and village Breaks. Several builders, about four to six days with look rounds. The Ringer goes first,
   as the bar.
2. **Then the middle (17 to 70 hours).** The Unmade and the Sky Warden; works to the Ringing Gate; bells 31 to 75; the
   Descent, the Above, the Veil and the Lucidium brought up to the bar. A week or more.
3. **Then the end (70 hours on).** The Keeper's Shadow and the Last Loop; the Court's eight domains; the Great Belfry; the
   full peal and the endings; server limits. The biggest phase.

**Risks.** A hundred bells could become a checklist: each one must be a moment, not a drop (rule: no bell is a random
drop). The Provings are six premium fights on top of the bosses already in flight, and Euan's bar for bosses is high.
The works overlap Between the Gates' Hearth and settler code (one owner). Bells overlap Carillon's city sessions (the
city's bells, districts and bell-founders). Nobody plays a hundred hours to test it, so every bell, Proving and work
needs a command to reach it and a CI tour.
