AWAKEN · enchanting plan · for your yes

The Inscribing Table

You asked for enchanting to be on par with the weapons and armour, and linked to them. Here's the plan. Enchantments are found in the world and learned for good. You write them onto your gear at a table of your own, in a short scene with your hands. How well you forged a piece, its rank and your Hearth decide what it can carry.

Your forge decides how strong a thing is. Enchantments add what the forge can't.

Status: plan only. Nothing is built until you say yes.
What's wrong today

Enchanting is an older game sitting next to ours

  • The 23 sculpted armour sets can't be enchanted at all. They aren't in vanilla's armour tags, so the table and anvil refuse them. Someone in Protection IV diamond gets less protection by switching to your best-looking sets. Only the six Hunter sets take enchantments. (Read in the code; stage 0 fixes it on its own.)
  • It does the forge's job. Sharpness and Protection add the same numbers that tempering and tiers add. The fights were tuned on unenchanted gear, so they stack past what the combat sim planned for.
  • It's dice. Lapis, levels and bookshelves give random rolls. Nothing about it knows gates, the forge, ranks or your Hearth.
  • It has no look. Every enchantment is the same violet shimmer, and a Marked weapon wears the same glint with no enchantment on it.
  • It has no path. Books come from the scribe, who now sells only in Carillon and the holdfasts, or from luck in a chest. The Journal and Tasks know nothing about it.
The loop

Find it, learn it, write it, grow it

Find a page

Every kind of gate keeps its own enchantment, and its Lord carries the page. Carillon's market sells a few, and old books still teach theirs.

Learn it

Read the page and you know that enchantment for good. Nothing is rolled, and nothing you've learned is ever lost.

Inscribe it

At your Inscribing Table, lay the piece down, pick from your book and trace the mark onto it with your own hand.

It grows with your gear

A better-forged piece holds more. A higher rank, and your Study, let enchantments go deeper.

The scene

Ten seconds at the table, with your hands

In the Forge Song's family: its camera (cinematic by default, first person in settings), your hands, the sounds, a short game of skill that never fails. Writing an enchantment you've traced cleanly before takes two seconds, from memory.

Lay it down

The camera settles over the desk. Your off hand lays the piece flat on the slate.

The book

Left page: the piece and its room. Right page: what you know that fits, each with one plain line and its price.

Choose

The page lifts, and the enchantment's mark burns faintly onto the blade's flat, the breastplate or the tool's head.

Trace

Your main hand draws each stroke with the table's burin. On the line, the cut fills with lapis light and rings a note.

Wake

The piece lifts and turns. Its new light runs along it, and all its enchantments ring together as a chord.

Storyboard sketches, not game footage. A clean hand (every stroke finished, mostly on the line) gives back a third of the levels. Nothing ever fails, and leaving cancels with everything refunded.

The thirty

What an enchantment may do

Five families, each with its own colour and kind of mark. Vanilla's enchantments keep their names. Ours use the same plain style.

Wards what your armour guards against

A whole suit warded against one Force takes at most 30% less of it from ordinary creatures, and 20% from Lords, Wardens and named beasts. Only Force attacks count, never melee, so bosses stay as hard as the boss fights tuned them.

How it shows

Its own look, on your gear and at work

  • On your weapons and armour. The single violet shimmer gives way to a thin line of script running along the blade or the armour's seams, each enchantment in its own colour, lighting in turn. A Marked weapon keeps its violet glint.
  • Seen doing its job. A ward's mark flashes where the blow lands. A bane leaves a brief mark on the creature. An extra drop glints gold as it falls. Mending's orbs bend into the piece on a thread of light. Depth Strider leaves a faint wake.
  • The table. Dark wood under a slate top, a lapis channel round its edge, your book floating open over a brass stand. In your Study, the bookshelves' letters drift to it, vanilla's best effect redone in our own script.
  • The sound. Heavy leather and pages, the burin on metal or leather, a note per stroke in each family's voice, and the piece's chord when it wakes. Every recording is listed to you first, as for mining.

Journal: a new ENCHANTMENTS category in the catalogue you already have. A found one shows its mark, what it does, what it fits and where you learned it. One not found is "?" and its number, nothing else.

Tasks: every step shows in the K menu, so you know enchanting is there. From your first armour, the Armour and Weapons tabs say "Find an enchantment for your armour" (and weapon), with where pages come from. After your first gate, "Read the page", "Make an Inscribing Table" and "Inscribe your first enchantment" lead in turn. Later, "Inscribe your armour" shows while a piece has room and you know something that fits, and "Set your table in your Study" shows once a piece could go deeper. Each one leaves the moment it's done.

Tooltips: every piece says its room plainly: "Room for one more enchantment".

Sketch of the layout, not the game's art.

Fair to old worlds, safe with friends

Nothing taken away

  • Items keep every enchantment they already have, Sharpness and Protection included. A piece over its room says "Full" until you scrape one off.
  • The vanilla table can't be crafted any more. Any old one you use turns into an Inscribing Table in place. The anvil still repairs and renames, but it won't glue enchantments on. Old books teach what's on them.
  • Knowledge is yours alone and kept through death. Pages can be given to a friend. One person writes at a table at a time. The server judges everything: writing always succeeds, the trace only decides the refund, and leaving refunds everything.

Every stage merges only on green CI and CI tour shots I've reviewed.

Agreed with the weapon forge (its plan, decision 12): it owns the metal (forging, tiers, quality, tempering, attunement). This plan owns what's written onto an item after it's made. There's no enchanting step inside the forge scene. The two meet in one small hook that tells the table how well a piece was forged and its rank. Agreed with the boss fights: banes never touch a boss, wards take at most 20% off a boss's Force attacks, and it adds a warded fighter to its fight sim to tune against.
