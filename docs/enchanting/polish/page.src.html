Part three

The words: one script, seen when it works, six that are new

One script

Today a mark is generated from one of five templates, so a family's marks read as one, and every page shares an icon. The plan gives each inscription a glyph of its own: a frame for its family (a shield, a spear, a cup, a gable, a knot) and its own sign inside. That one glyph is on its page, on the desk, in the trace, in the piece's sockets, on a Word Stone's face, in the Journal and in the air when the inscription works.

Seen when it works

Each of our own inscriptions is redrawn as a lit shape at a size you can see in a fight: more than half a block, half a second or longer.

Six new words

None of the nine inscriptions we wrote makes anything happen that you could watch: five wards, four banes, Clear Blood, Harvest and Insight are all percentages. And 7 of the 15 Lords carry a vanilla name as their gate's own enchantment. Five of those Lords get a word of ours instead, each one something you can see. The vanilla five stay writable and move to the market, old books and Word Stones.

Numbers are first values. None adds damage or armour, fires on a fight moment, shows you anything hidden, or does your work for you. Left out on purpose: running on water (the Frostwalker set owns that), felling whole trees and replanting (they do the work for you).

Part four

Sound: picked by your ear

Since the sound audit every enchanting sound is one of Minecraft's recordings, which is the right start. But the wake, learning a page and the Study's hum are all the vanilla table's own sound, the stroke notes are amethyst chimes pitched up a step each time, and nobody has heard any of it in the game. I can't judge a sound from here. The plan is a local listening page like the weapons', with a first pick and alternatives for each beat, and you choose.

  • The engineer. The server's side of writing is untouched. The risks are drawing: the armour piece needs one line from the armour module, and Lantern's moving light needs its frame time measured on your Mac before it is on.
