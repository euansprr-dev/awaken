Fight Feel

The swings are fixed. What's left is the moment of impact and what happens around it: how a struck creature moves, how a kill lands, whether fighting back mid-wind-up pays off, whether you step into a blow, whether the blade meets the world, and whether the fight has music and presence. Eight changes in four stages, each one switchable.

Plan only, no game code changedBranch claude/weapon-anims-plan-1678a2SPEC §25 (Arms), §11 audio, §21.13 forms
In short
  • The blow lands: creatures move the way the blade went. A side cut throws them sideways, an overhead pins them, an uppercut lifts them. Every struck body recoils and springs back. Today vanilla pushes everything straight away from where you look.
  • The kill counts: a longer freeze, its own sound, the body thrown and falling the way the blade went. The last foe of a fight gets a breath. Today a kill is the same as any hit.
  • Fight back: hitting a creature while it winds up a told blow is a counter. It does 1.5× posture, and ordinary creatures' blows are cut off. The simulator says this adds 13–29% more breaks and keeps fight length the same.
  • Step into it: each strike carries you 0.15–0.45 blocks forward. It stops short of the target and never steps you off a ledge.
  • The world: blows stop at walls. Today a sweep hits through them because nothing checks line of sight. A blade that meets stone clanks off with sparks, and sweeps cut wild grass.
  • Crowds: each extra body in a sweep adds a layer of sound and a bigger kick.
  • Presence: drawing a weapon sounds and moves like drawing one, and the katana's sheath draw becomes visible. Fights outside gates get the layered fight music that gates already have.

Eight decisions at the end, each with my recommendation.

1 · What you asked for

Your words: "using everything you learned, all the entire fighting system... is there anything else that would make this more satisfying and more immersive to use... not just animations, but everything else? I really want it to be extremely satisfying." Then: "plan all of them properly".

The job, the pain, the goal

The job: fighting in AWAKEN, mostly in the overworld against ordinary mobs, and in gates against our creatures and the Lords. You play first person on a Mac with keyboard and mouse.

The pain: the swing now looks right, but what happens after it does not answer it. A creature hit from the side slides straight back. A kill is the same as any hit. A strike into a wall goes through it. Swinging at a winding-up creature earns nothing extra. The weapon is silent until it swings, and most fights have no music.

The goal: every blow gets a physical answer that matches what the blade did. Killing feels final. Reading a tell and striking first pays off. The weapon has weight in the world.

What strong, weak and unacceptable look like

Strong

You can tell from the struck body alone which way you cut. The kill stands out from the hits without anything on screen telling you. A counter feels like a reward you earned. The step makes the chain feel committed. Swinging in a cave clanks and sparks off the walls.

Weak

Things move more but read the same: a random wobble on every hit, a kill flash that looks like a hit flash, a step so small you can't feel it.

Unacceptable

Fights get harder or clumsier: steps that carry you off ledges or into lava, walls that eat blows aimed past a door frame, grass cutting that wrecks your garden, knockback that pulls creatures toward you, any frame-rate cost you can feel, or anything that breaks multiplayer.

Constraints kept

  • The server decides what happens; the client predicts what it can. Everything here must stay safe for multiplayer (your guild server idea).
  • No mixins. Everything here is done with NeoForge events and render-state hooks we already use.
  • Every new feel effect gets a comfort switch, and reduced motion halves it, as the kicks and moments do today.
  • The combat numbers stay in SPEC, and anything that changes balance is run through the combat simulator first.
  • Our own creatures and the levelled forms (C and up) already flinch and fall with their own clips. This plan gives them the blow's direction; it does not replace their animation.

Not in this plan

Third-person swings (already planned after you sign off first person), new weapons, balance work on the Lords (the simulator says the dodger and parrier profiles already miss their targets; that belongs with the bosses work), controller support, gore.

3 · Options, and what I recommend

A · Targeted

Only the server rules: knockback direction, kill hit-stop, line of sight, counter posture. Small and safe. But the vanilla bodies still don't move, the kill isn't seen, and nothing gets the music or the draw. It covers maybe a third of the feel.

B · Recommended: one blow, answered everywhere

Give each blow one description (its direction in the world, its weight, whether it killed, whether it was a counter, how many bodies it hit). The server applies the rules from it and sends it once. Every client, every renderer and the music read it. One rule, one payload, many answers. It reuses CombatFeedback, SwingBlend, the dodge's movement, MusicLayers and the audio kit.

C · Ambitious

Real ragdolls and physics for every body, procedural hit-reaction animation per bone. Much more work, needs a physics layer we don't have, and would fight the forms' and our creatures' own clips. Not worth it while B covers what you'll notice.

Why B: the hard part is consistency. The knockback, the recoil, the death fall, the forms' hurt clips and the sound all have to agree on one direction and one weight, or the blow reads as noise. A single "blow" record gives all of them the same answer. A could be done first and B built on it later, but B costs only a little more and needs no rework.

What would change my mind: if a render-state recoil looked cheap next to the forms' own hurt clips, I'd keep it for vanilla bodies only and let forms use their clips with the direction.

4 · How it works

One server record per landed blow, built where ArmsServer.hit already sends HIT:

Blow (server, per landed hit; sent in the existing arms_result / arms_seen, a new kind or flag set)
  target       entity id
  dir          the blade's travel at the hit, in the world, flat (yaw, from the move's screen direction u and the look)
  lift         -1 pinned (a chop or slam) .. 0 level .. +1 lifted (an uppercut or rise)
  weight       snap 0.6 | blade 1.0 | heavy 1.5, x1.3 for a finisher, a perfect heavy, an execution
  flags        KILL, LAST (no other hostile hunts you within 16), COUNTER, CLEAVE n (bodies this move)
  hitstop ms   as today, plus kill and cleave extra (capped)
One blow, described once on the server, answered by every rule, renderer and sound. Blue edges are new.

The eight changes

Each change uses the same rows: the problem, the change, what it preserves, the cost, and the test. Numbers are first values to start from; the Swing Studio's note command collects your notes on them as you play.

1 · The blow moves the body

ProblemKnockback ignores the swing. Vanilla bodies only freeze and flash.
ChangeServer: while an Arms hit resolves (pendingTarget), LivingKnockBackEvent sets the direction. A side cut pushes 55% away and 45% along the blade's travel (never toward you). A thrust pushes straight away at ×1.15. A chop or slam pushes away at ×0.7 with no lift, so the body is pinned. An uppercut or rise lifts by +0.18. Strength otherwise stays vanilla's, and knockback resistance still applies. A Lord (150 health and up) keeps today's push.
Every client: the struck body recoils on a damped spring once the freeze ends: shoved along the blow by 0.04/0.06/0.09 blocks (snap/blade/heavy), turned 6–12° with the cut, tipped 4–8° away. The spring is ω 18, ζ 0.45, so it settles in about 0.35 s. It is scaled down by body size (a ravager barely rocks, a silverfish flips). The shove and turn use the render state (every renderer); the tip uses a pushed pose in RenderLivingEvent.Pre/Post at the lowest priority, skipped if anyone cancelled the render.
Our creatures and forms: their own hurt clips stay; they read the blow's direction from core.api.CombatFeedback.blow(entityId) instead of "away from the camera".
PreservesVanilla's knockback strength, enchantments, resistance; the freeze; every clip.
CostSideways knockback can push a creature off a ledge it would not fall from today. That's the point, but it's a change. The forms and mobs modules each adopt the direction in their own package (a small cross-module step, SPEC'd).
Testarms_knock_dir: a right-to-left cut moves a zombie at least 30% sideways; a thrust moves it straight back; a chop lifts it less than 0.05. A pure test of the spring (no overshoot past 30%, settled by 0.4 s).

2 · The killing blow

ProblemA kill is the same as any hit.
ChangeServer: after the hit, if the target is dead or dying, flag KILL, and LAST if no other hostile hunts you within 16. The body's death push is ×1.6 along the blow.
Fighter: the freeze grows by 40 ms (capped at 160 ms total), the view's kick ×1.3, and arms.kill plays over the impact: a low body drop with a steel ring tail, by material. The ribbon flares once.
Everyone near: a vanilla body falls the way the blade went. The render-state hook zeroes vanilla's death flip, and our own fall turns about the axis across the blow, with vanilla's timing (√ over 20 ticks), so it still sinks and puffs out as before. Forms and our creatures play their own death clip, turned to fall with the blow.
The last foe: a short breath in the view (field of view out 2.5% over 14 ticks), and the fight music resolves (change 8).
PreservesVanilla's death timing, drops, XP, the poof; every death listener.
CostThe kill flag reaches the fighter about one round trip after the hit. At 50 ms ping it still lands inside the freeze (35–220 ms). At higher ping, the extra freeze is skipped and the sound and fall still play.
Testarms_kill: a kill sends KILL; the last of three sends LAST; a hit that doesn't kill sends neither.

3 · Counter-hits

ProblemStriking a creature that is winding up earns nothing.
ChangeServer: a blow that lands while Strikes.tell(target) is set (a told blow winding up) is a counter. It does posture ×1.5. An ordinary creature (posture 20) has the wind-up cut off (Strikes.stagger, 10 ticks). An elite or beast (posture 40) has it cut off only by a heavy or finisher counter. A Lord never; for Lords it's only posture.
Feel: arms.counter (a dry crack over the impact), the tell's mark shatters in white and gold, a small field-of-view snap (2%). Untold blows (zombies and the like) have no wind-up, so they can't be countered, which keeps "nothing foretells an untold blow" true.
PreservesDeflect and riposte as the defensive skill; the break; every told blow's window.
CostFights against our creatures get a little easier for aggressive players. That is the intent, and the simulator below says by how much.
Testarms_counter: a strike in a told wind-up does ×1.5 posture and staggers a normal creature; the same on a Lord does ×1.5 and no stagger; a strike outside a wind-up is unchanged.

4 · Step into the strike

ProblemThe body stands still through every strike, so a chain feels like waving.
ChangeClient, predicted like the dodge: over the last 2 wind-up ticks and the first active tick, the body gets a forward push of 0.15/0.3/0.45 blocks (snap/blade/heavy). A thrust, heavy or finisher gets ×1.3. Moves with their own motion (plunge, undercut, slipcut) get none. It stops 1.1 blocks from the focused or aimed creature's body. There is no step while holding back, in the air, guarding, in water, climbing, sliding or mantling. It never carries you over a drop deeper than one block, into lava or into a solid block (checked at the step's end). The server accepts it as normal movement; 0.45 blocks over 3 ticks is slower than a sprint.
PreservesThe dodge and its invincibility; soft focus; movement checks.
CostFighting at a cliff edge feels different. The drop check is what stops this from becoming a hazard. Comfort switch strikeStep.
TestA pure rule (ArmsRules.step) tested for distance, the stop at the target, the drop, the lava and the back key.

5 · Blows stop at walls; the blade meets the world

ProblemBlows land through walls. Swinging into stone is silent.
ChangeLine of sight: a creature counts only if a ray from the eye reaches one of three points on it (nearest point, centre, top) without hitting a solid block first. That's lenient, so a creature half behind a door frame is still hit.
The glance: a strike whose active ticks hit nothing, with a solid block face inside its reach along the swing (a fan of 3 rays at the cut's middle), glances off it. The sound and sparks go by the block's sound type: stone and metal a bright clank and sparks, wood a thock and splinters, earth a thud and dust, leaves a rustle with no recoil. In first person the blade bounces back for 2 ticks (an extra offset, like the guard's jolt) and the view kicks 1°. The chain goes on and the block isn't damaged (decision 2). The client predicts it from its own copy of the world; others hear it from the server.
Grass: in the active ticks, wild plants in the arc and reach are cut (short and tall grass, ferns, dead and plain bushes, dry grass; at most 12 a blow): never flowers, crops, saplings or firefly bushes, never inside a Hearth's radius or a village, never in adventure mode. It goes through destroyBlock as a player break, so claims can refuse it, and drops as vanilla's (seeds).
PreservesAimed hits on things that aren't alive (the rift core); cutting webs and leaves under the crosshair.
CostSome blows you land today round corners will miss. A few rays a blow (under 20 per active tick in a crowd).
Testarms_walls (a zombie behind a wall isn't hit; one half behind a door frame is), arms_cut_grass (grass cut, a poppy and wheat left, nothing cut inside a Hearth's radius).

6 · Cleaving a crowd

ProblemThree bodies in one sweep feel like one, only louder.
ChangeThe move counts its bodies. The first plays the normal impact. Each extra body adds arms.cleave one semitone higher (up to 3). The one freeze grows by 12 ms per extra body (up to +36), the kick by ×0.2 per extra body (up to ×1.6). Feel only: no extra damage or Resonance.
PreservesOne freeze per 4 ticks; each body's own impact material.
TestFolded into arms_kill: a sweep through three sends CLEAVE 3.

7 · The weapon's presence

ProblemWeapons are silent until they swing; the katana's sheath bonus can't be seen.
ChangeChanging to a styled weapon plays a draw by its family: blades a steel ring, hafted weapons a wooden thock, the chain-blade a rattle, the Precursor Arm a hum, gauntlets a leather creak. Everyone near hears it (server, on the equipment change). First person gets a 6-tick flourish on top of vanilla's rise (a small wrist roll and settle; halved with reduced motion). The katana: after its 2 s rest the hands lower to a sheathed hold with a soft click, and the drawn strike plays arms.draw_katana (a rasp and ring) with a brighter ribbon. The ×1.7 becomes something you see and hear.
TestThe katana's existing drawn rule is already tested; the sounds go through the listening page.

8 · Fight music outside gates

ProblemOverworld fights have no music. The layered engine plays only in gates.
ChangeA new music provider fight (priority 80: under gates 100 and the opening 160) names awaken:fight/wilds outside gates, Carillon and villages, but only once Intensity reaches combat. It holds through tension and lets go at calm, so a walk stays quiet. It uses the four stems the gates use, with no calm bed: percussion and a low ostinato for combat, horns for the lord level (named beasts, elites hunting you, boss-role mobs). They are made with audio_layers.py like the gate themes, around 8 MB of Ogg (within the 60 MB budget). A LAST kill lets the combat layer go in 3 s with a resolving hit. A vanilla song fades out first, as in gates.
PreservesGate music, city and village soundscapes, the Music slider, cues.
TestThe context resolves only in combat outside gates (a unit test on the provider); then your ears, through the listening page.

State, multiplayer, failure

Saved data: none. Everything here is per blow or per fight, not saved, so there is no migration. The payload kinds are added to the existing arms_result/arms_seen; an old client on a new server ignores the new kinds.

Multiplayer: the server sets the direction, kill and counter. Observers get the same record in arms_seen, so they see the same recoil and the same fall. When two players hit the same creature, the last blow's direction wins.

6 · Testing and stages

Tests: one GameTest per mechanism (arms_knock_dir, arms_kill, arms_counter, arms_walls, arms_cut_grass), pure tests for the spring and the step rule, a unit test on the music provider, the full suite on CI. No tours from the builder. The listening page for every new sound and the music before you hear them in game. Then you play, with the note command from the Swing Studio for anything that feels off.

Gates before merge: all tests green on CI. Counter at 1.5 lowers no profile's win rate and keeps time to kill within ±5% of today. The recoil costs under 0.05 ms a frame with 20 struck bodies. Every switch off gives exactly today's behaviour. The last gate is your verdict in play, stage by stage.

Rollback: every change has a switch; a stage is one revertable merge.

  1. Stage 1, the blow lands: the Blow record, directional knockback, recoil for every body, the kill moment, cleave. The direction API for forms and our creatures (each module adopts it in its own package). About 3–4 hours at our pace.
  2. Stage 2, fight back: counter-hits and the strike step. About 2–3 hours.
  3. Stage 3, the world: line of sight, the glance, grass. About 2–3 hours.
  4. Stage 4, presence: draws, the katana's sheath, overworld fight music with its listening page. About 3–4 hours, most of it the music.

Estimates are from this branch's pace (the Swing Studio took about a day). The music could take longer if the first theme doesn't pass your ears. The forms session is actively changing FormBodies, so its adoption of the direction is timed with that session.

7 · Decisions for you

1. Should blows stop at walls?
A creature counts only when a ray from your eye reaches it (three points, so a body half behind a door frame still counts). Some hits round corners you land today will miss.
Recommend: yes.
2. Should a blade that hits stone be penalised?
Kingdom Come and Mordhau stop your combo when you hit a wall. In Minecraft's caves that would be constant.
Recommend: feel only (clank, sparks, bounce); the chain goes on.
3. Should sweeps cut grass?
Wild grass, ferns, bushes and dry grass only, at most 12 a blow. Never flowers, crops, saplings or firefly bushes, never in a Hearth's radius or a village, and claims can refuse it.
Recommend: yes, as described.
4. Should striking during a wind-up be a counter?
Posture ×1.5. It cuts off an ordinary creature's blow, an elite's only with a heavy, never a Lord's. Untold blows (zombies) stay uncounterable.
Recommend: yes, at 1.5.
5. Should knockback follow the swing?
Side cuts throw sideways (never toward you), thrusts straight back, overheads pin, uppercuts lift. Same strength as vanilla, Lords unchanged.
Recommend: yes.
6. Should your body step into each strike?
0.15 / 0.3 / 0.45 blocks by weight. It stops short of the target, never over a drop, into lava or while backing off. A comfort switch turns it off.
Recommend: yes, on by default.
7. Should fights outside gates get music?
One overworld theme, heard only once a fight starts and gone when it ends. Walks stay quiet.
Recommend: yes, after you've heard it on the listening page.
8. In what order?
Stage 1 (the blow lands) first, since it changes every hit; then fight back, the world, presence.
Recommend: in that order, each stage merged after its tests and your play.
