What's wrong today

It isn't the warning time. It's what you see.

I measured every boss move table and every creature ability against the real dodge (6 ticks, 2.6 blocks), with a quarter-second reaction. Bosses give you a median 1.35 s from the first cue to the hit. The trouble is that for most attacks nothing comes at you, and the aim keeps following you until the last moment.

34%

of the bosses' 148 ranged and effect attacks are something you can see travel. The rest go off under you or hit at once.

0.25 s

before the hit is when 22 of 36 aimed boss attacks stop following you. Nothing shows it. A dash when you first see it gets followed.

58

boss attacks have no class tell at all, so you can't tell a parry from a dash. 12 red, unparryable shots look exactly like the parryable ones.

95%

of boss area attacks poison, blind, slow or lift you. 28 of them push or pull you even when your dodge evaded the hit.

0–34%

7 of 15

Lords lose to a player who just stands and trades if we apply the approved lower-damage curve. Lowering damage isn't the fix.

Also: no boss reacts when you parry it, a parried bolt just breaks, finishers are capped at 8–12% of a Lord's health, and the fight scripts (phrases) were never built for any boss. Reassessments are a walled 48-block box.

The fix · part 1

Five carriers: everything that hurts you travels

Every ranged attack, boss or beast, reaches you through one of five carriers. You see it leave the attacker, or you see where it will land before it does. An aimed attack stops following you at least half a second before it lands, with a flash and a sound. After that flash, a dash gets you out of everything except a wave, which you jump or dash through.

Shot

was: bolts, rings of shots

A glowing core with a trail, in its class colour, humming as it flies. Leaves the hand on the release frame. No homing.

White-gold: parry it back. Red: dash or take cover.

Volley

was: marks under you

Thrown up from the body where you can see it. A ring shows where each will land: where you stood at the throw. It never moves.

Dash out of the ring: rings ≤ 2.2 blocks, 0.7–1.0 s to impact.

Wave

was: instant rings, slams, auras, gusts

A ring that leaves the boss's feet and rolls outward along the ground, about a block tall.

Jump it, dash through it, or stand in its safe middle. 0.6–0.9 s to reach you at 8 blocks.

Line

was: beams, cones, fangs, roots

A beam aims, then locks with a flash and a sound before it fires. Roots and fangs race from the boss along the ground.

Step off the line after the lock.

Body

was: charges, leaps, pulls

The boss itself. A leap shows its landing ring when it locks. A pull is a visible draw of wind or water, slower than your dash, and never hurts by itself.

Sidestep, leave the ring, dash against the pull.

Clouds, fire and auras become fields that a volley or wave leaves behind: a visible edge, no harm for the first half second, never put down on you, small enough to walk out of.

The fix · part 2

Colour is the class, every time

The same four colours you already see on melee tells go on everything: the boss's flare, the shot's core, the ring's edge, the wave's crest. The theme's colour stays on the body and the smoke. Every move that hurts gets a class, and each carrier plays its own sound from where it is, so you can hear it coming.

White-goldParry, block or dash. A parried shot flies back.
AmberParry or dash. It breaks a block.
RedDash or jump only. Can't be parried.
VioletA grab. Dash only.
  • Not everything parries: at least 40% of each boss's blows white-gold or amber, at least 20% red, the rest waves and fields you move out of.
  • Effects only from a hit that landed. A dodge or a parry stops all of it: poison, blindness, the pull, the lift.
  • One signature effect per boss at most (the Mother's poison, the Abbot's chill), on its signature moves only.
The fix · part 3

A duel with a rhythm and a payoff

phrase of 2–5 blows→ you parry, dodge, punish→ opening 0.8–1.5 s→ posture fills→ it breaks and falls→ your finisher
  • Phrases, not single blows. Bosses fight in strings of two to five blows (single blows are 52–100% of what they do today), ranged when you're far, close when you're close. Two deflects in a row and it answers with a red blow; back off and it closes the gap.
  • An opening after every phrase, where it starts nothing. That's your punish.
  • Parry pays. The boss's limb recoils with sparks, a clang and a short freeze. Parry the last blow of a phrase and it staggers. A parried white-gold shot flies back into it.
  • The break. Parries, perfect dodges and punishes fill its posture strip. Full, it drops to a knee and your finisher lands with its own animation, worth up to 15% of a Lord's health (8–12% today). Two to four breaks a Lord fight.
  • Weight. Bosses shrug off light hits mid-swing and flinch from heavy ones, parries and hits in openings. Hits sound like what they hit: bronze, glass, bark.
  • No add spam. One summon move at most, two adds alive, never in the first phase.
  • Difficulty. Blows keep hurting enough that trading loses. Each Lord's health is tuned with the combat sim to the targets you approved: trading wins 10% at most, a good dodger 60%+, a good parrier 80%+, fights 80–120 s.

Beasts

  • Named beasts, hunt quarries and gate monsters attack through the same machinery, so all 247 of their ranged and effect attacks get the carriers, colours and the effect rule in stage 1.
  • Big beasts topple when their posture breaks: down on their side for a few seconds, your opening.
  • Pack hunters and the Gnawed get a short told lunge (a crouch and a growl, parryable), and only two of a pack strike at once.
Reassessments

The Provings: harder every rank

The six Wardens you approved with the Journey, each in its own hall built for one fight (100–200 blocks across, Lens light, changing with the phases), never announced. Each must test harder than every gate Lord of its rank and than the Warden before. They get harder through longer phrases, more mix-ups, more phases, tougher posture and changing halls, never through cheap hits: red blows stay told at least 0.6 s at every rank.

Animation

On Fresh Animations' level, measured

Today's meters check kinks and stillness, and all 41 bosses pass. They don't check what makes FA feel alive. Six new meters, and your eye on the three pilot bosses in game as the real bar:

  • Feet that plant: a planted foot slides at most 0.03 blocks a tick, in moves and turns.
  • Turning with weight: head, then chest, then hips; the feet step on a big turn; no snap.
  • Seams: every move starts from what the body was doing and flows back into walking.
  • Release: a shot leaves the hand on the frame the hand lets go.
  • Reactions: every boss gets parried, staggered, knocked down and getting up; big beasts a topple.
  • Follow-through: cloth, chains, tails and hair lag the body and settle.

FA is the reference we film beside ours. None of its files come into the repo.

Three fights you know

The Mother of Spores (E)

Today

All four of her ranged moves poison. Spore Rain marks the ground under you and strikes with nothing falling. Spore Bloom is an instant ring you can't dash out of from 8 blocks. Her shots look parryable whatever their class.

After

She shakes pods up from her cap, rings show where you stood, they fall 0.8 s later and you dash out. The Bloom rolls out as a wave you jump. Poison only if the Bloom catches you. Parry her white-gold shots back into her.

The Drowned Archivist (D)

Today

Undertow and Ink Cloud have no class tell and land 0.75 s after the first dust. Undertow drags you in from 12 blocks whether you dodge or not. The cloud lands on you, wider than a dash, and pulses for 5 s.

After

Undertow is red: the water runs toward her, slower than your dash, then her wave rolls out. She spits the ink as a globe you watch fly; it bursts into a small pool where it lands. Blindness only if the globe hits you.

Delivery

Five stages, each one playable

  1. See it coming

    Carriers, the colour language, the lock, a class on every move, the effect rule and the beasts' told lunge, for every boss and beast at once.

    Gate: tests green, every attack escapable at the lock in the reach model, a CI tour of each carrier mid-flight. About 1–2 days.

  2. The duel, on three pilots

    The Bell Warden (a figure), the Glass Serpent (a serpent) and the Gearwright Colossus (a giant): phrases, openings, parry recoil, shots flying back, breaks and finishers, hit sounds, the six animation meters.

    Gate: you play these three in game, then go or no-go for the rest. About 1–2 days.

  3. The Ringer

    The first Proving and the Drowned Belfry, replacing the D box. Early, because every player meets it around hour four.

    Gate: you play it. About 1–2 days.

  4. The other 38 bosses and the fighting beasts

    To the pilots' bar, in batches by body type, each Lord at its sim target.

    Gate: a close CI tour per batch. About 3–4 days.

  5. The other five Wardens

    One at a time, each replacing its box trial.

    Gate: you play each. About 1–2 days each.

I build all of it myself. Each stage merges on its own, so reverting one restores the old behaviour. What I can't check: the feel in your hands, the sounds, and FA side by side in game.

Your call

Eight decisions

Everything that hurts at range travels or is drawn first

Five carriers; an aimed attack locks visibly at least half a second before it lands. Marks under you become thrown volleys; instant rings become waves you jump or dash through.

Recommended: yes

What a parry beats

White-gold: parry, block or dash. Amber: parry or dash. Red: dash or jump only. At least 40% of each boss's blows parryable, at least 20% red. A parried white-gold shot flies back into the boss's posture.

The other way: no shot can be parried, only dashed.

Recommended: yes

Effects only from a landed hit

One signature effect per boss, on its signature moves, never through a dodge or parry.

The other way: no effects from bosses at all.

Recommended: yes

The payoff

Parries, perfect dodges and punishes fill its posture; a break drops the boss; your finisher is worth up to 15% of a Lord's health; every parry makes it recoil.

Recommended: yes

Difficulty by skill, not by less damage

Keep blows that hurt and tune each Lord's health with the new sim to your approved targets, instead of the never-applied lower-damage curve that lets trading beat 7 of 15 Lords.

Recommended: yes

The Provings ladder

Six Wardens in their own halls, each harder than its rank's Lords and the one before, by the table above, never announced. Each box trial goes when its Warden passes your look.
