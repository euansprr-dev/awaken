Fights That Read You

Every boss and fighting creature stops rolling dice at you. It watches what you've started, moves like a fighter between blows, changes its mind halfway through a string, and takes turns in a pack. You win by reading it back. This is the plan. Nothing in it is built until you say yes.

The goal

What "smart" has to mean

It can't mean a cleverer computer. It has to be something you notice with the sword in your hand. So these are the promises, and each one is something you could point at.

  1. Every blow means something. Aimed at you, it lands unless you answer it. Done, and waiting for your playtest.
  2. It reacts to what you've already started. Drink, wind up a heavy, cast, turtle or run, and about half a second later it does something about it. It never reacts to a button.
  3. You can see that it noticed. Its head snaps round, its weight drops, you hear it. A reaction nobody sees looks like more randomness.
  4. It moves like a fighter between blows. It circles, steps in, gives ground, stops and watches you. The quiet moments are when it's scariest.
  5. Its rhythm can catch you out, and you can learn it. Same opening, different endings. A blow held a beat too long. A slow one after a fast one.
  6. It has a temper, and that changes. It tests you, then hunts you, then gets desperate. You could describe it in a sentence.
  7. You can use all of this against it. Fake a drink to bait the dive. Step behind it and punish the turn. If it reads you, you can lie to it.
  8. Packs take turns. They spread out and come at you one or two at a time.
StrongWeakNot acceptable
After a fight you can say why it did what it did. "It went for me when I drank." You lose and know what to try next. More animations on the same dice. Or it reacts, but you can't tell that it did. It reads your button presses. It catches you with no warning. It dances around and won't attack. Five things hit you at once. Anything derpy or cheap-sounding.

Not in this plan: more health, damage or speed for bosses; more minions; learning AI; new buttons for you; anything that announces a boss before you meet it. The fair damage numbers, the tells and their colours, the five carriers and the openings all stay exactly as they are.

The plan

Six parts

1. It watches what you've started

Why
The server already knows when you raised your guard, dodged, began charging a heavy, or started using an item. None of that reaches the boss's choice.
Change
Each decision reads a short list about its target: drinking or eating, guard up and for how long, a heavy winding up, a power being cast, stuck in recovery, just dodged, running away, low on health. A fact counts only after it has been true for the foe's reaction time: about 0.6 s for an E-rank foe down to 0.35 s for the highest ranks, as a first guess.
You get
It only knows what someone watching you could know.
Keeps
Server decides everything. Worked out per target, so it stays right with several players.
Cost
The server has no single "is casting" fact yet, so Powers needs one small public call.
Test
Start a drink: nothing changes before the reaction time, and the boss acts after it.

2. It answers, and you see it notice

Why
Nothing connects what you do to what it does next. That's what "random" is.
Change
A short list of answers made from moves it already has (the table below), each with a cooldown and a chance. They start only between blows or at a fork, and a swing that has begun is never cancelled. Before it answers it shows you: head snaps round, weight drops, a short sound.
You get
"It went for me when I drank." And the chance to bait it.
Keeps
Every blow keeps its tell, its colour and its minimum warning.
Cost
New short sounds per body type that I can't hear. You'd be the first.
Test
One test per answer.

3. It moves like a fighter

Why
0.7% sideways. Bodies have idle, walk and run, and you said turning around looks bad.
Change
Between strings it circles either way, steps in, gives ground, or stops and watches, for half a second to three seconds, always facing you. Strafe and back-step get built where walking is built, once per body plan. Fliers drift and bob, casters glide. How long it waits depends on its temper and whether it's winning.
You get
Pauses with tension in them. Time to breathe and read it. Fewer of the big turns that look bad, since it stays facing you.
Keeps
Feet locked to the ground, by the same rule as the walk.
Cost
The biggest looks risk in the plan. Fewer blows per minute, so fights need retuning.
Test
Share of time moving sideways, close CI shots for sliding feet, then your eyes.

4. Strings that can change their mind

Why
A string is fixed at its first blow. Tempo is flat. Repeats are common.
Change
Up to two fork points in a string, where the next blow depends on what you're doing right then: guard up brings the guard-breaker, rolled away brings the lunge, still there brings the second cut, out of reach and it breaks off. Any class of blow can be held a beat. A move can't follow itself, each has a cooldown, and distance bands get tighter.
You get
Same opening, different endings. Something to learn.
Keeps
Branches look identical until the fork, so reading still works. Every string still ends in an opening.
Cost
Every fight script needs forks written, from templates with hand edits.
Test
Repeat share, a fork test, the fair-damage sweep.

5. A temper and a plan

Why
All 47 fights make decisions the same way, so they feel alike under the skin.
Change
Six tempers as data: Hunter, Duellist, Brute, Beast, Sentinel, Keeper. They set how close it likes to be, how long it waits and which answers it prefers. Each phase has a plan you can feel. Its mood moves with the fight: it presses when it's hurting you, turns wary when you keep parrying it. Each boss also gets one trick of its own.
You get
You could describe any boss in a sentence, and no two feel alike.
Keeps
Moves, numbers and phases.
Cost
Writing per boss. A trick sometimes needs a little code.
Test
Probe shows tempers behave differently. You play them.

6. Packs take turns

Why
"Too many blazes." Nothing coordinates them.
Change
Each target has a set number of attack turns, and a creature needs one to begin an attack. Those waiting spread out around you and circle at a distance. Big attacks cost more. A boss's helpers count too: while they're on you, the boss eases off.
You get
A pack that feels organised and flanks you. You don't get hit by five at once.
Keeps
Creature stats and tells. A lone creature is unchanged.
Cost
Packs deal less damage in a burst, so gate difficulty needs checking.
Test
Attackers at once never pass the limit. Tick cost.

On your earlier verdict. Derpy and rough: footwork is built the way the walk is built, and any body whose footwork doesn't pass your eyes stays on today's behaviour rather than shipping bad. Bad sounds: I can only check these by numbers, so you hear them at each pilot. Too many blazes: part 6, and no new minions anywhere. Not Elden Ring: promises 2 to 7 are what that means in practice.

How it fits together

One decision, in a fixed order

Today
distance, behind, above→dice over the pool→whole string chosen→blows→opening↻
Proposed
watch what you've started→a turn free? (packs)→phase change→an answer due?→a fork?→dice + cooldowns, no repeats→aim→tell→blow→opening→footwork↻

New steps are marked. Everything else exists and carries on unchanged: FightRunner, the move tables, the aim, the tells.

Rules that never bend

  • It never reads input, only what has started and only after its reaction time.
  • Every blow keeps its tell, its colour and at least its minimum warning. A fork or a hold can only add warning.
  • A blow begins only if it can land (the aim from part 1 of this job).
  • Every string still ends in an opening.
  • No extra damage, health or speed comes from any of this.
  • Everything is decided on the server for each target, so a future guild server works the same.

What gets saved

Nothing new. What it has read about you and its mood last for the fight only, and go when it resets. Fight files gain optional temper, forks and answers. A file without them plays as it does today, and the schema version goes up by one. The read goes through a public call in core.api, so the modules stay separate.

Order of work

Pilot first, then everyone

  1. The recorder

    The probe becomes a test. Baselines for packs and fight length.

    Gate: CI. Hours.

  2. The mind, on two pilots, with no new art

    Watching, answers, forks, cooldowns and held blows on the Hive Regent and the Gearwright, using the clips they already have.

    Gate: tests, then you play. About a day. If this already feels right we'll know early, before spending anything on art.

  3. Footwork

    Strafe and back-step per body plan, the waiting and circling, on the pilots.

    Gate: CI shots, then your eyes. About a day. This is the stage that could fail on looks.

  4. Tempers, moods and notice sounds

    Pilots get a temper, phase plans and a trick of their own. Short notice sounds rendered.

    Gate: you play and listen. About a day.

  5. All 47 bosses

    In batches by body plan. Temper, forks, answers and a trick each.

    Gate: the recorder per boss and a close CI tour per batch. Two or three days.

  6. The creatures

    Turns, spacing and circling for about 205 kinds, and one reaction per family.

    Gate: pack test, then you play a gate. A day or two.

  7. Balance

    The combat sim learns the aim and the mind. The fair-damage sweep runs again, fight lengths checked.

    Gate: sweep and sim. Half a day.

I build all of it myself, as with Part 1. Estimates are scaled from how long Part 1 took and could easily be off. Each stage merges on its own, and removing a boss's temper puts it back on today's behaviour. What I can't check: the feel in your hands, the sounds, and the look in a real game.

Your call

Seven decisions

Give every fight a mind

Keep the moves, tells and aim. Add the six parts above, pilots first.

The other way: only tune the dice (under a day, and it still won't look at you).

Recommended: yes

Drinking can get you hit

If it can reach you, it comes for you about half a second after you start. Far away is safe.

The other way: healing is always safe.

Recommended: yes

Dodge-spam gets caught

On your third dodge in a row it holds a beat and hits where you land. It never reacts to the button.

The other way: it ignores dodges, and spamming stays safe.

Recommended: yes

How far feints go

Held blows and strings that stop short, for everyone. A true fake, where a wind-up turns into nothing, only on the top-rank duellists and the Wardens.

The other way: no fakes at all, or fakes for everyone (very punishing with this many tells).

Recommended: held blows everywhere, true fakes rarely

How relentless they are

About half the time mid-move, instead of three quarters, with circling and watching in between. Fight length stays about the same, and I'd retune health to hold it there.

The other way: keep them swinging and only add reactions. More pressure, less tension.

Recommended: about half

How many can attack you at once

Easy gates: two close and one ranged. Hard gates: three and two. A boss's helpers: one at a time while the boss is attacking. First guesses, adjusted after you play a gate.

The other way: stricter (one at a time) or looser.

Recommended: these numbers to start
