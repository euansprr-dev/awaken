Gate World Depth

The gate rooms still read as a floor, four walls and a lid. This plan gives the places in every gate world ground that rises and falls in soft half steps. Walls change as they go up, roofs sit at more than one height, and heaps and formations fill the space between. The further a spot is from the way you walk, the more it has grown over, the way the overworld gets wilder away from people. Nothing gets built until you say yes.

What you asked for

"It still feels like walls and floors and a roof… look at the terrain generation for the overall. A lot of half slabs and shapes… the more wild it is, the more overgrown it is… I want you to create a whole new plan that's going to basically add to every single one of the rooms… so that it truly feels like a place that you're in instead of just a fabricated room."

The job. Walking a gate floor should feel like exploring a place that formed over time, the way walking the overworld does. Looking around should be a pleasure in itself.

What fails today. You said the newest gate is much better, but inside a place the floor is flat, the walls go straight up, and there's one roof. What detail there is is thin and spread evenly.

What you pointed to. The overworld's ground: half slabs and shapes, wild patterns, more overgrowth where the land is wilder, and variety that looks natural.

What has to keep working. Every fight, key, cache, touchstone, Lord's arena and Rig room works as it does now. Every floor can be walked from the arrival to the end. Gates already open in your saves keep working, and floors stay inside the performance budgets.

Strong result

In any place, the ground steps up in half slabs to a shelf, dips where something has pooled, and banks up into the corners. A wall leans out over you. The roof is at three heights. It's all in the theme's own materials, no two places look alike, and the way through is still an easy walk.

Weak or unacceptable

Weak: the same flat rooms with more props scattered on them, floors bumped by random noise, or shapes that repeat in every room. Unacceptable: a place you can't walk into, a monster spawning inside the ground, a buried key, a blocked doorway, dropped frames, or a gate you're standing in changing shape.

What the rooms do today

I read the code and measured every world. Here is what I found.

In the code

  • 85 of the 90 worlds are carved by one engine, the cave engine. Open lands, isles, the fold and the endless plain are its variants. Only the Bell Crypt's five worlds have engines of their own: hall, passages, shaft, void and the secret rest.
  • The floor is one height. Every place is cut as open space between one floor line and its roof, so every column of floor in it is the same height.
  • The walls go straight up. A place's outline is drawn once from above and pushed straight up. Height plays no part in it, so nothing leans, overhangs or makes a ledge.
  • The roof is one dome over the middle of each place, roughened by about a block and a half up or down.
  • Every surface is a full block. None of the roughly 190 theme blocks has a slab, stair or layer shape.
  • Detail is an even scatter. The Hive, for example, hangs a drip on about 7% of its roof spots and stands a candle on 2% of its floor, the same everywhere in a place apart from near the doors.

What the overworld does that the gates don't

  • Soft steps (the Soft Land, SPEC 31.2). A column one block lower than its neighbour gets a half slab of its own ground, which cut one-block risers by 97% on rolling land.
  • Boulders are half sunk and crowned with slabs. Pebbles, twigs and fallen logs lie in drifts, as clusters around a spot rather than an even scatter (SPEC 31.3).
  • Wildness (SPEC 41.1): the land is tamed near people and grows wilder with distance, and the plants thicken with it.

The gates have none of these layers.

What it isn't. The textures are rich and the light is good, so neither of those is the gap; the shape of the space is. If the Hive still reads as a room once its ground and walls change, then the cause is the plan outline (places too round and even). Then we'd add lobes and side chambers to the outlines next.

Ways to do it

A. Dress the rooms more

More props, plants and heaps on today's flat floors.

Cheap and safe, but the floor stays flat, the walls stay straight and the roof stays one dome, which is exactly what you called out.

B. Give the engine real ground recommended

Ground heights, walls that change with height, roof levels and formations. Add soft steps, a way-and-wild field, and each theme's own ground.

One engine change reaches 85 worlds, and it keeps the programme, the dress and every test that guards them. Heaps and drifts from A are folded in.

C. Rebuild as full 3D noise caves

Caves made from 3D noise the way the overworld's own caves are: the most natural shapes possible.

It loses the control that keeps fights, keys and doorways working, and the walk guarantee with it. It would carve slower, and it's a rewrite. Keep it in reserve in case B falls short.

The plan

Seven layers, built into the cave engine and the dress every theme shares, then given each theme's own materials and habits.

  1. Ground that rises and falls. Each place gets its own ground: gentle rolls, terraces stepping up toward a wall, dips and basins with something pooled in them (honey, water, ink, a crust of lava), mounds and humps. Some spots stay flat because they have to: every doorway's mouth, the arrival, every key, cache and touchstone, lake shores, and the ground round hero pieces and built rooms.
  2. Soft steps. Where the ground steps up a block, a half slab of that ground's own stone sits on the lower side, as on the overworld. On the way you walk you never have to jump. Stairs sit at the feet of walls, and turned upside down where the roof steps, so floors and roofs meet walls in a slope, not a corner.
  3. Walls that change as they go up. Bands of ledges and overhangs, alcoves and bulges, and walls that lean in toward the roof or out over you. Each theme adds its own ribs: comb, roots, girders, shelving.
  4. A roof at more than one height. Vaults rise into chimneys and openings. Low masses hang down (drip clusters, rock teeth, caps, icicles), and ribs and arches span the space.
  5. Things standing in the space. Outcrops half sunk with slab crowns, pillars from floor to roof that flare at both ends, fallen pieces (columns, gears, comb, shelves) and heaps of rubble. They come in clusters, never as an even scatter.
  6. The way and the wild. This is the gate's version of the overworld's wildness. The line you walk from door to door is trodden: clean, worn ground. The further a spot is from it, the wilder it gets: heaps, drifts banked against the walls, growth thickening, broken floor. Corners and dead ends are the wildest. Where a place hosts a fight, its middle stays open ground.
  7. Drifts and bits. Each theme gets a drift block that piles up in layers, one to eight deep like snow: pollen, ash, spores, leaf litter, paper, sand, snow, soot. It banks into corners and against walls, so floors meet walls in a slope. Small bits lie underfoot too: wax crumbs, slag, shards, bolts, pages.

High ground (decision 5): ledges and shelves off the way that you reach with a jump or a mantle, for a view or a quiet corner. No fights are placed up there.

New blocks, about 120. About 90 slabs and stairs cut from each theme's main ground and wall stones. They keep the stones' own textures, so they need no new art. Each theme also gets a drift layer and a block of small bits, about 30 in all, with new art in the theme's style made by the existing art scripts. Outside gates they drop themselves like the theme's other blocks; inside a gate they can't be broken, the same as now.

How it's built

The contract

  • When. At plan time, once the chain and places are laid and before anything is carved, the engine builds the floor's relief. That's three small grids over the plot: ground height in half blocks, roof height, and how wild each spot is. They're about 200 KB a floor, kept with the floor's cached layout and rebuilt from the saved plan like everything else. Nothing new is saved.
  • The carve follows the ground grid. Walls take a height term (bands, lean, alcoves), the roof reads its grid, and formations are cut from the grids too.
  • The dress stands on the ground. Every place in the 17 dresses that uses the old floor line (about 400 in the code) asks for the ground at that spot instead.
  • The surface pass runs last on each chunk. It adds soft steps from the ground grid; the whole floor's grid is known, so nothing breaks at chunk edges. It also adds stairs at wall feet and roof steps, drifts by wildness, and bits.
  • Spawns and features. On a world floor, the spot a monster spawns on is read from the ground. Spawn spots are only on whole-block ground with a level patch round them, and keys, caches and touchstones sit on their flat pads. The hidden lights hang at head height over the ground.
  • Old gates. The gate content version goes to 7. A version 6 floor carves exactly the blocks it carves today, which a test checks block for block over 40 plans. As with every gate update, gates nobody has entered are rebuilt as version 7 on the next start, and a gate someone entered keeps its version to the end.
  • The guarantee. Once the ground is built, the plan walks it: a quick walk over the grid from every doorway to every other doorway, the arrival, every spawn spot and every feature. Steps are at most one block, and on the way at most a half step. If a place fails, its relief is halved and checked again, then made flat, so the floor always builds. The log names the place that was eased and why.
  • Recipes. Each world can tune its ground with new optional numbers (how much relief, how wild, how many ledges). Only version 7 floors read them, so a released world's shape never changes.
  • Multiplayer. Everything comes from the saved plan, so every player sees the same ground.

Performance

The budgets stay as they are: a floor plans in under 50 ms, and a chunk carves in under 4 ms on average and 40 ms at worst. Every world's test measures both. The grids should add a few milliseconds to a plan; I'll measure that in stage 0, not promise it. On the client, slabs and stairs are as cheap as they are on the Soft Land. The bits are what would cost frames, so they're capped. Frame-time checks stay on the Mac, so the busiest world gets one Mac check before roll-out, run by the orchestrator or you.

An ordinary place

Before: you step into a Comb cavern. The wax floor is flat, the hex walls go straight up, there's a dome overhead, and candles and drips are scattered evenly.

After: the way in is packed, clean wax. To one side the floor dips to a seep of glowing honey, and on the other it steps up in wax half slabs to a comb shelf along the wall. Pollen banks into the corners. Comb bulges out of the walls at three heights, and a cluster of drips hangs over the seep.

Your screenshot

Before: on the Hive's floor 1 the honey patches lie flat on a flat floor, the comb walls are sheer, and the roof is one height.

After: those patches are pools in low ground with wax rims. The walls step and lean, and the roof breaks into a vault and a lower shelf with drips. What's in front of you, the space behind it and the far wall now sit at different depths.

A fight

An elite room hosted in a cavern keeps a fighting floor in its middle: gentle ground with soft steps. The heaps, shelves and drifts stay at the edges. Monsters spawn on level whole blocks. A Lord's arena and the Rig rooms aren't touched.

Something goes wrong

Say one place's ground would leave a cache out of reach. The plan's walk finds it, halves that place's relief and checks again; if it still fails, that place goes flat. The floor builds either way, and the log says which place on which floor was eased. You never get today's plain rooms just because the ground misbehaved.

Order of work

  1. Stage 0, the safety net (no visible change). Add the golden test for version 6 floors, the depth test, the content version 7 switch, ground-aware spawns and features, and a server switch, gateWorldsDepth: off means new gates are made at version 6. Measure the plan cost.
  2. Stage 1, the kit and the Hive. All seven layers in the cave engine, dressed for the Hive's three cave worlds. CI shots before and after go to you.
  3. Stage 2, every other theme. Each theme's ground, the new blocks and their art, open lands and isles, and the rare worlds through the looks they wear.
  4. Stage 3, the Bell Crypt's own engines. Worn and sunken flagstones, fallen tracery, rubble, the crypt's drifts.
  5. Stage 4, high ground, if decision 5 says so.

Rolling back. Each stage merges and works on its own. If something's wrong, the server switch makes new gates at version 6 again, and gates already made keep the shape they were made with. Nothing saved has to change.

Effort. About a day to a day and a half of building, judged by how the Gate Worlds went: stages 2 to 6 (over 60 worlds and about 150 blocks) took about a day. Two things could change that. If the art for drifts and bits needs more than one pass, slabs, stairs and the ground ship first. If fights read badly on uneven ground, the fight floors get flatter.

Who builds it. This session, after your yes, with no sub-agents. I run no tours on the Mac; CI runs the tests and the shots.

Decisions for you

1. Which gates change

→
New gates, and gates nobody has entered yet (rebuilt on the next start, as every gate update does). Gates you've been into keep today's rooms until they close.
·
Also reshape the parts of entered gates nobody has loaded yet. You'd see seams where old ground meets new.

2. How bold the ground is

→
Gentle on the way and on fight floors, bold at the edges. On the way, half steps only and never a jump. Away from it, heaps, shelves and ledges up to four blocks high.
·
Gentle everywhere (two blocks at most).
·
Wild everywhere: big rises even on the way, some needing a jump or a mantle.

3. New blocks

→
Yes to about 120. Slabs and stairs from each theme's stones keep their textures; drifts and bits get new art in each theme's style. No style-sheet stop: you see them in the Hive's shots.
·
Send a style sheet for the drifts and bits first, and wait for your OK before they go into the worlds.

4. When you look

→
Build the Hive first and send you the shots, then keep going through every theme unless you say stop.
·
Stop after the Hive and wait for your go.

5. High ground

→
Include ledges and shelves you can climb to in the big caverns, with no fights up there. Leave full second storeys, with galleries and bridges you fight on, for a later plan once you've walked the new ground.
·
Do second storeys now as well.
·
No high ground for now.
