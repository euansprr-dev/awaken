Gates in Motion

Every stretch between fights asks for real moves (slide, mantle, wall run, wall jump, hang, grip), strung in a new order every run, harder as the rank goes up, with small fights on the stairs and ledges. The rolled crawls come first, then every other kind of gate.

Why it's boring today

Everyone has every runner move from the first second, but no gate ever asks for one. Underneath all of it is one cause: the gate's checker only knows walking and the sprint jump, so the generator only builds what it can prove.

The jumps are simple by design

All 8 crossings are sprint jumps over 1 to 3 cells, level or down. The code says "nothing is ever higher than what you jump from" (CrawlLand.java:723). A run has one of them.

The spiral is the same everywhere

Every shaft is one spiral: 1.5 turns, 24 down, the same size at E and at S (GeneratedCrawl.java:665). Nobody is on it.

Rank only adds waves

Rank changes how many waves a stand has, and nothing else. An S gate's jumps are an E gate's jumps.

17 kinds, 2 lists of crossings

Each kind has its own stone, but the crossings come from just two lists, one for dug places and one for built places. The kinds look different but move the same.

Fights never meet movement

Monsters only appear in dens, stands, Wardens and the Lord. A leg you move along has no monsters (RealmLive.java:183).

The good news

The pieces we need already exist: the moves' own rules can be tested, fall catches, required-move declarations from the Gauntlet, the gold lesson figure, Flow and plan versions. This plan joins them up.

Every family of gate: the 17 rolled crawls; the designed crawls (the Broodwarren, the Leaper's Gauntlet); the designed realms (Amber Reach, Hour Crypt, Kingsroot Keep, Saltcomb, Vine Seat, Quiet Barrow, Wax Galleries); the rolled lands (Ember, Frost, Fungal, Glass and Temple countries, all off today); and the older Gate World floors, which Stage 0 checks to see whether any still roll.

Three ways to do it

A · about a day

Four new crossings

A wall-run gulf, a slide gate, mantle steps and a hang lip, plus fights on shaft landings. But it's still one set piece a run, the same every time, with no change by rank, and the other levels aren't touched.

B · recommended · 3-5 days

Pieces strung by seed

24 hand-made pieces, a composer that strings them into every leg by seed, kind and rank, a checker that proves each line with the body's own rules, six shafts, and fights while you move.

C · risky

Fully generated parkour

Shape the ground from a simulated runner. The most variety on paper, but hard to make look built, hard to keep fair, and slow. It stays a later experiment.

Why B: "actually thought out and a ton of different ones" means pieces made by hand, and "always feel like a unique gate" means the order and the sizes change with the seed and the kind. It's the same idea good platformers use: short groups of moves, each checked playable, strung together (Launchpad, 2011), and hand-made room templates with random chunks (Spelunky).

The kit: 24 pieces in five families

Each piece is hand-made, rolls its sizes within its rank's range, is built in the kind's own stone, and has a fall catch around it. Every move uses the body's real numbers. For example, a wall run reaches about 11 blocks, so no wall-run gap is ever wider than 9.

Low · slides

  1. Slide gate: under a fallen beam or a half-shut door
  2. Slide jump: slide off an edge, and the jump carries further
  3. Chute: a long stair or ramp you slide down
  4. Landing slide: drop and land into a slide under a low roof
  5. Closing teeth C+: slide under a press or a bulkhead on its cycle
  6. Rail run: rails, coffins or benches to vault in a row

Wall

  1. Wall-run gulf: 5 long at E, up to 9 at A and S
  2. Chimney: two walls close together, wall-jump up
  3. Switchback: run the left wall, jump, run the right
  4. Run to hang: the run ends at a lip you catch
  5. Corner run: round an inside corner on the wall

Ledge · mantles

  1. Mantle steps: rises of 1.1-1.6, one after another
  2. Hang and shimmy: along a lip over a drop
  3. Drop to hang: step off and catch the lip below
  4. Jump to hang: jump a gap to a higher lip
  5. Ledge to ledge: hang, then jump to the next lip
  6. Broken wall: climb a fallen wall by hangs and mantles

Grip, flow and fall

  1. Pipe or chain line: hang and move along it over a gap
  2. Chain fall: down hanging chains, one to the next
  3. Crumble run: the floor falls in behind you
  4. Drop and roll: a big drop, then a roll

side routes, never required

  1. Stride wall: a Wall Stride climb to a find
  2. Swing out: a rope shortcut, if you have the rope
  3. Leap gap: a Gauntlet move to a hidden find

Every kind moves its own way

Each of the 17 rolled kinds leans toward two or three families and has one signature piece it uses at most once a run, built in its own stone. So the Silvered Halls play like a wall-running gallery, and the Lower Combs play like a chase through burrows.

Fights while you move

Holds

Two to four light monsters from the kind's own roster wait on a landing and wake as you come near. They stay on their landing and never lock the way, and the kills count toward your spoils.

Perches C+

One or two archers on ledges across a gap shoot while you cross. A side line reaches them if you want them.

The chase B+

Once a run, the brood or the swarm comes up behind you down a corridor of pieces with no dead ends.

Rooms that go up

Dens and wave rooms get mantle steps, ledges and a ring you can wall-run, so a fight can climb.

Teaching, co-op, and the rules that hold

Teach once, then quiet

Each family has a sign in the stone: dust under a fallen beam, scuffs along a wall at running height, a worn pale lip, a pipe rubbed bright. The first time a family appears for you, the gold figure shows the move once, and the Now line says it plainly, for example "Slide under it: sprint, then crouch." After that, nothing.

Co-op

Timed pieces cycle, so your partner always gets another go. Holds scale for the party. On her easier setting, a caught fall costs her less. You both get the same line.

Never stuck, nothing floats

Every line is proved with the body's own rules at 80% of each move's reach, and each move is proved needed. Each piece is tested by a mock player at its smallest and largest size. Beams sit in walls and chains hang from the roof. A piece that won't fit falls back to a simpler one, then to today's crossing.

Safe to roll back

Gates you've already opened keep their layout. One setting (gateBeats) turns it off for new gates. No score or timer goes on screen: a clean line only gives a quiet sound and a little Flow.

When a piece won't fit: a low rift in the Cinder Workings

What fails
A wall run doesn't fit under the low roof, so the proof rejects it.
What happens
A simpler piece takes its place (a crumble run). If that fails too, today's pillars do. The gate can still be finished, and the test sweep counts every fallback.

How we'll know it worked

Checked by machine, on CI

A sweep of 17 kinds × 8 seeds × 6 ranks. It checks:

  • every run asks for at least 4 of the 5 families from C (3 at E and D);
  • every leg needs a real move;
  • two seeds of a kind share at most half their order of pieces;
  • fallbacks are at most 3% of legs;
  • nothing is stuck and nothing floats;
  • generation is at most 15% slower.

Judged by eye, then by you

One CI tour per kind showing two pieces and a hold, checked against the Gates bar. Then a seed list for you: one run per kind at C, and a few at E and S. Your verdict is the real test.

Stages (all coded, then tested once)

  1. Measure today. Count the moves every run needs today, time the generation, and check whether the old Gate World floors still roll.
  2. The checker learns the moves. It proves lines with the body's rules, and a mock player runs every piece.
  3. The kit. 24 pieces dressed for every kind, plus a bench hall that lays them all in a row for tours.
  4. The composer. Pieces strung by seed, kind and rank, the six shafts, kind accents, and the fallback.
  5. Fights while you move. Holds, perches, the chase, and rooms that go up.
  6. Teaching, flow and co-op.
  7. Every other level. The Broodwarren and the designed realms' ways get pieces placed by hand (with care at Amber Reach and the Hour Crypt). The open lands get outdoor pieces, built but off until they come back. The Gauntlet stays as it was remade.
  8. Check once. The sweep, the named tests and CI tours, then a seed list for you.

About 3 to 5 days for one builder. The biggest unknown is dressing 24 pieces in 17 different stones well.
