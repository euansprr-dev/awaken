What working a block is like today

I read the code for every block a player can work, then filmed them all in the real game at your window size. Sixty-odd blocks, four tours. This is what the film shows.

  1. The hand never carries anything. The stack on your pointer is a flat screen icon. Your hands wait at the table's corners and tap a square after the click has already happened.
  2. The scene is only as good as the spot you stand on. The view is framed from your own eyes. Two blocks back, a grid square is a sliver seen almost side-on.
  3. In third person there is no scene. The plain grey screen opens and your character does nothing. Nobody else ever sees you work.
  4. The furnace view drops, but nothing crouches. No body, no bend in the move.
  5. Nine workstations hide their own hand moment. The hands play and a vanilla grey screen opens over them in the same tick. Four of ours do the same with their own screens.
  6. It is silent and it pops. No sound for laying or lifting. Items appear and vanish in one frame. A stack on a square shows no count.
  7. Quick clicks twitch. Each click sends the hand back to its rest before it goes out again.

Try the hand

Move your pointer over the table. Click a square to lay the whole stack, right click (or Shift and click) to lay one, click a laid stack to pick it up. Two planks one above the other make sticks; take them from the right border.

The demonstration runs until you move in

The rules it keeps

  • The stack is exactly on your pointer. No lag at all. What you click is what it is over.
  • One straight vanilla arm, lying back across the table toward you like an arm reaching over a table. Its far end is under your inventory, never in view. No elbow.
  • It is small on purpose: 0.8 of a square wide, so it can cover only the squares nearer to you in the same column.
  • Nothing is hidden. Whatever lies under the sleeve shows through it as a glass ghost. The result moves to the right border, vanilla's own side, where an arm never crosses it.
  • Life without lag: the arm leans against your pointer's speed and the stack tips with it.
  • Stacks read as stacks: a small pile, and a count on a glass tag. Today a square shows one item whatever is on it.
  • Quick hands: a new target bends the hand from where it is. It never goes home first. No click ever waits for it.
  • Your pointer and your clicks decide the hand. A reply from the server changes only what lies on a square, as on any screen.

One view, from wherever you stand

Today the view is framed from your eyes, so it changes with every step. The plan gives each station a view of its own. For the table it sits over the near edge on the side you came from, looking down at 54 degrees. From one block or four, the view glides there in a quarter to half a second, and the grid is the same size in the same place every time. Your body does not move.

  • Clicks are live on the first frame. The slot under the pointer is found through the view as drawn, as it is now. Your inventory strip never moves.
  • Still while you aim. The view's small breath stops while the pointer is over the station.
  • If the place is taken (a low roof, a wall): lower and nearer, then your own eyes as today, then the plain screen. Never through a block.
  • Sneak and use still opens the plain screen. Reduced motion cuts instead of gliding.

The furnace: you really crouch

Today the view drops to the mouth on a plain ease and your body is simply hidden. In the plan the view goes down the way a body does (a dip and a nod as the knees bend, then down and in), your character kneels where others can see it, and your hand takes the food in.

  • The fire answers. Fuel on the coals brings a flare, embers of its own and a whoosh. The fire stands as high as the fuel that is left.
  • Cooking shows on the food. It turns toward the cooked thing through the last third. The two thin lines stay for exactness, in the band's own style.
  • From the side or the back you get the plain screen, as today. Smoker and blast furnace are the same scene with their own mouths and sounds.

Chests: keep them fast, make the moment yours

Nothing is faster than slots on a screen for twenty-seven stacks, and you open chests more than anything else. So the glass screen stays, and everything around it changes.

  • The lid is in your hands, up and down. The hands follow the lid's real edge each frame. A barrel's hatch and a shulker's shell get their own lifts.
  • You look in: the view tips four degrees toward the open box while the screen is up.
  • The glass rises out of the box. A glow runs from the chest's mouth to the panel. Slots are clickable in their final places from the first frame.
  • Living slots on every glass container and your bag: a stack you shift-click travels to its slot in 120 ms, the stack on your pointer lifts and tips with your speed, a count rolls to its new number. No click waits.

Third person, and other players

Two different things, and the plan answers both.

  • If you play in third person, using a station brings the view over your character's shoulder down to the bench view, and takes it back when you leave. You get the same hands and the same grid as everyone. On the way in and out you see your own character bend to the work.
  • What others see. Your character bent over the table, down on one knee at the furnace, bent to a chest with a hand on its lid, and reaching to each slot as you click it. They see what lies on your table and the largest stacks in a chest whose lid is up, the way they already see inside a furnace.
  • How it stays honest. Your clicks reach the server as they always have. The server passes on which slot you touched, at most six times a second, and nothing is saved. It shows no more than standing behind you would.

How we will know it works

  • Same view from anywhere: a test frames the table from twenty standing places and seven window sizes and gets one view.
  • Nothing hidden: a test checks every slot, both main hands and seven window sizes: the arm never covers the result or the square under the pointer, and every square it lies over is ghosted.
  • Same clicks as vanilla: the existing click tests run against the new view.
  • Quick hands: nine clicks in nine ticks all land, and the hand never goes home between them.
  • Two players: a second player gets your presence, beats and contents, and loses them when you close, leave range or log out.
