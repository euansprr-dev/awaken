AWAKEN · the journey · plan for your yes or no

The Far Landing

You want survival to feel like real Minecraft, away from the city, and you want to build your home far from Carillon without the land being too dangerous for a starter. My recommendation: keep the city for the first hour, then a skiff takes you from Ilse's fire to a landing about 6 km away. The real game starts there, and danger is measured from the landing instead of from the city. Everything you need comes to you. Carillon asks you back a couple of times for the story, and once at the very end, when you've rung every bell, to thank you.

What you asked for

Two problems with one cause

"I don't necessarily want to go back to the city during the gameplay. I want it to be full on survival ... when I build my base I don't want to be too close to the city, but then if I go too far, the level is way too high for my beginning level." You, 11 Nov. Then: "some kind of fast travel ... the actual gameplay happens far from it ... you return to it from time to time" for the story, and maybe a leaderboard later.

1. The city keeps pulling you back

Small errands need you to stand next to someone in Carillon. Each trip breaks the survival feel. Your V20 world has one waiting right now: Bram is holding the handbell you earned for your first gate.

2. Distance from the city is difficulty

The world measures danger from the city. Your home must sit past the ridge (510 blocks out), and the land stays low level only to about 1,300. That leaves a ring about 790 blocks wide. Your Hearth in V20 is 1,380 blocks out, right where harder land starts mixing in.

Both come from the same place. The game treats the city as the middle of the world, for danger as well as for errands. Fix where the middle is and both problems go away.

What is true today

Where danger comes from now

When a world is made, it saves one centre point, the city's site, and measures everything from there. Regions whose middles are within 1,300 blocks are all E (levels 3 to 8). Further out the odds shift: at 2,500 blocks, 55% E, 35% D and 10% C; at 4,500, about a quarter each of E, D and C, plus some B and A. Calm valleys of E land wind between everything, so you can always walk anywhere.

The choices

Four ways to answer it

Smaller fix

Widen the calm ring

Keep today's start. Push the all-E land out to about 2,500 and send the errands to you.

Cheap and quick. But the city stays your neighbour, its spire on your horizon, and walking back is still short. It eases problem 2 and only half solves problem 1.

Recommended

The Far Landing

The city for the first hour, then the skiff to a landing about 6 km away. Danger is measured from the landing. Errands come to you. A few story visits, and the homecoming at the end.

Fixes both at the root. Most of the pieces exist already (the wagon, Homeward, the gates' crossing look, the windways).

Your first idea

Never go back until the end

The same landing, with no flights back at all.

The purest survival, but you'd lose the city's people for 100 hours, and Hale, Oren and the city bells would still need new homes. Your second message leans toward occasional returns.

Rejected

Danger from your first fire

Measure levels from wherever you light your Ember.

The danger map is made with the land, before you choose. Your fire can't change land that already exists, and on a server two players' fires would disagree.

The recommendation

The world, after

The landing

  • One per world, chosen from the seed when the world is made, so everyone lands in the same place. Friends start together, and so would a guild server.
  • Fair ground, not luck: 5,000 to 7,000 blocks from the city; forest, plains, meadow or birch; no ocean, swamp, desert or peaks; fairly flat; a river or lake within 300.
  • Its land is made quietly during the first hour, so you arrive to a finished world.
  • Out of sight of the city. The far view draws real city blocks only about 2 km out.

Levels

  • Measured from the landing exactly the way they're measured from the city today: all E to 1,300, harder by distance, calm ways everywhere.
  • The city keeps a calm ring of about 1,000 blocks for the first hour (the run, Hale's yard, the road to Ilse).
  • Between the two the land is as hard as its distance from the landing makes it. Walking back is an expedition, but the calm ways still join them, so you're never walled in.

The skiff

  • An old Precursor skiff on the ridge just past Ilse's fire, beside Aldo's milestone, so you still meet him. It flies itself on the wind.
  • The flight: take off over the road, up into the clouds while the landing loads (a cloud crossing drawn like the gates' crossing screen), then down to its mooring. About a minute, and it can be skipped after the first time.
  • Runs both ways any time. A party within the mooring boards together.
  • Home from the city is already instant with Homeward.

The landing's mooring

  • A mast and a hold. Nobody lives there.
  • The hold is the post both ways: Bram's bells, the townsfolk's letters, Oren's lessons, keepsakes you send home.
  • The Association's board (Hale's bounties) stands beside it.
  • A shared meeting point for a server.
The recommendation

Going back: for the story only

Two invitations

When a city bell is due (around C rank for the Roost and the Square, around B for the Market and the Harbour), the bell is yours wherever you are. Then a letter in the hold says Carillon is ringing it for you and asks you to come and hear it. Going is optional and nothing is lost by waiting. In the city people talk about what you've done, Hale shows his new rack, and Oren looks at your work.

The homecoming

The morning after the Hundred Peal, the skiff comes for you. This one time it comes to your Hearth. Every bell in Carillon rings, people fill the streets and roofs, and Bram, Kestrel, Oren, Hale and Hettie each thank you in plain words. Ilse walks down from her fire for it. Then the Seed still waits, because the Deep answered. After the Wake, Ilse's visit to your home stays as it's built.

How we'd know it works

Checks, risks and what it costs

Checks before it ships

  • The landing on 50 seeds: always on fair ground, 5 to 7 km out, never water or peaks.
  • The danger census round the landing and the city on three seeds: E to 1,300 round the landing, the city's calm ring, calm ways joining the two.
  • The journey test from the landing: at every stop, four open goals, each with something in the world within 200 blocks.
  • Never stuck: everything due in the city has a way to you or waits for you, and each one shows on the Tasks page.
  • The flight never loses anyone: logging out mid-flight, a party, a server stop, a full inventory.
  • CI tours of the flight, the landing and the homecoming, for your eyes.

Risks

  • Roads and towns are laid on the region grid. Centred on the landing, the grid has to still link to the city's own north road. Stage 0 checks this first.
  • The skiff and the mooring are new art. They need their own look, not something vanilla. A picture comes to you before they go in.
  • The flight's landing must be ready on arrival. Making the land during the first hour covers it, with the crossing screen as the fallback.
  • Your V20 world doesn't get it. You'd see it in a new world.

Stages, built whole and tested once at the end

  1. The landing and the levels: choosing the landing from the seed, danger measured from it, the city's calm ring, saved with the world; the census numbers.
  2. The skiff and the mooring: the flight both ways, the crossing, the party, the hold and the board.
  3. Errands come to you: Bram's bells by the wagon, Hale's rack from the Armoury, letters and parcels, the city bells rung from afar.
  4. The world round the landing: Ilse's lines, Aldo by the skiff, the stone people, the first walled town and the lost villages measured from the landing.
  5. The story visits and the homecoming.
  6. Checks: the tests above, then the tours.

Effort at the pace this project builds: about one long day for one builder. The skiff's art and the flight are the least certain parts and could add half a day.
