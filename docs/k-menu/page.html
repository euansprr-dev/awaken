1 · Diagnosis

Why it feels like a dashboard

The Status tab is one column of 22 sections from 11 modules, each with the same loud blue title and the same size of text, in a window of 246×192 units (about 17 lines). Nothing leads. The first screen is the Atlas picture, and your stats begin about three screens down.

22sections on the Status tab today, one column
2.8screens of scrolling before the stats begin (543 units for this hunter; 1.9 to 3.4 depending on the Journal)
30mouse-wheel notches to reach the stats (18 units a notch)
6.5screens to reach Powers, the most hands-on part of K
2.6:1contrast of today's labels over overcast sky, in the game's own shot (4.5:1 is the bar)

What a player meets today

Seeing your stats means scrolling past NOW, the map picture, the Journal, your status line and what's saved for later. Spending a point is permanent on the first click. Powers is near the bottom. Gates live on a radar of dots in one tab, while the good map (the Atlas on M) has no gates on it at all.

None of this is a styling problem. Every block has equal weight and they are stacked in one feed, so the eye has nowhere to land. That is what makes it read as a dashboard.

The thesis

The band is the System's page about you: who you are now, and what's next for you. It should read like a character sheet, not a feed.

Commands attention: your rank, your level and how far to the next, your six stats, and anything that waits on you. Sits close by: what just happened, and the one thing you're doing. Waits until asked: everything else, each on its own page. Hierarchy comes from scale, light and position, not from boxes and titles.

2 · Three directions

How the work could happen

They differ in structure, not paint. All three keep the band's choreography, its glass and its sounds.

A · Reorder and refine

Same panel and tabs (the Gate Map tab becomes Map, which opens the Atlas). Status stacks status, stats, Journal, then the rest. Quiet labels replace the loud titles.

For
  • The smallest change: about one builder hand-back.
  • Nothing new to learn.
Against
  • Still one feed of 21 sections; the Journal is below the first screen.
  • Powers is still 4 to 6 screens down. A tidier dashboard.

B · The band's pages

Recommended

A rail of six pages down the wrist edge, like the band's own nodes. Each page is composed for its job: Status is one screen, Powers leads with your keys, Map is the Atlas with the gates on it.

For
  • Every ask met; the main tasks take no scrolling.
  • Removes the cause of the dashboard feel.
  • Builds on StatusSections, HoloPanel and the Atlas.
Against
  • Six pages to learn (labelled rail, keys 1 to 6).
  • Each module moves its section (one line each). About four hand-backs.

C · The orrery

The same pages as panes in an arc round the wrist. The one you look at comes forward; the others turn away into the world. The map hovers over the forearm as a diorama.

For
  • The most striking moment of the three.
Against
  • Several panels at once: more frame time, more text over the world.
  • Side panes show content you can't use.
  • Two to three times B's build. B's pages are what C would present later.

B is the smallest change that removes the dashboard, because it changes the structure. It puts stats and then the Journal on the first screen, and it makes the two maps one.

2b · Euan's idea · recommended as how the map opens

The map in the world

"It kind of zooms out and gives you a top view of your character looking at the map… as if the map was like a hologram, basically, in the world. But it still needs to look really sharp in the way it does now." So the camera rises out of your eyes to a high three-quarter shot from behind, and the band projects the Atlas in front of your character. The land, the zones and the clouds are the Atlas's own picture, so they look exactly as sharp as they do on the table today.

The cursor is a ray into the scene, as the K panel and the station scenes already pick, so pins and plates are hovered where they stand.

Legible, safe, cheap

  • The shot picks the backdrop: 35–45° down puts ground, not sky, behind the map.
  • A plate and a darkened pool under the land, and the world dimmed to about two thirds; text only on the band's glass plates.
  • In a fight M opens the quick table instead. If you're hit, or a hostile comes within 8 blocks, the scene folds in 150 ms. The world never pauses.
  • No room for the camera (a forest, a room): the other shoulder, then higher, then the table.
  • Cost: the Atlas picture as today (+1.5–2.0 ms while open), plus your model in third person (an estimated +0.2–0.4 ms) and up to 8 plates (+0.1 ms). The picture is drawn from the scene camera's own viewpoint, so one card in the world reads as true 3D.
  • Built with the Atlas builder: the Atlas paints from an outside camera; the opening's shot system places the camera (no mixin); Lens keeps its light off the card; Shoulder Surfing and Camera Overhaul are held off while it runs.

Recommended: the Map page and M open this scene, and the flat table stays as the instant map and the fallback. It is the most magical moment the band can have, and because the map is the Atlas's own picture, nothing about how sharp it looks is lost.

5 · The visual system

Light on glass, sized in whole pixels

The glass, its scan lines, halo and brackets stay: they are AWAKEN's look. What changes is how the content uses them.

Type

Minecraft's own font, always on whole framebuffer pixels. Sizes are steps of font pixels tied to the panel's pixels per unit (ppu), so nothing is ever blurred or uneven:

RoleScaleMacBook (5)4K (6)Used for
Display210 px12 pxlevel, stat values, rank letter
Body15 px6 pxnames, entry titles, buttons
Small(ppu−1)/ppu4 px5 pxsecond lines, hints, distances
Micro(ppu−2)/ppu3 px4 pxspaced capitals: labels, rail, chips

Pixels are per font pixel. Never under 2 (1080p clamps micro). Words are plain: "Level 15 opens The Veil", "Press + on a stat to raise it, then confirm."

Colour has a job

Blue is the System's structure. White is you. Amber means it waits on you. Green is done. Red is danger. Rank colours appear only on rank things. Every colour also has a shape or a word (◆ new, ◇ read, ✓ done, ▸ current, ! danger, the rank letter), so colour is never alone.

Light, material, depth

  • Light comes from the wrist. A soft glow from the lower-left corner, where the beams meet the glass, so the panel reads as projected.
  • The active page leaks light from its rail node into the page.
  • The tint behind text is 0.46 (was 0.38) and rises to 0.60 when the scene behind is bright, like a lens that darkens in sun.
  • Depth only where it means something: the rank sigil sits in a faint field of its own light; the focused stat has a column of light.

Motion reports change

Nothing animates on every open. Only what changed since you last looked moves: XP gained fills its segments, new entries print in. A page change prints the new page behind the band's scan line, the gesture the panel already makes when it opens.

Access

  • Keyboard only reaches everything; focus is always visible (corner ticks and a lit rim).
  • Band size setting: Small, Normal, Large. It steps pixels per unit, so every size moves by whole pixels.
  • Contrast as measured above; the faint ink never carries meaning.
  • Reduced motion uses the existing setting (see the motion table).
  • Narration: each focused thing gives the narrator one sentence ("Agility 22. Moves faster, and builds Flow sooner. 3 points to spend.").

Controller

No controller mod is in the pack today. The keyboard focus model is the contract one would map onto: LB and RB change page, the d-pad or left stick moves focus, A acts or places a point, B undoes then goes back then closes, X is the context row's action, Y confirms, the right stick scrolls or turns the map.

Where it touches the game

  • holo: the page screen, the rail, page changes (one layer transform and a scissor line, so text isn't rebuilt each frame), the 288-unit panel, the map growth, the adaptive tint, Band size, sounds.
  • progression: StatusSections gains pages and a context-row provider (the old tabs stay as an alias for one release); the Status sheet; the spend request and its server check.
  • One-line moves in powers, body, veil, living, signal, hearth and descent.
  • atlas, gates, above: the rail on the table, legend tabs, gates and scanner readings as Atlas marks; the Gate Map and the glance removed.
  • opening, tips: Bram's line "Status, Truths, the Codex, the gate map" and the band tip get plain words for the new pages.
  • Not touched: the progression builder's top-right task card (the Journal page reads the same task) and the discovery builder's Journal filing.

Cost and the checks before release

  • Cheaper Status: three composed blocks instead of measuring up to 22 sections every frame; the glance's paint (0.23 ms) and texture go.
  • Map: what the Atlas costs (+1.5 to 2.0 ms while open); about +0.3 ms during the 300 ms growth.
  • Budget kept: the holo K menu within 0.25 ms of the flat one; no allocation per frame.
  • Tests: layouts fit at 3 to 7 pixels a unit; Status never scrolls; every section sits on exactly one page; the spend request can't spend what you don't have.
  • Four checks: a new player finds "what next" with no scrolling; an expert spends 5 points in 6 inputs; contrast measured on a CI tour over sky, grass and night; keyboard-only and reduced-motion passes.
  • Release gates: CI green, the CI tour reviewed, the Mac perf tour within budget, your yes in game.
