The Living Wilds

Everything you do outside the gates leads to something, and you see it. Your band reads the creatures you fight, XP streams into it, the land is a patchwork of named regions, each with its own danger, people out in the wilds need your rope and your wings, herds and packs live in their valleys, and six crafts level as you survive. Gates stay the big jumps. Survival stays the core.

PHASE B

Everything pays, and you see it

  • Plates. The creature under your crosshair (24 blocks; 32 and 40 at PER 20 and 40) and everything fighting you, at most four at once. Ten segments of light; a hit breaks some off and they drain in amber. Ten levels below you the chip greys; five above it turns ember with a skull glyph. The rim tints warm for hostile, cyan for neutral.
  • Damage as glyphs. Numbers written in strokes of light, coloured by Force; crits 1.6× larger in gold with a fractured-glass flare; quick hits merge.
  • The XP stream. Motes lift off what you killed and curve into your band with "+142 XP" riding along; the band's nodes light and a thin arc fills. Herbs, ores, catches, quest steps and discoveries stream the same way, smaller.
  • Fresh and worn. Grinding one thing already pays less after a while. Now you can see it: the motes thin, the number shrinks, and once a toast says "Worn · hunting pays less for a while." Variety is always fresh.
  • Rare drops stand in a light pillar of their rarity colour, like a gate's, until picked up.

The level-up

The band flares and the world dims a touch. Two rings of light spread across the ground from your feet, lifting square motes, and a faint column rises. A tall pane of Holo glass unfolds from the band with your new level and points typed in light, over a rising glass chord in the System's own voice. During a fight it waits: the band flares, a toast lands, and the full moment plays when the fight is over. Reassessments keep their own scene.

Everything is Awaken's own: our particles, our sounds from the audio pipeline, the Holo's glass. Nothing vanilla, nothing that reads as an add-on.

Regions: the world stops following you

You said distance shouldn't be the only thing, because rings make the world linear and take away what Minecraft is: exploring for hours in any direction. So the overworld is a patchwork of named regions, each with its own level. Distance from Carillon only tilts the odds. A dangerous valley can sit a couple of kilometres from the wall, and a calm one can wait five kilometres out.

  • Regions are 600–1,400 blocks across. Their borders lean toward rivers, ridges and biome edges. Each gets a seeded name from its land, like Rimewold, Ashfen, the Gloam Reach or Willowmere.
  • The deeper in, the worse it gets. Each region spans about six levels from its border to its heart: Ashfen is 30 at the edge and 36 in the middle.
  • The Commons is home: levels 1–8 round Carillon (world spawn in classic worlds), the first hour. Its neighbours never go above 20.
  • The land tilts it. Harsh land (pale garden, badlands, high peaks, dark forest) often moves a region one band up; gentle land (plains, meadow, flower forest, cherry grove, mushroom fields) one band down. The deep dark adds 10 under anything.
  • Every direction is fair. Bands are dealt from a seeded shuffle per 3×3 block of regions, so wherever you wander there's somewhere at your level within a region or two.

PHASE C

Wayfarers and quests

  • Found, never assigned. A Wayfarer scene is placed near you in a region of the right level, with a sign you can see from far off: a distress flare, a caravan's smoke, a lantern on a ledge. No "!" over heads, no waypoint spam.
  • Solved with what you can do: the rope, the Leaper, wings and flight, powers, the Veil, dreams, PER tracking, mounts, Runner moves. A step that needs a tier you lack says so and waits.
  • Rewards that change how you play: a relic per chain, recipes, titles, Power Core Shards, Atlas reveals, renown with Carillon's factions.
  • The Journal is a new K-menu tab with only the chains you took, as short journal pages. The tracked step takes the Horizon line.
  • Bounties at Quartermaster Hale's board by the High Gate: cull, hunt an elite, bring family materials, clear a camp you've seen.
  • World events you stumble on: a herd migration at a ford, a blight surge, the Wild Hunt at night, caravan days on the road.

They tell the world's story beside Carillon's: the Choir and its bells, the Runners and Kestrel, the Association, the bell metal thread at C, the Precursors' three paths. Bright, funny and sincere, then suddenly brutal. Vael stays out: his beats belong to rank milestones.

PHASE D

Creatures live somewhere

  • Herds graze between pastures on trails you can see: stags, Mossbacks, rams, Dune Rays, Honey Bears.
  • Packs have dens. By day they sleep there; at night they stalk the herds and you find what they left. Kill a pack and the valley's herd grows.
  • Flyers roost on cliffs and leave at dawn or dusk.
  • Hostiles come from places: blight nests left by broken gates, riftborn camps, hollow dens at cave mouths, ruins whose machines wake. Clear one and it stays clear for days; an ignored nest grows a tier every two play-days and at tier 4 raids, warned a play-day ahead.
  • Vanilla's night stays dangerous: zombies and skeletons still rise in the dark, at the region's level.

Cheap by design

  • A ledger, not entities, the pattern Carillon's citizens use (measured at p95 0.015–0.04 ms): each 512-block region gets at most 6 population records and 2 camps, made the first time a player enters it, about 40 bytes each.
  • Movement is a pure function of the route seed, the time of day and the play-day, so nothing simulates while nobody is watching.
  • Bodies only near players: within 96 blocks a record becomes creatures (herd up to 12, pack 5, roost 6, camp 10); past 128 they go back into the count. At most 24 per player.
  • Catch-up instead of ticking: herds regrow and pack hunts settle in one step when you come back.
  • Budget: ≤ 0.3 ms per tick, sliced; only visited regions are saved.
PHASE E

Crafts

Six crafts, each to level 50, with perks at 5, 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50 that change how you play survival. They pay craft XP through the same fresh-and-worn budgets, so a farm left running levels off.

  • Hunting: tracks glow on the Holo for 30 s after you sight a creature; later the plate marks a weak spot.
  • Foraging: ripe herbs glint within 16 blocks. Herbs aren't new blocks: a plant is ripe by its position and the day.
  • Mining: the band pulses as you face stone with a pick, and ore within 6 blocks shows through it as projected glass.
  • Fishing: calm water bites sooner; rare catches glint on the surface.
  • Smithing: cheaper tempers, repairs at a campfire, masterwork chances.
  • Cooking: herb meals with buffs; a campfire feast shared with your party.

The line we don't cross

Survival stays the core

  • Hunger, building, crafting and dangerous nights are unchanged. Vanilla animals farm as ever.
  • Regions change how strong things are, not what lives where.
  • Quests are found in the world. Nothing is listed before you find it, and the Journal only keeps what you chose to take.
  • Pure survival (a world option) turns off plates, numbers, the XP stream, quests and bounties. Regions and crafts stay.

Risks and performance

  • Frame: plates, glyphs, motes and pillars within 0.3 ms a frame, gate fights included.
  • Server: region lookups are free; one sync per spawn; two tiny event payloads; the ledger under 0.3 ms a tick.
  • A hard region next door: an ember caption, the band's warning, skulls on plates and the Atlas all say so first. It's a place to come back to, not a trap.
  • MMO creep: found scenes, no markers, a short Journal, Pure survival.
  • Old worlds: regions apply to new spawns; beasts re-level when their lair wakes them.

Order

One builder at a time. B the combat layer, regions and XP → C Wayfarers, quests and bounties (the eight chains for levels 1–20, then the twelve) → D living creatures and camps → E crafts. Opus at extra-high effort for the look and the quest writing; Sonnet for data, spawn tables, tests and docs. Each phase goes to the orchestrator with its CI run.
