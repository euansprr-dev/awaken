Made by Hand

A new way to build in AWAKEN, where you do everything yourself and every hit feels as good as a fight. Rules first: nothing builds, grows or picks itself. No menus full of parts.

In one minute

  • The Mallet is the one tool. Hit the part of a block you want to knock away. Hold C and try other looks on it. One click sets it on every touching block of that kind.
  • Hundreds of new looks. Tap any block anywhere and the Mallet learns it, even in Carillon and inside gates, whose blocks you can't normally get.
  • Four moves that level up: Lift, Raise, Hang and the Mallet itself. They work anywhere, including for getting around. They use no power slot.
  • Hang ropes, chains, lanterns and cloth between any two points, and climb them.
  • Furniture from one recipe, and a piece from every boss. Kin move into homes you make for them.
  • A garden full of plants Minecraft doesn't have, grown exactly the way you grow wheat. You mark which ones you want.
  • Riding Along lets someone who can't fight come into any gate and be useful, without being able to die.

Out, as you said: the Dressing Brush, houses that grow, Rooms That Aren't There, the Walking Hearth, crop chores, and the Home Bell.

What I understood

You've made the fighting and the gates feel better than anything in Minecraft, and the other half of the game hasn't had that attention. You want to make a home that's yours, in a cinematic way, with far more options than vanilla. You want it simple, unlike architecture mods. And you want someone who isn't good at fighting to have just as good a time.

The rule I took from your last two messages: the fun is in doing it yourself. Anything that does it for you makes it worse. So every idea here makes your own actions feel better or gives you more to choose from.

Strong result

Ten minutes after making a Mallet you've tried three looks on a wall, knocked an arch into a doorway, strung a canopy over it and climbed the rope. You think "I made that".

Weak result

It all works but feels like operating a tool. Blocks pop into place, the dust looks vanilla, menus appear.

Unacceptable

The game builds or harvests for you. A wheel of forty options. Lost or doubled blocks. A finished house that drops frames. Griefing on a server. Skipping the mining.

Three ways to do it

Small

Just a Mallet and the freed kit. Cheap and well worth doing, but it's vanilla with nicer parts, which you've told me isn't enough.

Recommended

One tool, four moves. Everything goes through the Mallet in your hand. Real blocks wherever one exists. Builds on what ships already. The small version is its first three stages.

Too far

A free-form building engine. It stops feeling like Minecraft, brings back the complexity you dislike, and breaks room reading, movement and Far Sight.

Try it · mockup in your browser, using the game's real textures

The Mallet

Click the wall. Top of a block: step, bevel, round. Bottom: undercut, arch. Middle: slab. Hover a look below to try it on. Click a look to set it.

How it works in the game

While the Mallet is in your hand, your power icons swap to building moves. Scroll off it and your powers are back. That's the answer to your slots question: it uses no slot at all.

Right clickKnock. Hit the part you want gone. Hit again for the next shape. Hit edges, corners, faces. There is no list. Hold CLooks. A small ring appears. Moving over one shows it on the block, only on your screen. Click to set it, or let go and nothing changes. Tap CSame look again. The look you set stays on the Mallet. Sneak and tap to do the whole touching run. Middle clickCopy the look of anything you can see. RUndo. Up to 32 steps back.

Your "one stone into another" idea is exactly that ring. Ordinary stone, wood and clay looks are free, instantly, and become the real block. Before you commit, you can see the whole run change on your screen, so a wall and the hill behind it can't surprise you.

Custom blocks work the same as vanilla ones. The Mallet reads a block's own textures, so any full block in the game works, and any you add later. A test will fail the build if a new block hasn't been sorted.

Learning looks. Tap any block in the world with the Mallet and it learns that look, without changing the block. Walk Carillon tapping the walls you like. Tap the tiles in a gate. Learned looks go on as a skin: the block stays what it was underneath, so nobody can use this to make rare materials and nothing in the economy changes. The Journal keeps a grid of what you've found, as a record.

At higher levels: change a single face of a block, lean a block by a few degrees, or sink it partway in.

The four moves, level by level

1
Mallet
Hang V
Lift Z
Raise X

First numbers, tuned by playing. You level up by building things, and most of all when the Hearth reads a room you made as Fine or Grand. One quiet line tells you, the way the Ascent does.

Lift

Hold Z and sweep your view across blocks. They light up. Let go and they tear out and hang in front of you as one piece, with dust and a crack of stone. Scroll to turn it. Right click slams it down. Press Z to throw it. You can shift a finished wall two blocks left, or rip a slab off a hill. Out in the world it works just as well.

Raise

Aim at the ground and hold X. Stone climbs out of it for as long as you hold. Sweep sideways and you get a wall. Do it under your own feet and it throws you into the air, which is your movement use. Bridge a gap, get onto a roof, block a charge.

Balance

Holding a Mallet means your attack powers are off, so using these in a fight is a real trade. Lift and Raise spend energy. Thrown blocks hit softly, and never as hard as a power of your rank. Levels keep you from being overpowered early.

Try it · click two pegs, or four for cloth

Hang

Pick a kind, then click pegs. Press Climb it to see someone go along your last line.

In the game you press V at one spot and V at another. Scroll picks the kind. The ends get a small peg that can go on any solid face.

  • Climbing: jump into a line to grab it. Go along it hand over hand. A sloped line slides you down, and sneaking brakes. A steep line climbs like a ladder. Jump to let go and fly off with your speed.
  • Cloth has 3 to 6 corners, moves in the wind, gives real shade, keeps the rain off and bounces you softly if you land on it.
  • Lanterns give real light. Your ropes stay where you hang them, so a cave or a gorge can get your own way across.
  • Later: rope bridges you can walk, hammocks, baskets hanging off a line.

Furniture

No forty recipes. One recipe makes Stock. Put a Stock block down, hold C, pick what it should be, and knock it into shape.

  • Carillon's whole kit comes free: benches, market tables, shelves, casks, awnings, braziers. Tap one in the city to learn it.
  • About 40 new home pieces: chairs, tables, cabinets, four-posters, lamps, chandeliers, rugs, curtains, stoves, tubs.
  • Tap a part to change that part. Hit the back of a chair and it becomes a different back. Hit the legs and they change. The ring changes the wood and the cloth. Every chair ends up one you designed.
  • You can actually sit down, and the Hearth reads all of it.

Kin move in

A Kin Door goes wherever you want: a tiny door in a wall, a burrow in a bank, a perch under the eaves. When a kin comes home, it picks your door. You see a little light go on and hear it settling in. There's kin-sized furniture too: tiny beds, bowls, ladders. And they use yours: naps on the rug, sitting on your chairs, curled up by the fire. Old saves keep the homes they have.

Your call · I've missed on farms twice, so mark each one

The Garden

You grow these exactly like wheat: plant, wait, pick. No chores and no new rules. The fun is that they're things nobody has seen in Minecraft, with shapes it doesn't have, and that everything you grow does something silly or useful when you eat it. Seeds come from exploring, gates and kin. One quiet rule: the more different things grow near each other, the better everything grows. No score and no list. Sketches below; Stage 6 starts with the six you like best.

Also from Stock: raised beds, planters, hanging baskets on your lines, scarecrows you can dress in your own armour. Burrowbuns and Mossbacks keep working vanilla crops and leave all of these alone.

Riding Along

Both of you switch it on at the gate door or in the K menu, and off the same way, never mid-fight.

  • The rider keeps every power and weapon. Their hits count for 40%, so they help without carrying.
  • They can't go down. A killing blow folds them into your shadow for 4 seconds, then they spring back out at half health.
  • The gate and the Lord don't get harder for having them along. Monsters don't pick them first, so they can't be used as a free shield.
  • They get their own loot and boss pieces, so their half of the home fills up too. Half XP, and no rank credit: Reassessments have to be done for real.
  • One rider per run. A thin thread of light shows the bond. Works anywhere within 48 blocks of each other, so the Wilds as well.

And everything else on this page is partner-friendly on purpose. Nothing in building or the garden needs fighting.

Engineering detail

The full working: audit, root causes, research with sources, contracts, saved data, failure cases, tests. Same text as docs/made-by-hand/PLAN.md.

1. Understanding and success contract

The job. Spend hours making a home that is entirely yours and looks like nothing else in Minecraft, with the same moment-to-moment joy the fighting now has. A partner who doesn't try hard at games has just as good a time.

The pain (verified, section 2). Building is vanilla clicking. The game's best-looking pieces can't be had. One look can take a chain of crafting steps. Furniture means stairs as seats and a post with a carpet as a table. Nothing can be strung between two points. Farms have nothing new to grow. A partner who can't fight loses their Satchel when they die and makes the gate harder for you by coming along.

Euan's rulings, in order given.

  • Nothing builds or grows on its own. "Automating it makes it less fun." The Dressing Brush and the self-growing house are out.
  • No architecture-mod complexity. Simple, fun, a whole new way to build, not vanilla with helpers.
  • Mallet: yes, must work on custom blocks, must change one stone look to another without a ten-step process, with an easy way to set that look and change many blocks of that kind at once.
  • Moves: yes, not limited to home, usable outside for movement, ripping out a hill is fine, levelling up so you aren't overpowered at once.
  • Hang: yes, canopies and ropes, and you can climb them.
  • Bosses give furniture: yes. Kin move in: yes.
  • Riding Along: yes, preferred over the Home Bell. Can be switched on and off, keeps attacking and powers, effectively can't die, must stay balanced.
  • Rooms That Aren't There and the Walking Hearth: no.
  • Farms: every idea so far missed. Hands-on chores add complexity and automatic growth takes the fun away. He wants "variation and new custom things" and more options, with prettier farms kept but never a task list.

What strong, weak and unacceptable look like.

  • Strong: within ten minutes of making a Mallet someone has tried three looks on a wall, set one, knocked an arch into a doorway, strung a canopy over it and climbed the rope. Their reaction is "I made that".
  • Weak: it all works but feels like operating a tool. Blocks pop, particles look vanilla, menus appear.
  • Unacceptable: the game builds or harvests for you. A wheel of forty options. Lost or duplicated blocks. Frame drops from a finished house. Grief on a shared server. Skipping the mining game.

6. Alternatives and recommendation

Targeted repair. A Mallet item and released kit pieces, nothing else. Cheap and worthwhile, and it's Stages 0 to 2 of the recommended plan. It doesn't give the new way of building he asked for.

Recommended: one set of hands. The Mallet is the one tool and the door to every building move. Real blocks wherever a real block exists, a shaped block only where none does. Reuses protection, ledger, flying blocks, model wrapping, kit art.

More ambitious: a free-form building engine. Off-grid meshes and physics for everything. Rejected: it stops feeling like Minecraft, adds exactly the complexity he dislikes, and breaks room reading, movement and Far Sight.

Sub-choices:

  • Shapes: a registered block per material and shape would be thousands of blocks and could never cover future or other mods' blocks. A single shaped block that reads its look from data covers everything.
  • Lines: blocks can't go at angles; an entity per line is heavy and fragile. Pegs (real blocks) own the lines and one client list draws them.
  • Where the moves live: normal power slots force a trade with attacks, a separate key adds a mode to remember. Holding the Mallet needs no slot and no key, and you can see what mode you're in. Decision 1.

What would change this: if Stage 0 shows knocking by aim is fiddly, shapes fall back to a plain cycle. If shaped blocks cost too much when meshing, they get limited to detail work and big walls stay real blocks.

7. Behaviour contracts

7.1 The Mallet

Crafted early (two sticks and cobble) and a Hearth step on the K menu. While it's in your main hand the HUD's power icons swap and Z X C V R mean Lift, Raise, Looks, Hang, Undo. Change hotbar slot and your powers are back.

Knock (right click). Trigger: a block in reach. Input: the block, the face and where on the face you hit. Aim picks the part: middle of a face takes the slab cut, an edge takes the step, then bevel, then round, then back to whole; a corner works the same way; under an edge gives the cove and arch. Output: the real vanilla or kit shape if one exists, otherwise a shaped block. Refused with one line if the block has a block entity, isn't a full cube, or the permission check fails.

Looks (hold C). A ring of looks for that kind of block opens round the crosshair. Moving over a look shows it on the block on your screen only. Left click sets it on that block. Right click sets it on every touching block of the same kind, up to the level's cap, rippling outward, and the preview shows exactly which ones first. Letting go changes nothing. Sneak while clicking changes one face (level 4). The look you set stays on the Mallet: a quick tap of C puts it on what you're aiming at, sneak and tap does the whole touching run. Pick block (middle click) copies a look from anything in the world.

Undo (R). The last 32 Mallet and Lift actions, per player, kept in memory. An undo that would overwrite something changed since is skipped with one line.

Invariants: never changes a block the permission check refuses; preview is client-only; server re-reads the target and refuses if it changed; a knock never loses the block's identity.

7.2 Looks and the Look Book

  • Ordinary looks (all vanilla stone, wood, clay, concrete and colour families, from a generated table): free from the start. Swaps are real blocks.
  • Learned looks (kit and custom blocks): tap any block with the Mallet, anywhere including Carillon and gates, to learn its look. The tap changes nothing there.
  • Skins: a learned look goes on as a skin. The block keeps what it was underneath: it breaks and drops as its base, sounds like its look. So nobody can make rare materials, and recipes and the economy don't move.
  • Ore, lode hearts, Deepglass, metal blocks, gate function blocks and the like are never offered.
  • Every new block must be classified. A looks_every_block_classified test fails the build for a new full block with no decision, as journey_ledger does for items.
  • K menu, Journal, Looks: a grid of what you've found. A record, never orders. Members of a Hearth share theirs.

7.3 Stock and furniture

One recipe: Stock, from planks and sticks. Place a Stock block, hold C, pick a piece from what you know, knock it into shape. A starter set is known from the start; everything else is learned by tapping it in the world (Carillon's bench, a gate's lamp). Tapping a part of a piece changes that part (legs, top, back, cushion), and the ring changes its materials, in any wood or colour you know. About 40 new home pieces plus the freed Carillon kit. Breaking a piece returns Stock. Empty hand on a seat sits down. The room reading counts them through tags.

Set Anything Down (Decision). Sneak and right click with any item on a surface and it sits exactly there, scroll to turn. At most 4 in a block space, 64 in a chunk.

First Light (Decision). Light the first lamp in a room that has just reached Fine or better and the camera glides through it once, 5 seconds, any key skips, once per room, setting to turn off.

7.4 Hang (V)

Press V at one spot, press V at another, a line strings between them. Scroll picks rope, chain, lanterns, bunting or vines while a line is pending. Keep pressing V at more corners to make cloth (3 to 6 corners); sneak and V closes it. A real peg block goes on each end, on any solid face. Rope comes from the bag at one per 4 blocks; cloth at one per corner.

Lines hang with a real sag and move with the wind. Lanterns give real light. Cloth casts real shade through invisible sky-blocking cells at its height, so it keeps the rain off too.

Climbing. Jump or sprint into a line to grab it. Forward and back to go along it hand over hand. A slope slides you down, sneak brakes. A steep line climbs like a ladder. Jump lets go with your momentum. Cloth gives a soft bounce when you land on it. New Ascent state LINE (ordinal at the end), predicted, the server checking the line exists. The arrival system counts it as LINE, so wards still hold.

Break a peg or its wall and the line drops as an item.

7.5 Lift (Z)

Hold Z and sweep the view across blocks. They light up, up to the level's count. Let go and they tear out and hang in front of you as one piece. Scroll turns it. Right click slams it down. Z throws it, a small hit, and it lands as real blocks. Can't take block entities except shaped blocks, liquids, unbreakable blocks, sealed ward blocks, ore (Decision), gate or city blocks, or anything Constructs.mayChange refuses.

7.6 Raise (X)

Aim at natural ground or stone and hold X: a column climbs, as far as your level. Sweep sideways to make a wall. Anything on top goes up with it, so raising under your own feet throws you up. Costs energy, no blocks (Decision). Sand comes up as sandstone. Refused on built things.

7.7 Levels

Shaping level 1 to 10, the same curve as Ascent mastery (level = floor(sqrt(points / 25)), level 10 at 2,500 points). Points come from new looks, new lines, moved blocks, and most of all from rooms the Hearth reads: Snug 10, Fine 25, Grand 60. Repeats on the same block give nothing. A level gained is one quiet line above the hotbar, the same as Ascent.

7.8 Boss pieces

One signature piece per boss (41), a guaranteed first-kill drop beside the trophy, cut from that boss's real body in kit/parts.py. One small life each: the Hive Regent's crown chandelier hums and wax motes drift, the Gearwright's table has turning gears. Pilot three, Euan sees them, then roll out.

7.9 Kin move in

A Kin Door block you place anywhere: tiny door, burrow mouth, perch, knocked into a design with the Mallet. A kin that's coming home picks a door before it scans. Existing saves keep their nooks. Kin-sized furniture in the Stock ring. Kin use yours: nap on rugs, sit on seats, curl by fires. Count stays set by the Hearth's tier. New act sit, first pass on existing clips.

7.10 The Garden

Same as vanilla to grow: plant it, it grows, you pick it. No chores, no new rules, no minigames. About 24 new plants with shapes Minecraft doesn't have, from the world, gates and kin (page gallery, Euan marks each). Everything you grow does something fun when you eat it. One quiet rule: the more different things grow near each other, the faster everything grows and the more it gives, up to 1.6 times. No score, no list; a Journal page records what you've grown. Beds, planters, hanging baskets on Hang lines, scarecrows you can dress, all from Stock. Burrowbuns and Mossbacks leave new plants alone (tag).

7.11 Riding Along

Switched on at a gate door or the K menu by both players; off the same way, never mid-fight. One rider per run. Rider keeps powers and weapons, hits count for 40% (Decision). A killing blow folds them into your shadow for 4 seconds, then they spring out at half health near you. Nothing targets them first. They don't count in the party size for scaling. They get their own loot and boss pieces, half XP, and no rank credit: Reassessments can't be done riding. Works anywhere within 48 blocks of the other player (Decision). A thread of light shows the bond.

10. Ownership and privacy

Server decides everything. One permission check (Constructs.mayChange) for every move, and Hearth membership decides who can change a home. City protection, wards and gates keep their rules. Look Books are per player and only the pair's Hearth members read each other's. No external services or personal data involved. AI and retrieval: not applicable.

11. Performance and cost
  • Static shaped blocks live in the chunk mesh, so they cost nothing per frame beyond their triangles. A bevel or round has more quads than a cube. Not measured. Target: remeshing a section full of them takes under 1.5 times a section of plain blocks.
  • Block entities aren't ticking and carry a small update tag. Stage 0 measures chunk load with 5,000 in a build.
  • Lines draw through one custom geometry pass within 96 blocks. Target: 200 lines and 16 cloths in view under 0.5 ms a frame on his Mac under Cinematic. Not measured.
  • Held pieces use block displays, capped by level at 128. If that's heavy, merge into one mesh, only if measured.
  • Perf tours stay on the Mac under slot.sh. Euan's 4K numbers (Cinematic 40-55 fps) make this a real budget.
  • Art and sound follow the visual bar: no vanilla particles for any of this. A thud, dust, chips and each material's own sound.
  • Cost: no paid services. Agent hours in section 13.

14. What I challenged
  • Is this still too much? The core is one tool and one ring. Everything else is optional in order.
  • Does it move work rather than remove it? It removes crafting chains and rebuilds and adds only choosing, which is the fun.
  • Can less do most of it? Stages 0 to 2 alone give a large gain, and they come first.
  • What would prove me wrong? Stage 0, in his hands.
