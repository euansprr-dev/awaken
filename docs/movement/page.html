Rope and Wings

The rope works only where the world is tall, and even there it hangs on too long and stops at every landing. The wings are one flat sheet. Powers that move you do nothing in the air. This plan fixes those three things and adds six moves worth earning.

Approved, 10 Nov. “Yeah, this looks fantastic. Like, make this one-to-one. The only thing is for the flying animation for the wings, it needs to be better … it clips a bit and it just doesn't look professional. But everything else is amazing. Go ahead and build all of this one-to-one.” The flying beat has been remade: press Flying in the wing model below.
“analyze the entire movement system in the game. So more so like the later movement, like the rope, the wings, the things like that. And I want to massively optimize the enjoyability of that and the satisfaction that comes from it. The rope, like this kind of swinging, needs to be massively optimized. Right now it's just not good, in my opinion. It's not where it needs to be. Like, I want it to be a bit more guided, and it should feel like you're basically playing like a kind of, in a sense, a Spider-Man game nearly. And then the wings need to be massively improved in terms of visual quality. That's the biggest thing. It's like right now they look a bit rushed. I want them to truly feel cinematic and awesome when you have them, like truly, truly awesome. And then obviously it should be thought out how they tie it. They link the powers and everything, so you can make sure you can use powers in the air. And any kind of extra movement that comes to, from these, that makes it like them better and more satisfying to have, and like something that's truly insane, awesome to unlock, would be awesome. Like, really push these to the maximum satisfaction level.”
Euan, 10 Nov 2026
Built

All of it is in the game, and nobody has played it yet

Everything below was built on 10 Nov, on the branch claude/movement-plan-e38dfa, and waits for your hands. These pictures are from the game itself, taken by a tour on GitHub. The wings match the model on this page box for box; their beat is the remade one.

The first tour caught two faults: with only the rope key held the rope never threw again after a landing, and the Sovereign's six wings drew as garbage on the test machine. Both are fixed. The second tour held only forward and the rope, never jump, and the first three pictures are from it.

  • Try 1
    The rope in any wood. Sprint, jump and hold the rope. It should throw you up and over the treetops one after another, and keep going when your feet touch down.
    Measured with a bot that holds only forward and the rope, never jump: an oak wood at 152 blocks in 15 seconds and a lane of giant trees at 261, where running covers 75. No hard hit on a rope. One tree alone is taken 87 times in 100 from a sprint jump. In the game itself the lane went faster than that, and a real forest slower: 104 blocks in the first 15 seconds.
  • Try 2
    The wings. Type /awaken wings show hover to hold your wings open and walk round them in third person. Also open, glide, fly, dive, land, folded, and a rung after it: skyborn or sovereign. /awaken wings show off gives them back.
    In first person the wings' place is two numbers I set without seeing them through your eyes. /awaken wings set fp_ahead 0.8 and fp_up -0.12 move them while the game runs.
  • Try 3
    A power in the air. Dash or leap while gliding: the push should carry. Hold a power's key or the use key in flight: the flight keeps its line while you look around.
    Checked by tests, not yet by anyone's hands.
  • Try 4
    The new moves. Land slowly and level on your wings (the flare). On the ground, look up, hold sneak a moment and jump (the takeoff, once you can fly). On the rope, attack a creature that is out of reach (the rope strike, at rope mastery 5).
    The Tasks page shows the next move and how far its mastery has come.
  • Hear
    The sounds. Every movement sound is now layers of Minecraft's own recordings. Nobody has heard them in the game yet.
    Old sounds: set movementScores = false in awaken-feel-client.toml. The same file turns the new wings and the guided rope off.

One thing for you to decide. The sling needs a treetop ahead of you and above your hand, as the plan says. On the tallest tree around there is nothing above, so you cross its crown, walk off the edge, fall and sling again from the ground. In the forest tour that left three or four seconds between throws, twice in fifteen seconds. I can let the held rope also take the next crown ahead when it is level with you or a little lower, so you skip from crown to crown and stay in the canopy. It isn't in the plan, so it isn't built. Say yes and I'll build it.

What differs from the plan below: the sling aims higher and throws harder than first written (the first numbers failed at a single tree); a pull up a wall is limited by a check of its path, not by 16 blocks; the two rope-paying-out loops keep their old sound. One old fault went with it: a double jump, a dash or opening your wings from a run used to drop your speed across the ground to nothing, and now keeps it.

What I found

The rope does nothing in most of the world

I read all of the movement code, ran the game on GitHub's machines to photograph it, and ran the rope's own bench on trees of real size, played the way you play: holding the key. I can't feel a swing, so I measured it.

  • Rope 1
    An ordinary wood gives the rope nothing. A swing needs something 10 blocks over the ground. An oak's trunk is 4 to 6 blocks, a birch's 5 to 7, a spruce's 5 to 8, with a block or two of leaves on top.
    Measured: in an oak wood and a spruce wood of real size the rope fired 0 times in 40 runs. The body covered 103 blocks in 15 seconds, the same as on open plains.
  • Rope 2
    The rope's own test never saw a real wood. The forest it was tested in on 28 Oct has trees 13 to 21 blocks tall.
    Read in SwingBench.
  • Rope 3
    Held, the rope hangs on too long. It lets go 95 degrees past the bottom, when you are moving up and no longer forward. Then you hang in the air until the next rope.
    Measured: a player who only holds the key covers 120 blocks in 15 seconds among giant trees, 166 in the city. The 28 Oct test passed at 190 and 243 because its bot let go by hand at the right moment. In the tour, after each let-go the runner moved forward slower than a walk for one to two seconds.
  • Rope 4
    A landing ends the chain. With the key still held, nothing more is thrown until you let go and press again. In the lane of giant trees the runner threw one rope and then ran for seven seconds.
    Read in code (the held rope throws again only in the air) and seen in the tour.
  • Rope 5
    It can still pull you into the thing. When a swing meets a wall the rope pulls you up it. In the lane that was a 41-block pull into a tree's crown and a hard bump.
    Seen in one of two lane runs in the tour: the bump took 1.16 blocks a tick of speed. It is what you said on 28 Oct.
  • Rope 6
    Your rope key and your jump share one thumb. The rope is on Left Option and jump is on Space, so “Space jumps off the rope” can't be pressed while you hold the rope. You can only hold.
    Read from your key settings. The plan makes holding enough.
  • Rope 7
    In first person the fist is in the middle of the view, over the place you are swinging toward.
    Seen in the pictures.
  • Rope 8
    The rope's four main sounds ring. The throw, the bite, the let-go and the snap each start with a held high tone, the kind you called synthetic on 9 Nov.
    Measured with the ding checker: 8 of the 24 movement sounds ring, and all 24 are synthesised.

The wings are one flat sheet

  • Wings 1
    One sheet, no feathers. Each wing is 14 flat strips, one piece thick, with a soft painting of feathers stretched along it. It is see-through and it is not made of boxes.
    Read in WingLayer; seen in the pictures.
  • Wings 2
    A beat doesn't push down. The flap turns the wing toward your head and back. Standing, that looks like a beat. Flying belly down, the wings scissor forward and never press on the air.
    Read in code. Press Flying in the model below with Today selected: it runs the game's own numbers.
  • Wings 3
    Every rung wears the same wing. Wings, Skyborn and Sovereign share one sheet. The Sovereign's is tinted gold.
    Read in code; seen in the pictures.
  • Wings 4
    Closed, they vanish. There is no folded wing. Opening is a single ease from nothing.
    Read in code.

Powers and the air don't meet

  • Air 1
    A power's push is lost. While you swing, glide or fly, the game sets your motion from the move's own speed every tick. A Dash, the new air versions of Haymaker and Spirit Palm, Nova's lift and the new Updraft power all ask your body to move, and it doesn't.
    Read on both sides of the code. Not yet shown by a test; that test is the first thing built.
  • Air 2
    A blink closes your wings. Any jump of more than 3 blocks that the move didn't ask for ends it.
    Read in code.
  • Air 3
    A cast's gesture is hidden in flight. The flying pose plays over it.
    Read in code (the two animation layers' order).
  • Air 4
    You fly where you look. You can't look at something beside or below you without turning toward it.
    Read in code.
  • Air 5
    On the rope your weapon is put away and nothing you do reaches an enemy.
    SPEC §24.
The proposal · the rope

The line finds the way

Hold the rope and it takes the best line there is: a swing where something stands tall, a sling where it doesn't, a pull up and over a wall in the way. In open sky it still does nothing, as you decided on 28 Oct.

Place
15 s
A side-on sketch of the two rules, on the same ground and the same clock. Blue is a swing, orange is a sling. Its distances are set to sit near the bench's; the bench's own numbers are in the table above.

The sling

  • It needs something ahead of you and above your hand: a tree's crown, a roof's edge, a hill's crest, 6 to 30 blocks on.
  • The rope bites it, pulls you toward a point just over it, and lets go before you reach it, so you fly on over the top.
  • Before it throws, it plays the whole flight forward with your body's box and takes the first one that hits nothing. The swing already does this.
  • Never the ground you stand on. Never open sky.

The swing, where it already works

  • Held, the rope lets go at about 55 degrees, while you are still moving forward. Today it hangs on to 95. It waits longer only when letting go would throw you at something.
  • Your own let-go in the window keeps its extra snap, so timing it yourself is still faster than holding.
  • Land with the key held and you hop straight into the next rope. Today a landing ends it.
  • A pull up a wall is taken only when the way to it is clear and short. Otherwise the swing lets go early and looks again.

Where it attaches

  • The marker says what a press would do: a ring for a swing, a chevron for a sling, a bracket for a pull up a wall.
  • Arriving on a top with forward held, you spring straight off it and the next rope goes. Without forward you stand on it, as now.
  • In first person the fist moves down to the frame's lower corner, out of the way of where you are going.
  • Your keys stay as they are. Holding is enough: nothing on the rope needs Space.

What stays exactly as it is

  • The giant trees, the city, the ring ladders, the Tether Spires, the vault's first line, the rappel.
  • The server checks every anchor. A refused rope still becomes free flight, never a pull back.
  • A switch turns the new rule off and gives back today's rope exactly.

The proposal · the wings

Feathers of light, in boxes

Twenty-two flight feathers hang from three bones under two rows of smaller ones. Every feather is a slab one texel thick with a stepped point. No slants, no curves, nothing Minecraft doesn't have. They are light, so they glow under the Look you play on.

Doing
Hovering
Drag to turn it. Today's wing is rebuilt from the game's own numbers and its own painting. The proposed wing is a model of the boxes and the motion for this page; in the game it is drawn by the same layer that draws wings now.

How they move

  • Opening: the folded wings lift and draw back, then spread out from the body feather by feather, then settle.
  • A beat is one smooth cycle. The wing bends along its length and the tip trails. It goes down spread, and comes back up with the wrist folded. The inner wing stays over your back, so nothing cuts through the body.
  • A dive tucks them into a W. A landing cups them forward and sheds a few square motes of light.

A look for each rung

  • Wings: one pair, about six blocks across. Today's is a little over four.
  • Skyborn: a larger pair, and a second smaller pair beneath it.
  • The Sovereign's form: six gold wings and a turning halo of boxes, for its thirty seconds.

When they are closed

  • After you land they fold onto your back, stay four seconds, and fade into a small lit mark between your shoulders.
  • The mark is worn from the day the wings are given.

Through your own eyes

  • The leading edge and the long feathers sit at the sides of the frame.
  • On a beat the tips sweep through the top corners. In a dive the folded wrists close in. Nothing is drawn across the middle of your view.

Sound

All 24 movement sounds are remade from Minecraft's own recordings, named in the game's sound list so none of Mojang's audio is in the repo. First picks: the throw from a fishing rod's cast and a crossbow, the bite from a trident in the ground, the strain from a crossbow being drawn, the wings from an elytra and a dragon's wingbeat, the wind from the elytra's own loop, the boom from the Warden's. You get a local listening page with two alternates for each before any go in.

What I could not check. How the rope feels in your hands. I measured where it fires, how fast it goes and when it lets go, and I am inferring the feel from that. The box at the bottom asks what you felt, so the build aims at the right thing.

Elsewhere: Spider-Man 2 ships with its swing assist at the top of ten settings, which its makers say was the standard in the earlier games (GamesRadar, MP1st). Its makers decided early that webs had to attach to real buildings (The Ringer). That is the same rule as yours: full help, and never a rope in open sky.
