Far Sight 3: within a pixel

Every far shape should stay within about a pixel of the real thing, so loading it in changes nothing you can see, and detail should fade with distance the way air fades real mountains, so no step is ever sharp. Carillon gets this first: the whole city drawn from its real blocks, at any render distance, from the first frame of the flight.

"The biggest issue is the city itself … half the city is using Far Sight, so half the city just looks blocky … if you're moving super fast with flight and things are just rendering different than they looked, it takes a lot of the consistency away." Euan, 26 Oct

Already fixed today

Four root causes, each found by measuring, not guessing. They are on pack/far-sight with the tour frames.

Why a bigger change is still needed

Far Sight 2 draws one height per cell, 2 to 128 blocks wide, mostly predicted from the terrain noise. That causes the remaining problems:

ProblemWhy a heightfield can't avoid it
Half the city blocky while you fly in or walk the roofsPast the render distance the city is a 2-block heightfield: no facades, arches, eaves or gaps between houses
Small things look massive, then tiny once they loadA cell takes its tallest block, so a one-block spire becomes a cell-wide column
Things look different once they load, worst in fast flightLand nobody has generated is a prediction. It knows the height (within 0.6 blocks, median) but not where trees and structures will be

Can the whole city stay loaded?

Not in the game's own sense. The client keeps only the chunks inside its render distance, and the server sends only those. From Carillon's walls the far side is about 660 blocks away, render distance 42, far too heavy, and changing the render distance on the fly hitches while every chunk reloads.

But the server already generates every city chunk when the world is made. So Far Sight can draw those real blocks itself, with their real textures, at any distance. The city then looks loaded everywhere, and the render distance stays where it is.

The design

  1. Detail follows the air (Euan's idea)

    Real mountains far away are soft and low in contrast; the closer something is, the more you see. Far Sight will spend detail the same way: one curve of distance sets the haze, the contrast, and how much detail each place gets. Textures fade to colour, colour to shaded relief, fine levels to coarse ones, each gradually and inside a band where the air has already softened it, so nothing steps from sharp to blocky. The rule for choosing a level becomes "under a pixel of error after the haze", so farther land needs less detail. The real chunks get the same curve, with no clear-near, hazy-far line at the render distance. The haze still leaves the far peaks visible (clear days reach 16 km, rain closes in); its strength gets tuned by eye in the tours.

  2. City Sight: Carillon, whole, from its real blocks

    When the world is made, the server turns the generated city into compact block data (about 1,300 chunks, only faces that can be seen), saves it with the world and sends it to the client once. Far Sight draws it with the real block textures: at 500 m a block is still 2 to 3 pixels, so textured faces look like Sodium's own distant chunks. Past that, 2-block levels. A block someone changes updates its section.

  3. Spans for the rest of the world

    Each column holds a few vertical runs (bottom, top, block, colour), not one height, so arches, overhangs, bridges and walls keep their outline. Built from every chunk the server has ever generated and kept on disk, so coming back shows the truth at once.

  4. Real land ahead, and a dissolve on every swap

    Built differently from the first draft: the game picks where the next tree goes from the same random stream a tree grows from, so tree spots can't be replayed without growing the trees. Instead, while the server keeps up, it generates the real chunks in a thin band past the render distance and further ahead of where you're heading, and City Sight captures them. Wherever the far view's guess still differs, the real land dissolves in over a second instead of popping.

  5. Size-true levels, chosen by pixels

    A coarser level keeps a block only where at least half of its finer cells hold one, so thin things thin out instead of swelling. Each tile uses the coarsest level whose error, softened by the air in front of it, stays under about a pixel, and levels cross-fade. Swapping to the real chunk then moves nothing you can see.

  6. Work goes where you're heading

    The queue ranks land by where the camera will be, so in fast flight the land ahead fills first, and the workers burst while the view is cold (built today).

  7. One renderer per section, known exactly

    A Turbo hook (under the contract, locked to Sodium 0.9.3) reads Sodium's own list of sections with a mesh, in place of today's rule. Every section is drawn once: never twice, never by nobody.

  8. The opening waits for the city

    With City Sight the whole city is there before the card lifts; the card also waits for the flight path's chunks.
