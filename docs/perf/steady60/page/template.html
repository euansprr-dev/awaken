Steady Light

How to get ImmersiveVFX's look at a frame rate that doesn't move, on your M4 and the 4K Dell. Nothing here is built yet. It ends with five decisions for you.

The short version. I ran the game fullscreen on your Dell with your settings, in a copy of your world. ImmersiveVFX can't hold 60 there: flying with an open view it needs about 17 ms of GPU time a frame, and a 60 fps frame has 16.7 ms for everything. Its shadows are most of that. Lens Cinematic needs about 9 ms on the same route and almost never misses a frame.

So Lens already has the frame rate. What it lacks is the look, and most of that look turns out to be cheap: the far blue fog, the sky, the clouds and sunlight that reads. My recommendation: give Lens that look inside a fixed frame budget, in stages, and then add real shadows that are redrawn a few times a second instead of every frame.

What you asked for, made checkable

  • The look: nearly ImmersiveVFX's. Its far blue fog, its shadows, its sun rays and its atmosphere, with soft 3D clouds that still look vanilla. A little under is fine.
  • Steady 60: on your screen a frame is on time when it stays up for two refreshes (16.7 ms). The target is 99% of frames on time standing, turning and in the city, 98% flying over land you've already seen, and never three late frames in a row.
  • Nearly on par: your eye, on same-spot, same-moment pairs at five moments (morning, noon, dusk, night, the open view). The bar is that you can't call ImmersiveVFX clearly better in four of the five.
  • Kept: Vulkan, your render distance and resolution, every gate look and story scene, and the rule that nothing in ImmersiveVFX's code is read or copied (it is All Rights Reserved).

Your questions

Is Lens a mod?
It's part of AWAKEN itself, not a separate mod and not a shader pack. It is a set of shaders that run on the finished picture through Minecraft's own post-effect system, plus two small renderer hooks. It also carries about a hundred looks of ours: every gate theme, every land, the caves, the powers and the story scenes.
Why was it built that way?
On 24 October Vulkan measured 15 to 33% faster than OpenGL on your M4 and became the pack's renderer. Shader packs need Iris, and Iris only runs on OpenGL, isn't supported on Apple Silicon, and its required Sodium version crashed on our NeoForge. So no shader pack could run at all. Post effects were the one thing 26.3 gives a mod on both renderers, and the rule then was to stay out of the renderer's insides.
ImmersiveVFX is a mod, not a shader. Is there something in that?
Yes. It works inside the world's drawing instead of after it. Before the main picture it draws the world a second time from the sun to make real shadows, it changes the terrain's own shader, and it has its own sky, clouds and particles. That is why its shadows are exact, and it is also exactly why it can't hold 60 for you: that second drawing happens every frame and grows with how much land you can see. Lens works only from the finished picture and a height map of the land, so its shadows are cheap and approximate.
What I'd take from it: the look of its sky, fog and clouds; shadows drawn on the texture's pixel grid; and real shadow maps, but made our way (below), so the cost doesn't land on every frame.
Would making Lens its own mod be better?
Not for the look or the frame rate. The same code in a second jar draws the same frame at the same cost. A separate mod only matters if you want to publish Lens for other packs, which could be a nice thing later. What changes the picture is where the code hooks in, and the plan takes that step where it pays: shadow maps of our own.
Can ImmersiveVFX itself be made steady?
Only by switching its shadows off: then it runs 96 to 98 fps everywhere I flew. Its lighter shadow mode with reflections off still runs 63 fps flying. Shorter shadow distance, the fog modes and the mists made no real difference. We can't change its code. A newer version exists (1.9.2, it needs a newer Sodium beta); its notes list shadow fixes and no speed gains.
Do we still need Far Sight?
Not by default on this look. You said you prefer the fog, it costs 4.2 ms at near reach, and the fog now reaches the edge of the world. I'd keep it as a choice on the Look page for vistas.

How Steady Light works

Three rules. Each one comes from a number above.

1. Spread what moves slowly

The sun crosses a third of a degree a second, and shadows drawn on the texture's pixel grid can only move a pixel at a time. So a shadow map a quarter of a second old looks the same as a fresh one. Shadows, the sky and the clouds are refreshed over several frames; each frame only looks them up.

ImmersiveVFX redraws its shadows every frame: its 9 ms. Budget here: 0.8 ms.

2. One pass at full size

On Apple's GPU a full-size pass costs by the memory it moves. When Lens's passes were timed one by one on 26 October, its one full-size pass was most of its cost. Everything heavy runs small; one pass touches every pixel, and it writes straight into the upscaler.

Target: Cinematic from 9.4 to 8.5 ms in the clearing, new look included.

3. A governor holds the 16.7

It reads the GPU's time for the last frames and, when a view gets heavy, trades what you can't see first: how often shadows and clouds refresh, then the light's working size. It steps back up when there's room, and the Look page shows which step it is on.

Target: 14.5 ms. Awaken's own background workers also pause while frames run late.

When something goes wrong

  • A shader or pipeline that doesn't compile: that piece falls back to today's path and logs one line.
  • The shadow map over budget for a second, or its meshes behind in fast flight: the height-map shadows take over until it catches up. You'd see softer shadows, never a missing frame.
  • The governor can't find room: it says so on the Look page; it never drops below its lowest step silently.
  • Each piece has its own setting, and with all of them off the frame is today's Cinematic exactly.

Risks

  • The look is taste. The proof is a first pass and stage 1 may take two rounds with you.
  • The soft clouds are layers of see-through polygons, which cost by how much of the screen they cover. I'll measure before promising a number.
  • A governor that changes its mind too often would be worse than none. It moves one step at a time with a delay, and it is the first thing to switch off if you ever see the picture shift.
  • Flying over brand-new land has late frames on every Look, from the world being generated. Stage 2 makes Awaken's own workers give way; vanilla's generation is outside this plan.
