The Ascent: from runner to sovereign of the sky

Make moving through the world the reward in itself. You start as a fast, grounded Runner. You end as a radiant, winged overlord diving out of the clouds. Each new way of moving opens places you've been staring at for hours. Between the gates sits a world built to be crossed with style. Inside them are rooms where the room itself is the puzzle. Nothing here is built yet; this page asks for your go.

1The brief, as I understand it

The job

Make Awaken more than a gate crawler: a world where the time between gates feels like a big part of the game. Movement is the spine of the progression, all the way to godlike flight.

The pain

In creative it looks good, but "what do you do between gates?" has no strong answer. Movement feels the same as vanilla. Flight is creative flight. Gates vary by mob more than by how you play.

What you're reaching for

Moving alone is satisfying, the way Spider-Man's swinging or Titanfall's wall-running is. You unlock more and more of it, up to flying like an angel and wrecking enemies from above. The map is full of places that need your movement. Gates have mirror rooms, gravity flips, collapses, chases and gauntlets.

Your ideas, treated as leads

Grapple swinging on trees, a sonic boom, parkour gauntlets, chases, light/mirror rooms, gravity flips, collapsing floors, and movement unlocks that open new gates. The real problem you named: "you can just put blocks wherever you want".

"Magical", in terms we can check

ResultWhat you'd notice
StrongYou take the long way on purpose. Crossing 300 blocks of forest by sprint → slide → swing → release → glide feels better than any shortcut. You can do it without looking at the HUD. Each new tier makes you look at the map again ("I can get up there now"). In a gate you stop and work out how the room works. The first time you fly feels like a scene, not a toggle.
WeakNew moves that are faster numbers on the same animation. Flight that's creative flight with particles. Places that need a move but are just as easy to tower up to. Puzzles that are the rune line again with new paint.
UnacceptableRubber-banding or kicks while moving fast. Motion sickness with no way to turn it down. Fps below the 120 target on your Dell. Vanilla-looking particles that read as an add-on. Your existing worlds breaking.

Constraints I'm keeping

  • Already approved: everyone is a Runner from the first second, and ranks improve moves (Carillon, 24 Oct). Below, that's the ground floor of the ladder. Your new ask, "unlock more and more", is the floors above it.
  • NeoForge 26.3, server-authoritative, client-predicted movement. No mixins outside the approved Turbo contract. GameTests on every change; CI runs them.
  • The 120 fps target on the M4 and the Dell. Every feature must look like it belongs to Awaken, never vanilla re-skins.
  • Building stays a core Minecraft freedom. We don't ban blocks; we make movement the better way, and only Precursor places push back.
  • Existing worlds keep working. Old gates keep their content version.

Not in scope: porting a parkour mod (none run on 26.3), PvP balance, removing elytra, new dimensions beyond the trial gates below, and the city itself (Carillon is paused; its Runner work folds in here).

3Three ways to do it

A · Polish what's there

Give Flight a pose and banking, add a swing to Grapple, put wards on ruins, add exploration directives.

Gets: quick visible wins. Loses: movement stays a pile of separate powers with no ladder and no flow between moves. Gates stay static. It wouldn't change how the game feels.

B · One body, one ladder, a world built for it recommended

Merge the Body moves, the Runner kit and the movement powers into one movement system with momentum and states. Climb it through the Ascent. Give the world a traversal grammar (anchors, currents, wards, sites). Give gates a rig for living rooms and movement-keyed gates.

Reuses the Body's prediction and server checks, the prototype's proven moves, the Ritual's machinery, PAL clips, Lens and the Holo UI.

C · Engine-level conversion

A custom player rig with procedural animation and IK, a moving world (drifting islands with real collision), Turbo GPU effects.

Gets: the ceiling. Loses: weeks of risk on 26.3 internals for gains that B gets most of. I take only two pieces from it: wings as a model layer, and a Lens "motion" look.

Why B: the audit shows the problem isn't a lack of moves (there are 17 movement powers and moves) or a lack of places (about ten systems). It's that the moves don't flow, aren't earned, and don't open the world, and that rooms can't change. B attacks those causes directly and reuses the parts that already feel good. What would change my mind: if Phase 0's swing and glide prototypes feel stiff even with the Feel Director, the bottleneck is animation fidelity, and we'd pull in more of C (a custom rig) early.

4The Ascent: the ladder

One movement system, climbed in six tiers. Each tier adds a new state of the body, with its own physics, animation, sound and camera. It's earned by a deed in the world (a place, a Lord, a trial), not by a random core, and its moves level up with traversal mastery. Ranks still improve everything, as approved.

RunnerGround · from the first second

Sprint, tactical sprint, slide, roll, mantle, wall-run, wall jump, ledge hang, shimmy

Everything grounded, with coyote time and input buffering. The Body moves plus the runner prototype's wall work, rebuilt with full feel: wall-run sparks and grip sound, a ledge-hang clip, a third-person-aware hang camera. Wall Stride replaces the Wall Run power, as Carillon decided.

Earned: awakening (vanilla before it, unless you choose the city start later)Mastery: distance run without stopping, wall chains
TetherTreetops · E–D

Swing, zip, reel, slingshot, yank

A real rope. Fire at any solid block (trees, cliffs, ruins), swing like a pendulum, pump at the bottom of the arc to gain speed, and let go to launch with all that momentum. Reel in or out, zip straight up, chain anchor to anchor without touching the ground. Hit a mob to yank it, or yank yourself to a big one.

Earned: the Tether Vow, a short trial in the first vault you findMastery: swings chained, momentum kept
LeaperCliffs · D–C

Air dash, double jump, wall kick, dive slam, updraft riding

Dash and Leap become air moves inside the same state machine, so a swing release can go into an air dash and a dive slam. Updraft pylons lift you.

Earned: a Leaper gate (a gauntlet gate, §7)Mastery: airtime chains, landing quality
WingsSky platforms · C–B

Wings of light, real gliding, the sonic boom

Wings unfold from your back (a model layer with its own animation, not Slow Falling). Pitch down to dive and build speed, pull up to climb by trading that speed away, and ride thermals and wind currents. Past a speed threshold, the sonic boom fires: a visible shock ring, a Lens flash, the sound cutting out, then a crack.

Earned: reaching your first sky platform the hard way (Tether + Leaper), where its Precursor core gives you the wingsMastery: glide distance, near-misses, booms
SkybornThe clouds · A

True flight

Momentum flight, not creative flight: hover, cruise, boost, banking with camera roll, barrel rolls, and a dive with a boom. You cast while airborne, and aerial combat has its own enemies. It runs on Aether, a flight meter that recharges in sunlight and in updrafts, so flight is generous but not endless.

Earned: the Sky Leviathan's Ascension trial (the Sky Archipelago Lord, rebuilt as a flight fight)Mastery: time aloft, aerial kills
SovereignAbove · endgame

God form: the angel overlord

For about 30 seconds: radiant wings, an aura, slowed time around you, a Judgement Fall from altitude that shatters the ground, and hover-casting with every power amplified. The camera pulls back and Lens turns gold. Enemies flee. It echoes the Seated One: you become what killed you.

Earned: the story's last arc (the Hollow Court and the Seed)Use: an ultimate on a long cooldown, charged by mastery
Flow, the glue. Chain moves without stopping and a quiet Flow meter fills: a little more speed, softer landings, a faint Lens shimmer, and style XP. Stop or bonk and it fades. Flow is what makes "taking the long way" pay, and it's the skill players get to show off.

5The feel: why games make moving satisfying

These are patterns from games known for movement (Insomniac's Spider-Man swinging, Titanfall 2's wall-running and slide-hops, Breath of the Wild's paraglider and stamina, Mirror's Edge's first-person momentum, Celeste's input forgiveness). They're design references from shipped games, not measured claims, and Phase 0 tests each one here.

PrincipleWhat it means in Awaken
Momentum is kept and rewardedSpeed carries from slide to swing to release to glide. Good timing adds speed (a pump at the swing's bottom, a slide-hop). Nothing resets you to zero unless you crash.
Anticipation, action, follow-throughEvery move has a wind-up (crouch before a leap, an arm drawn back before a tether shot), the action, and a recovery (a roll, a wing fold). PAL clips with bends, generated from specs like the body clips.
Speed you can feel, not just seeFOV widens with speed. Wind rises in pitch and volume. Light streaks and contrails form, the camera trembles a little at the top speeds, and a Lens motion look adds faint radial haze at the edges. Each is tuned so you feel speed without nausea.
Generous inputCoyote time, input buffers, magnetism to anchors and ledges (the tether snaps to the best anchor near your aim), auto-mantle at the end of a swing.
Soft failureChaining never hurts. A missed swing drops you into a glide or a roll, not fall damage. Punish crashing into walls, not trying.
Sound carries the weightSynthesised sounds for each state: the rope's creak and snap, cloth and wind, the wings' whoomph, the boom's crack, landings by surface.
Skill showsFlow, near-miss flashes, records on routes, and a ghost of your best line. Other players see your rope, wings and contrails.
Comfort is a setting, not a compromiseCamera roll 0–7.5° (5° default), FOV effects on the existing slider, reduced motion, and no screen shake for those who don't want it.

The Feel Director

One client system owns camera roll, FOV, shake, speed lines, wind and Lens motion for every movement state, with a shared budget, so the Body camera, FxScreen, CombatFeel and Camera Overhaul stop stacking. It's predicted: effects start on your input, not when the server answers. Other players get the same visuals through the Body's existing "seen" sync.

"You can just place blocks": the answer

We don't ban building. Three things together make movement the better way:

  1. Precursor wards (lore: the Signal rejects foreign matter). Only around Precursor sites (ruins, spires, sky platforms, hero-tree caches), blocks you place crumble after 3 seconds and drop back to you. Rank-door walls become unbreakable inside the ward. Your Hearth and the open world are untouched.
  2. Distance, speed and time. Sky decks sit 150+ blocks up, routes are timed, and chases fade. A tower would take minutes; the movement route takes 30 seconds.
  3. The reward is for the route. A cache's best roll, the route's record and its Flow XP pay only when you arrive by movement. The server tracks how you got there (the last 20 s of movement states). A tower still gets you there, just without the prize.

The world asks you out

  • The Horizon line: a second HUD line, just for exploration. It shows the nearest site you can reach with your current tier and, once in a while, a tease of one you can't yet ("needs Wings"). Gates and requests no longer hide it.
  • Real rewards: movement cores, mastery shards, and sky-only loot that ground vaults can't drop. The ten counters that feed nothing now feed Truths, Titles and new exploration directives. Biome discoveries, promised in plan-v2 but never built, finally land.
  • Pressure stays: ignored gates still break and spread blight near your Hearth, so the world keeps setting part of your agenda.

7Gate rooms: the room is the puzzle

The Gate Rig is a small layer under the painter, built from the Ritual's proven pieces. It gives a room a staged timeline of saved-and-restored block swaps, with smooth block-display animation on top. Triggers fire from plates, positions, a light beam reaching a block, runes or an encounter. Everything follows from the gate plan, so a gate generates the same every time and survives a relog. New rooms ship as gate content version 4; old gates keep version 3.

  • Lords change their arenas: phase 2 cracks the floor into a collapse run; phase 3 flips gravity or floods the room. They use the same Rig.
  • Movement-keyed gates: once you have a tier, the Director starts rolling gates built around it. Sky gates are islands in the void, Tether gates are vertical shafts, and Gauntlet gates are speed tests. Their card says what they need. You can see them before you can finish them, which is the "I'll come back with wings" feeling.
  • Ascension trials: each tier is earned in a hand-built trial gate staged like the Ritual (the Tether Vow, the Leaper's Gauntlet, the Wingborn, the Leviathan's Sky). Each is a cinematic first time, not a toast.
  • Gate rules fixed: every painted block is protected, not just the palette, and placing inside a gate is limited to your own constructs.

9Before and after, same moments

The "after" columns describe planned, unbuilt behaviour.

1 · Getting to a gate 300 blocks away (an ordinary evening)

Today

Sprint across the forest for about 70 seconds, jump over roots, maybe tactical sprint. Arrive. It's travel time.

With the Ascent

Slide down the slope into a swing off the first big oak, pump, release over the river into an air dash, then a second swing and a roll landing. Flow fills, the wind rises, and Lens shimmers at the edges. You arrive in 25 seconds, grinning, and the gate pillar has been in view the whole way.

2 · The sky platform (the case that disappoints today)

Today

The Now line says "The Sky Platform · Bring wings." at level 20. There's no way up for another 17 levels, unless you tower 200 blocks. You arrive to the same chests as a ground vault.

With the Ascent

The Horizon line says "Sky platform · 640 m · reachable". Anchor chains hang down from the keel. You swing up chain to chain, catch an updraft pylon and air-dash onto the deck. Blocks you placed on the way crumble in the ward. On the deck, the core opens: the Wingborn trial, and you walk out with wings. The next platform is a glide away on a windway.

3 · A Mirror Palace gate (a difficult room)

Today

Static mirror walls as decoration, a rune line, mobs. You win by fighting.

With the Ascent

A beam enters from a crystal. You rotate three mirrors to route it while Mirror Knights rush you. The light burns them, so you route it through the fight. A mirror with a crack leads your spirit into a hidden room. In phase 2 the Lord shatters the floor into a collapse run.

4 · Lag and a tower (things going wrong)

Today

No airborne movement to rubber-band. Towers reach everything.

With the Ascent

At 250 ms ping your swing keeps its line, and the server's small correction is blended in over a few ticks. A friend who towers to the platform watches the blocks crumble at the ward edge. They still get there by tower outside it, but the cache's best roll and the route record only pay for arriving by movement.

10Testing and delivery

How we know it's better

  • Baseline first: XP-by-source and time-by-activity telemetry (none exists today), plus a recorded tour of today's flight, grapple and the 300-block crossing, so "after" has something to beat.
  • Physics GameTests (on CI):
    • the rope keeps its length;
    • a swing's energy matches the pendulum within 5%;
    • the glide's sink rate and dive speed;
    • Aether drain;
    • the server refuses fake anchors;
    • 60 s of airborne states without a kick;
    • wards crumble placed blocks and leave the Hearth alone;
    • every room archetype is solvable across 1,000 seeds.
  • Feel tours (on the Mac): frame sequences of each move from first and third person, turned into short clips you can watch (tours make stills; clips need an ffmpeg step, which I'll verify). Plus the 300-block crossing timed before and after.
  • Your hands: feel can't be judged from screenshots. I'd ask for three 10-minute sessions in a sandbox world: swing, glide and flight. See decision 5.
  • Performance gates: the Dell at render distance 16 holds 120 fps p50 with wings and trails on, and p99.5 doesn't drop more than 10% from today's 89.
