Carillon

A walled city at the centre of every new world. You begin as a Runner, one of the couriers who carry Carillon's messages over the rooftops, so you are already fit. The first thing you do is cross the city to a gate that just tore open in the market. Then the Ritual, the Hunter Association, the infirmary, and a city that keeps living whether you are there or not. Survival stays the core: once you leave the infirmary you can walk out the gate and build a base.

Approved on 24 October 2026, with every recommendation. Euan also confirmed the movement change in the same message: everyone is a Runner from the first second, ranks improve moves, and Wall Run becomes Wall Stride. Stage 0 (six spikes) is running. The spec lives in SPEC §16 on carillon/main, and status in docs/carillon/STATUS.md. This page gets the measured numbers at gate G0.

What you asked for, and how we'll know it worked

Your asks, in short:

  • A walled city (Attack on Titan, Solo Leveling) that is extremely detailed and very alive. NPCs do real jobs: carrying goods, a burglary you can chase. Recognition, shops, and the open world all around it.
  • Survival stays core and optional at the same time. The game should feel like a mix of survival, MMORPG and adventure. You can still go out and build a base.
  • A cinematic entry. The camera comes from above the city, down into your own first- or third-person view, and switching between the two is easy.
  • A first goal: reach the portal that opened in the market square. Like the Spider-Man (2018) opening, it takes a few minutes and is less guided. You already have mantle, tactical sprint, slide, a wall-run that sinks as you go, wall-to-wall jumps, and Assassin's Creed edge hangs and edge jumps.
  • Arrival. Alone, a cinematic cuts in and you're sucked in. With friends, it waits until everyone arrives. Then the Ritual. The Hunter Association registers you and you wake in the infirmary.
  • A map. Diorama, or our own map on the Holo wristband with clouds over places you haven't been.
  • Planned with sub-agents per workstream and an orchestrator that keeps them from drifting, with real testing and a polished finish.

Not in scope

  • A server for hundreds of players. "MMO" here means an MMO's shape sized for friends: 1 to 8 players, tested at 4.
  • NPCs that talk through a language model. The spec bans it: everything is local and seeded.
  • Daily logins, real-time timers or anything you can miss forever. The city runs on play-days.
  • A quest list. Never more than three Directives, and no list of undiscovered places.
  • Replacing survival. The city is a hub you can ignore.

  • An audit of our own code: movement, the Ritual, Hearth NPCs, maps, the Holo UI, structures, the prologue, multiplayer, and the perf data. Plus the 26.3 sources for the camera, servers and spawning.

How the run teaches without prompts

  • Space teaches. Each new move first shows up where it's the only sensible way forward, then it's mixed with the others.
  • The Runner's eye only appears if you hesitate: stand still at an edge for 6 s and the next hold pulses once in bronze. It shows up less the better you get, and you can turn it off.
  • A key glyph appears near the surface only after a second hesitation, and only once per move.
  • Falling only costs time. In the opening no fall hurts you below one heart. A big fall plays a roll landing, and the street route is always close.
  • It keeps your first time. Your first-run time becomes a ghost on the Rooftop Circuit later.

Why a Runner

Carillon's streets are slow and crowded, so the city's messages travel over the roofs. Couriers of the Runners' Guild train from childhood. That's why you can do all of this before any System touches you. Your powers later are the impossible versions of what your body already knows. And the Roost on the wall is the first home you can respawn in.

It also explains the city's look: the rooftops are a road network of their own, with plank bridges, rope lines and worn bronze handholds.

This changes three approved rules, so they need your yes.
  • SPEC §4: "never-awakened players move like vanilla" would become "everyone moves like a Runner". A server option, runnersKit=false, brings back vanilla movement for packs that want it.
  • The Awakened Body rule "grounded moves only; anything airborne stays a power" would become "the body knows the move, and powers make it impossible".
  • Rank unlocks would become rank upgrades. The Wall Run power becomes Wall Stride so it can't duplicate the base move. New moves go at the end of the Move enum, so existing saves keep their bits.

Carillon itself

The lore makes the city a machine. Carillon was built on top of the Choir. Its bells are the Choir's resonators, and they keep the gates calm, which is why the city rings every hour. And it's why the bell rang out of time the morning the Choir found you. The walls exist because gates break and things come through.

How it's built V pattern

  • The plan is authored data: the wall line, districts, streets, canals, plots, landmark sites. It's the same in every world, and its hash is checked by a GameTest.
  • The painter works per chunk, like the gate rooms (GatePainter and its Sink). Each chunk builds only the pieces that touch it, so cost stays flat however big the city gets.
  • The grammar fills each plot: a foundation, 2 to 6 storeys of timber frame, plaster or brick, jettied upper floors (good for hangs), steep roofs with dormers and chimneys, then the details. A seed varies the details and never the shape.
  • Landmarks are hand-made: the Choir spire, the Association, the Roost, the four gatehouses, the Bell Bridge, the crane towers, the Works mills. About 12 of them.
  • Graphs: the same pass writes the street graph and the roof graph. Edges carry the move they need, plus door and stair links.
  • Pregeneration: the whole city and a margin (about 1,250 chunks) generate behind the opening card. After that, generator changes can't break a built city, and the graphs are saved with the world.

The detail kit

  • The Carillon kit: about 35 blocks of our own. Roof tiles in slate, clay and copper; timber frames; plaster; shutters; flower boxes; laundry lines; market awnings; crates and sacks; street lamps (lit and unlit); bells; district banners; drainpipes you can climb; signboards; bronze ledge trim.
  • The climbable language: bronze trim, dark beams and drainpipes mean "hold me". Plain plaster and glass don't. It's consistent across the whole city.
  • Interiors: every building on the run's routes, every shop, taverns, the Association, about 60 at first. Other doors are locked but lit from inside at night.
  • Sound: hourly bells, a crowd murmur that follows how many citizens are in a district at that hour, market calls, anvils, gulls, canal water, wind on the wall. Sound Physics (sound profile) adds the alley echo.
  • Light: Lens adds Carillon dawn (gold haze) and Carillon night (warm lamp bloom). Distant Horizons draws the whole city from the sky shot and from the hills.
  • Life you can see far off: chimney smoke, lit windows, boats moving, pigeon flocks that lift as you run past (client-only, so the server pays nothing).
The terrain needs one change from Awakened Lands. A city basin at world spawn: about 600 blocks across, gently flattened, with no lairs or ruins, and blending out to about 700 blocks. Spawn moves into the city through LevelEvent.CreateSpawnPosition (V exists in NeoForge). The spawn vault ring (240 to 360 blocks) and the Well (400 to 700) move outside the wall. The lands branch owns the blend, and the city owns everything inside it.

A city that lives

Every citizen is a person on record, with a home, a job, a day, friends and a memory of you. Only the ones near you have bodies. That's how 240 people can live in Carillon without costing what 240 villagers would.

The ledger · everyone

240 records (about 48 KB saved). Each knows where it should be at every hour and what it's carrying, and moves along the street graph abstractly. The whole ledger updates in slices: 12 citizens a tick, each one every second. Budget: 0.5 ms a tick.

Bodies · near you

Within about 56 blocks, a citizen gets a body where the ledger says they are. That's at most 40 near each player and 96 in the world. Bodies walk the street graph, not long A* routes, and brains tick less often the further away they are (Pufferfish's DAB rule). At most 4 pathfind at once. Budget: 1.5 ms a tick.

Signs of life · far off

Beyond the bodies the city shows its people through the world: lit windows, chimney smoke, shutters and lamps changing state, the district's crowd murmur, boats on the canals, pigeons. No entities, so almost no cost.

Twelve jobs you can watch

Lamplighter: lights every lamp at dusk, and the light really changes Bellringer: rings the hours and alarms Porter: crates from Quay to market, carried on the back Stallkeeper: shutters open at 7, close at dusk Baker: smoke at dawn, bread on the shelves Smith: sparks and anvils Watch: patrols and answers events Crier: reads the news, including news about you Boatman: punts passengers along the canals Washer: laundry lines fill and empty Mason: rebuilds damage block by block Runner: other couriers on rooftop routes you can race

The Lamplighter matters most. A monster can only spawn in a block light of 0, so the lamps are what actually keep the city safe at night. If a lamp is broken and left unlit, that corner of the street can spawn mobs.

sleepwork on the waymeals, tavern, home event window
A play-day for five citizens, on vanilla's day clock (0 is sunrise; villagers work from 2000 to 9000). Schedules are seeded per citizen, so the city changes from street to street while staying on the same rhythm.

Four ways to play that stack, and you choose how many. Each one stands on its own, and nothing in a later layer is required by an earlier one.

Survivor · today, unchanged

Hunger, building, mining, vanilla mobs, nights that are dangerous outside the walls. Your Hearth. You can leave the city after the infirmary and never come back.

The Atlas

A diorama of everywhere you've been, floating over your wrist, with clouds over everything you haven't seen. It takes Diorama's idea (an angled voxel map with sun shadows and clouds over the unknown) and makes it Awaken's own: it lives on the Holo, it knows the city, and the clouds only clear where you've actually been.

A mock-up drawn here in the browser, about 1,000 blocks across. After the run, only the streak you ran and the Association are clear. Climbing a bell tower clears a whole district, Assassin's Creed style but with bells. Markers show only what you've discovered: your Hearth, a gate you've seen, the bell towers you've rung.

How it works

  • What you've explored is kept on the server. One bit per 16×16 cell. A 10,000 × 10,000 block world costs about 49 KB per player. Parties can share it, and it survives a reinstall.
  • How it looks is cached on your client: height and top colour per column, sampled from chunks as they load, in versioned tiles under the game folder, capped at 64 MB. If the cache is lost, explored land shows faded until you pass by again.
  • The clouds clear where you've been (about 64 blocks around your path), not across your whole render distance. High places clear more: a district's bell tower clears the district, and the Choir spire clears the whole city.
  • Markers come from the existing MapMarks, landmark memory, gates, lairs, your Hearth, party members and contracts. Only what you know, ever.
  • Drawing: the table view is a screen using 26.3's picture-in-picture renderer (V PictureInPictureRenderer and its NeoForge event), with depth, orbit, zoom and tilt. The wrist glance is a small cached render on the Holo. Meshes rebuild in slices across frames.

Diorama, Xaero's and ours

  • Diorama (MIT, on 26.3 since today) is the look you asked for. It can join the pack now as a trial, on its own, the day you want it.
  • For the Atlas, its MIT licence means we may learn from or reuse its ray-traced baker with its notice kept. Stage 0 decides whether reusing it or writing our own is less work, since it's 11 days old and changing fast.
  • Once the Atlas ships, Xaero's leaves the pack plan's trial list, so there's one map and not two. Diorama can stay as an optional client mod for people who want it.

How we prove it

Your rule for this build is "no playtest gates; verify with tests and autopilot shots". So every claim in the success contract has an automatic check, and each gate ends with shots and numbers you can look at whenever you like.

GameTests (headless server)

  • Moves: each new move, each refusal, and spoofed starts the server must reject
  • City: the plan hash; a chunk built alone matches the same chunk built in context; protection
  • Graphs: every door reachable; three routes per act; the street route within 30 s of every roof node
  • Citizens: a simulated play-day in which every citizen reaches every schedule target; events resolve; no thief gets stuck
  • Renown and economy: gossip spreads, nothing duplicates, contracts never name undiscovered places, at most 3 notices
  • Opening: the state machine, a relog at every beat, the multiplayer wait and the straggler pull (with fake players)

Autopilot tours (bench worktree, one at a time)

  • Golden runs: a route bot replays recorded inputs for each route through the existing TryDriver. It checks the arrival time window and the hitch count. A generator change that breaks a route fails here.
  • A day in Carillon: dawn, noon, dusk and night shot pairs in every district
  • Studio tour: each landmark, framed the same way at every gate so the shots compare
  • Perf city: the market at noon, rooftops, canal, wall top and the sky shot, on the perf and horizon profiles
  • Atlas: opening, panning and revealing

Simulations

  • The pacing simulator (tools/sim) runs renown and the economy for 100 play-days and checks prices hold within ±30%
  • An event budget per play-day, so the city is busy but not chaotic

Multiplayer

  • Logic through GameTest fake players
  • A latency harness: a headless server with 100 or 150 ms added, and one autopilot client
  • One real two-client smoke at gate G4, only if you say yes, because it opens game windows

How drift is stopped

  • Contracts first. SPEC §16 and the core.api hooks land before any builder starts. A builder that needs a new decision asks the orchestrator, and the decision goes into SPEC in the same commit (the CLAUDE.md rule).
  • Briefs as files. docs/carillon/briefs/W1.md and so on: goal, owned files, hooks, tests, budgets, non-goals, and absolute paths (builders lose their working directory).
  • A merge order: Runner, City, Director, Citizens, Renown, Atlas, Guild. Each merge runs the full GameTest suite (flakes re-run once), then the tours.
  • A drift check at every milestone: the reviewer compares the branch with its brief. Each deviation is either written into SPEC or reverted.
  • Status: docs/carillon/STATUS.md, plus this page updated at each gate with shots, numbers and test counts.

House rules for every agent

  • One writer per package. Shared JSON goes through src/main/merge/<module>/.
  • Builds only through tools/gradle.sh, and at most 3 at once.
  • Autopilot tours one at a time, on the bench worktree only.
  • Never launch the game client without asking you. Never build in Awaken-play while it runs. Never edit a script that's running.
  • Verify every API in .ref before using it. A compile error costs more than a grep.
  • Small commits ending with the co-author line.
  • Opus for design-heavy streams (City, Runner, Director, Citizens, Atlas), a cheaper model for mechanical QA and data work.

Decisions (approved)

All thirteen recommendations were approved on 24 October 2026.

1 · The Runner's Kit from the first secondChanges three approved rules: vanilla movement before awakening, grounded-only base moves, and ranks that unlock moves. Recommended: yes. Everyone is a Runner, ranks deepen the moves, Wall Run becomes Wall Stride, and a server option brings back vanilla movement.
2 · Where Carillon sitsAt spawn in new worlds, as a far-off city, or both. Recommended: at spawn in new worlds, plus a Classic start world option (wild spawn, no opening). Existing worlds don't change.
3 · One Carillon or a new city per worldSame layout everywhere, or seeded layouts. Recommended: one designed Carillon, with seeded details. The routes stay designed and tested, and friends share a mental map.
4 · How friends startTogether at the Roost, or each at their own start point. Recommended: together. One sky shot, then everyone picks a route. Stragglers are pulled in 5 minutes after the first arrival, or after 2 minutes idle.
5 · The contracts boardThree notices at a time, or a full board. Recommended: three. It keeps "never a quest list" true.
6 · Building in the cityCivic blocks protected, or the whole city open. Recommended: protected, with your own plots free and a server option to turn it off.
7 · Can citizens die?Permanent deaths, or hurt and recovering. Recommended: hurt, never dead. They recover at the infirmary.
8 · Hearth people come from CarillonOren, Mira, Teodor and Sun live in the city first. At Trusted they can move to your Hearth. This changes how the Hearth gets its people. Recommended: yes. Your Hearth fills with people you actually know.
9 · The mapDiorama, Xaero's, or our own Atlas. Recommended: our own Atlas on the Holo. Diorama goes in the pack as an optional trial now if you want it, and Xaero's leaves when the Atlas ships.
10 · Third personVanilla's behind-the-back view, an over-the-shoulder view (needs a small camera mixin or the Shoulder Surfing mod), or both. Recommended: start with vanilla plus our View key, and decide on the shoulder view at gate G1 after trying it in the run.
11 · Wall-run camera rollHow much the view tilts on a wall. Recommended: 5° by default, with an option from 0 to 7.5°.
12 · Who orchestratesThis session, or a fresh dedicated one. Recommended: this session, since it holds all this context. STATUS.md lets a fresh session take over cleanly at any gate.
13 · Order against current workThe Ritual, body-anims, Holo and Lands branches are still open. Recommended: they merge first, and Carillon branches start from that base. Stage 0's spikes are throwaway, so they can start right away in parallel.
