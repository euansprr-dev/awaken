Awaken · Pack plan · 24 Oct 2026 · for approval

The Awakened Pack

How Awaken stops being a great mod inside vanilla Minecraft and becomes a game that feels new from the first hilltop: the horizon, the light, how your body moves, how the world sounds, and friends beside you. Built on 26.3, on your M4.

Distant Horizons is the "unlimited render distance" mod. Version 3.3.4-26.3 for NeoForge came out on 29 Sep 2026 and is a full release. I downloaded it from Modrinth and checked its SHA-512. Your game was running from the play copy, so I didn't install it. It's staged at /Volumes/MEDIA/Awaken-staging/, ready to back up your saves and copy into Awaken-play/run/mods/ once you close the game.

Your own Mac shader: yes, but not as an Iris shaderpack. Iris only runs on OpenGL, lists Apple Silicon as unsupported, and 26.4 makes Vulkan the default. The version that lasts is Lens: a light layer inside Awaken built on 26.3's own post-effect system. It compiles for both renderers, every player gets it, and it's tuned for Apple's tile-based GPU (few passes, half and quarter resolution).

Two surprises from the audit. 26.3 ships a new Vulkan renderer, but your game runs on OpenGL 4.1 translated to Metal. And Awaken has never been run next to another mod, although its plan says it was tested with Sodium.

1 · Understanding and success contract

What you need, and how we'll know it worked

You like Awaken as it is. The ask is everything around it: the frame Awaken sits in. Today that frame is vanilla. It has a 256-block fog wall, flat light, vanilla arm poses when you cast, synthesised sounds nobody has listened to, and no other mods. You want a curated whole that makes a first-time player feel they found a new game, on your MacBook and 4K monitor, and that friends can join later.

The job

Ship one coherent game, Awaken plus a few chosen mods, configs and art, that you and later friends launch and play. It's not a pile of mods to try.

What's missing today

No far view, no lighting pass, vanilla body animation, untested audio. And no way to install, pin or test other mods next to Awaken at all.

Your ideas, to test, not assume

Distant Horizons, a custom macOS shader, movement, terrain and structure mods, animations, textures, QoL, player animations for multiplayer.

Must keep (from plan v2)

120 fps on your M4, Minecraft feel and survival, vanilla mobs, single-player first, server-authoritative, every gate, dream and story system, all tests passing.

Strong

Each layer adds one thing you notice in the first ten minutes. Everything shares Awaken's art direction. The Swift look holds your 120 fps budget, Cinematic holds 60+, and Awaken's effects look the same or better with every pack piece on.

Weak

Sixty popular mods with clashing styles and a dozen config menus. 40 fps at 4K. A shaderpack that washes out Awaken's glows. Fewer Awaken creatures because a terrain mod renamed the biomes.

Unacceptable

Crashes or world damage. Awaken effects missing or broken. A client-only mod required on the server. A frame-rate regression under the SPEC budget. Old worlds getting terrain seams.

Not in this plan: changing Awaken's gameplay numbers, new dimensions, replacing Awaken's own systems with mods (combat, travel, quests), 32× or HD texture packs (they clash with Awaken's 16× art), and Fabric-only mods (no Fabric bridge exists for 26.3).

What changed in the plan because of this. I had a custom Iris shaderpack as the gated "ultimate" option. It's out. That's the one path that runs on both of Mojang's renderers, works for every player in multiplayer, and lets us control every pass for Apple's tile-based GPU.

4 · Alternatives

Three ways to go, and the recommendation

A · Curated kit

Pick about 25 available mods and a shaderpack, drop them in the play copy, play. About a day.

Loses because nothing checks that Awaken still looks and runs right. The styles won't match, and the pack can't be rebuilt or handed to a friend. It's the "weak result" above.

B · Integrated pack RECOMMENDED

A pinned, tested pack around Awaken. Awaken changes to fit it: biome tags, a far-terrain policy, its own light layer (Lens), full-body casts, adaptive music. Three look tiers, a frame-time harness, and an export friends can import.

Reuses Awaken's post-effect system, autopilot and progression. About 4–7 weeks in stages, with the first vertical slice in about a week.

C · Engine-level conversion

B plus our own shaderpack from day one, our own terrain generator, a render-scale mod with mixins, our own animation system and a dedicated server.

Loses: months of work. A custom Iris pack would target an OpenGL path that 26.4 replaces with Vulkan, on Macs Iris doesn't support. The parts worth keeping survive in B as gated options: Lens Ultra and render scale.

What would change this: if Lens's look doesn't beat plain vanilla in your blind picks at stage 2, we stop investing in light and put that time into the body and the sound. If 4K frame times miss badly even on Swift, render scale moves up to a decision at stage 2.

Lens, as a contract

Trigger: every frame on the client while in a world, unless an Iris shaderpack is active or the tier is Off.

Inputs: main colour and depth targets, the current dimension, time of day, the tier, and the per-dimension look file assets/awaken/lens/<dimension>.json.

Processing: half-res bright and haze pass, quarter-res separable blur, then one full-res composite (grade, bloom, haze, vignette, dither). Cinematic adds light shafts and dream depth of field. At most four render passes, because each pass costs extra on a tile-based GPU (see MC-311557).

Hook: prototype as a pure-asset end_of_frame effect; ship through NeoForge's FrameGraphSetupEvent so Awaken owns the order and other packs can't replace it.

Invariants: never changes gameplay or server state; never hides an Awaken gameplay effect (those draw after Lens); same look for every player in a dimension.

Budget: ≤ 1.0 ms Swift, ≤ 2.5 ms Cinematic at 4K. Over budget for 5 seconds: drop one step and say so once.

Failure: a shader that fails to compile disables Lens and logs it once; the game keeps vanilla light. It never crashes the client.

Data and lifecycle

  • Canonical: the manifest (pack/manifest.json: project, version, file, SHA-512, side, profile) and our config overrides in pack/config/, both in git.
  • Derived: installed jars, plus a lock file in each mods/ listing only what the installer put there. It never removes a jar it didn't install. Distant Horizons' LOD database inside each save can be deleted and rebuilt.
  • Updates: bump one entry, rerun the smoke and frame-time tours, commit. Rollback means reinstalling the previous manifest.
  • Worlds: back up a save before its first run with the pack. Terrain mods only in new worlds. Your Awaken and Azareth worlds stay on vanilla terrain.
  • Safety: the installer refuses to touch a game folder a client is running from, the same rule tools/play.sh follows.

6 · Concrete demonstrations

The same moments, today and proposed

The "proposed" columns describe behaviour that doesn't exist yet. Nothing in them has been measured.

An ordinary evening walk

Today

  1. You climb a hill at sunset.
  2. The game draws 16 chunks; past 256 blocks there's fog.
  3. Light is vanilla; a Precursor beam appears only once you're close.
  4. You pick a direction at random.

Proposed

  1. Same hill, in a Tectonic world.
  2. Distant Horizons draws ranges 2,000+ blocks away; Lens adds warm haze that deepens with distance.
  3. A beam stands on the far ridge. Zoom shows a ruin.
  4. You walk there. The world gave you the direction, as plan v2 intends, with no quest marker.

Less work: the decision of where to go is made for you by what you can see.

The disappointing one: casting looks vanilla

Today

  1. You cast a Lightning power.
  2. Your arms take the trident pose and the body leans.
  3. Particles and sound fire. A friend would see a trident pose with nothing in hand.

Proposed

  1. The same cast sends a payload Awaken already has a slot for.
  2. Every nearby client plays the "storm" family animation: a crouch and gather, an overhead release, a recoil.
  3. Lens blooms the bolt; Sound Physics echoes it off the hills.

Same timing and balance, so nothing to relearn.

The hard case: a Lightless gate with everything on

Today

  1. You enter an A-rank gate that rolled Lightless.
  2. It's pitch black by design; fine.
  3. If Distant Horizons were installed now, it could keep a void LOD of this cell and show phantom shapes when the cell is reused.

When something is missing

Sodium's beta breaks after an update

  1. The smoke tour fails on the manifest bump, before you ever launch.
  2. The manifest stays on the last good build. Your play copy is untouched.

A friend joins with plain Awaken

  1. The server needs only Awaken.
  2. They see vanilla light and their own render distance. Your casts show today's poses on their screen, because Awaken treats the animation library as optional and falls back cleanly.
  3. They install the .mrpack later and get the full look; nothing on the server changes.

Fixed scenes

Same seed, position, time and weather every run: overworld hilltop at sunset, dense forest, a full gate fight (legion), a Lightless gate, Lucidium, the Stillpoint cloud sea, Descent stratum 5, and a 60-second flight.

Frame time p50, p95 and p99; server tick p95; heap peak; log errors. Visual: a 12-shot contact sheet, judged by you once per stage, blind as A/B. Content: spawn census, /locate for every Awaken structure. All 121+ GameTests on every change.

The pack stays pinned to 26.3. Moving to 26.4 (Vulkan by default) is its own decision, once Distant Horizons, Sodium and the animation library have ported. Lens and every Awaken change in this plan are built so that move doesn't break them.

Estimates assume the betas hold. What could change them: Sodium or Iris breaking Awaken's custom geometry (adds days to stage 1), Distant Horizons lacking a per-dimension switch (we'd need its API), and how much of multiplayer you want before 1.0.
