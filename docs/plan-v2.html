AWAKEN · plan v2 · for approval

The Awakened World

Your playtest showed the first build fails exactly where you said it does. The overworld is untouched, the first hour gives you nothing new except a dungeon you can't survive, and the System windows cover half the screen without saying what you're meant to do. This plan keeps everything that works (gates, the Descent, dreams, the Veil, the Hearth, the story) and adds the missing layer: a world that changes the moment you awaken, powers from the start, a reason to level from 1 to 50, and a System that speaks in small, clear prompts.

1 · Understanding

What you need, and how we'll know it worked

The job

Play a long single-player Minecraft survival game where the world itself feels new from the first minutes, you always know what you could do next, and growing from level 1 to 50 feels like the best RPG packs. It should still feel peaceful and recognisably Minecraft.

The pain

  • The outside world feels exactly like vanilla.
  • Your first goal turns out to be "go find iron".
  • After the prologue, the dungeon kills you.
  • The pop-ups are huge and don't say what to do.
  • You don't know what you're supposed to do next.

What "revolutionary" has to mean in play

Your ideas, treated as ideas

  • Tons of new mobs in the world: adopted, with roles (ambient, neutral, night hunters, rare named beasts), not random hostiles.
  • Change the world itself: adopted as an "awakened layer" on top of vanilla terrain, not a terrain replacement (keeps the Minecraft feel and the M4 frame rate).
  • Smaller, clearer UI: adopted, sized like vanilla toasts.
  • Don't start with "find iron": adopted. Vanilla gear still matters, but gathering gets a purpose and powers come first.
  • Level 1 → 50: adopted as a designed "level road". Level 50 lands at B-rank in the current rank table (D at 10, C at 25, B at 45).

Must keep

  • Survival, peaceful days, Minecraft feel; vanilla mobs stay.
  • Single-player first, server-authoritative.
  • 120 fps on your M4.
  • No FOMO loops or daily-login pressure.
  • Gates, Descent, dreams, Veil, Hearth, story, 121 passing tests.

Not in this plan

  • Replacing vanilla terrain generation (a later option, see §3).
  • Multiplayer balancing.
  • New dimensions.

3 · Alternatives

Three ways to go, and the recommendation

A · Targeted repair

Starter power, fair first gate, XP retune, small System prompts, Now tracker, bug fixes. About 2–3 days.

Fixes the cliff and the confusion. Loses because the world would still feel vanilla, which was your main complaint.

B · The Awakened World RECOMMENDED

Everything in A, plus a living overworld layer: creatures native to every biome, anomalies that come to you, marked landmarks, materials and gear from the world, and a designed level road from 1 to 50.

Reuses the creature system, gates, story and progression engine. Needs real art work and careful pacing. About 3–5 weeks of building in stages, with a playable slice in about a week.

C · Total conversion

B plus new terrain generation, survival systems (temperature, thirst) and an animation library for every creature.

Loses for now: replaced terrain stops feeling like Minecraft, it multiplies art and frame-rate risk, and the best terrain mods don't support 26.3 yet (Tectonic lists 1.21.1 and 26.1). It can be revisited once B is fun.

What would change the recommendation: if the vertical slice shows that new creatures in the overworld don't change how the world feels to you, we stop scaling creatures and put that effort into anomalies and landmarks instead.

4.1 The awakening is a reveal

PROPOSAL The prologue stays (glitch, pillar, the Bell Crypt, the death, "Will you accept?"), but it ends with a payoff instead of a cliff:

  • Your eyes open. On Accept the screen pulses, and the awakened layer becomes visible: light motes in the grass, a creature watching from the trees, faint lines of energy over ore veins. In Solo Leveling terms, the gates were always there; you just couldn't see them.
  • You choose a first power from three, shown as big cards with a one-line effect and a 3-second preview (for example Haymaker, Charged Shot, Spirit Palm). Dash follows at level 2.
  • The prologue pays. Every kill and floor during the prologue is banked and paid on Accept, which should make you level 2–3 on waking.
  • The Bell Crypt becomes a fair first gate: content level 0, softer monsters, a ~190-HP Lord, no pressure clock. The message says what's true: "The gate will wait. It never breaks."

4.2 Creatures native to the overworld

PROPOSAL New creatures spawn naturally, by biome and time of day, alongside vanilla mobs. The names below are illustrative.

RoleShare of spawnsBehaviourExamples (illustrative)
Ambient~40%Never hostile. Makes biomes feel alive; some drop crafting materials or lead you somewhere.Lumen moths at dusk in forests, glass scarabs in deserts
Neutral~30%Grazes or wanders and fights back. Some can be tamed or ridden later.Mossback tortoise, frost stag, dune ray

  • Mechanism (verified to exist in 26.3): NeoForge's data-driven add_spawns biome modifiers, spawn placements, and an extensible spawn category. The new creatures get their own population cap, so they never crowd out cows, zombies or the rest of vanilla.
  • Reuse: the existing creature definitions, abilities, phases, scaling, loot and texture pipeline. Several of the 154 gate and stratum creatures fit the overworld as they are.
  • Peace: days are mostly ambient and neutral, and nothing spawns inside your Hearth radius or in lit areas.

4.3 Anomalies: the world comes to you

PROPOSAL Small events within 32–96 blocks, every 6–12 minutes of play (more often at night), at most one at a time, never inside your Hearth radius, and never while you sleep:

  • Rift tear: a 2-minute skirmish against monsters pouring out of a crack. Close it by breaking its core. Pays essence, XP and a Power Core chance.
  • Fallen star: a small crater with materials and a short-lived guardian.
  • Spirit procession: peaceful. Follow the lights to a hidden cache or a landmark.
  • Wandering gate: a one-floor mini-gate you can enter right there. You never have to walk a kilometre to fight.
  • Shimmer grove: a patch of altered rules for a few minutes (low gravity, glowing ore, a talking spirit).

4.4 The level road, 1 → 50

PROPOSAL Every level gives +3 stat points and one visible unlock from a data table, so there are no dead levels:

L6–10 · First gatesHearth Anchor · Echo binding · Gate Map compass · rank D trial
L11–20 · The VeilMeditation and projection taught in play · mounts · gear tier 2 · the Well opens up

L31–45 · DeepStrata 3–6 · named beasts in every biome · ultimate power

  • XP from the world: first sightings of a creature, biome or landmark; overworld creature kills; anomalies; gates. This replaces the flat 1.5%-per-minute cap with diminishing returns per source, so playing normally grows you without farming.
  • Pacing targets (to validate in tools/sim before tuning content): level 5 in about 45–60 min, level 10 in 3–4 h, level 25 in ~15–20 h, level 50 in ~55–60 h of the ~100-hour story. That avoids the Prominence II trap of hitting the cap by chapter one.

4.5 Gear and gathering with a purpose

  • PROPOSAL Hunter gear crafted from creature materials and essence, in tiers by rank, sits beside vanilla gear rather than replacing it. Iron still helps; it's just not a wall.
  • Mod recipes unlock in the recipe book the moment you first pick up an ingredient (vanilla's own mechanism).
  • A System request turns gathering into a goal ("Bring 8 moss hide to the Anchor"), the Vault Hunters altar pattern.

Performance budget: no more than ~16 new creatures active per player, no per-tick scans of all entities, and anomalies checked every 20 ticks. Frame rate is measured in the slice on your M4 before scaling up; the target is no measurable drop at 120 fps.

Old saves: every new saved field has a default. Your current world keeps working, and the awakened layer switches on for already-awakened players.
