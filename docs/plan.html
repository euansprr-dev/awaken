Minecraft Java mod · working title

AWAKEN

One home. Four directions. No ceiling.

You start as the weakest person in the world. A gate opens on the horizon, you walk into it, and you die. Then something called the Signal asks if you accept, and Minecraft becomes a different game: gates to clear, a pit with no bottom under your home, dreams you can wake up inside, a body you can step out of, and old machines that remember who built them.

This page is the full plan: what I think you're really after, what exists today, the options, the whole game, how it gets built, working demos, how we prove it's fun, and the decisions only you can make. Nothing has been built or installed. It stops here for your approval.

Section 1

What you're asking for

How I read it: you want Minecraft to feel like being the main character of your own Solo Leveling arc. You start weak, get chosen, go deeper, come home visibly stronger, and keep finding things nobody told you about. The pull comes from the world itself: a light on the horizon, a door that hums when you're ready, a dream that showed you a place. It never comes from a list.

The outcome contract

Usefulness
Deciding what to do next becomes effortless, and growth is felt, not just tracked.
Correctness
Rules are readable and fair. Every number the Signal shows is the real number. Every death can be explained by what you saw.
Effort removed
Wiki lookups, recipe hunting, long walks back (waypoints and lifts), inventory chores (your Echoes haul and sort), and grinding the same thing.
Responsiveness
Entering a gate takes under 2 seconds because it was built before you arrived. Your first fight comes within 60 seconds of entering, and your first level-up within 5 minutes of a new world.
Control
Automatic: stat growth, Echoes doing chores. Suggested: Directives, Truths, your Path. Your call: where points go, which gate, push or extract, which Truths you declare, which Path you take.
Reliability
A crash never loses progress. A gate that fails to build rerolls quietly before you ever see it. A dream that isn't ready becomes a normal night's sleep.
Cost
Free to play solo. No servers, no AI services, nothing recurring. The real cost is build time (Section 6).

Non-goals and things that must not break

Not doing (for now)

  • AI chat NPCs. They need a network connection and cost per conversation, they don't behave the same twice, and they risk the “AI-looking” feel.
  • Flying ships made of moving blocks. Huge engineering risk, low payoff.
  • Procedural nemesis rivals. WB holds a patent on that system, so our rival is authored.
  • PvP balance, Bedrock Edition, or working with every mod ever made.
  • Replacing vanilla mobs, biomes or progression.

Must not regress

  • Vanilla building, redstone, farming, exploring, enchanting. Our Level is separate from vanilla XP.
  • The Nether and the End stay as they are, and the mod adds to them.
  • Performance mods (Sodium and friends) keep working.
  • Your test worlds survive mod updates. Saves are versioned and backed up automatically.

Direction without a quest tree

This is the heart of your brief. There's no quest book. Direction comes from four layers that work together. Each is small on its own.

Spine

One line, always visible in the Signal: “Next rank: D. Reassessment at Level 10. You're Level 7.” It's the only goal you ever need to follow, and it never depends on finding a secret.

Pressure

Gates have timers counted in days you actually play, never real-world days. Ignore one and it breaks: monsters and a roaming Gate Lord spill out, and the land around it goes grey until you clear it. Breaks are events with better loot, not punishments. Breaks near home always warn you a day ahead.

Pull

Wants placed where you can see them: pillars of light on the horizon coloured by rank, a sealed Precursor door near spawn that hums once you're strong enough, a red pillar you can't survive yet, an empty core slot, the glow far down the Well.

Whisper

Directives from the Signal: at most three at once, picked for the moment, optional, and they expire quietly so they never pile up. Hidden Directives appear only when you do something unusual. “You haven't used Flow in three days. Clear a floor with only Flow and your fists. +1 AGI.”

The Codex is a mystery board, not a list. Rumours from spirits, visions from dreams and logs from ruins pin themselves to a board and link up as you learn more, like the rumour map in Outer Wilds. Things you've heard of but not found show as a question mark. Nothing undiscovered is ever listed.

  • One window at a time. During combat they shrink to a small toast and queue up.
  • Every window has at most one action.
  • The status screen has four tabs and no more: Status, Truths, Codex, Gate Map.

Ranks and Reassessment

E, D, C, B, A, S, then Sovereign, then Ascension tiers after the story. Hitting the level lets you take the trial: a short handcrafted challenge at the Spire in town. D: survive three waves. C: the Second Awakening, where your Path is revealed. B: a boss duel. A: a timed descent. S: fight a Sovereign's echo. Each rank-up brings a celebration in town, your name on the Spire board, a new tier of gates, a new stratum of the Descent, a Hearth upgrade, and an aura that grows with you (you can turn it off). This is the “become visibly recognised” beat from Dungeon City.

Two reward rhythms

The simulator in Section 5 turned up a real problem. With the rank pacing on target, level-ups land about every 20 minutes early on but stretch to about an hour apart by S-rank. That's the flat mid-game Vault Hunters players complain about. So levels are only the first rhythm. The second guarantees something new to use or show off at least every 20 minutes: a Power Core (pity-timed, so a dry streak can't last), a Title, a Truth coming true, a new depth record, a trophy for the Hall, a scanned machine. The Director tracks time since your last “new thing” and tilts drops and Directives toward one when it's been too long. Hypothesis to test in M2.

Gates

Gates are the main loop. They're short and varied, and they're always out there.

  • Where they come from. A pacing director keeps one to four gates open near you, 300 to 1,500 blocks away: 70% at your rank, 20% below and 10% above. You can see them from far away as light pillars coloured by rank: white E, green D, blue C, violet B, amber A, crimson S, black and gold Sovereign.
  • The gate card. Walk up to a gate and you see its rank, when it breaks and a one-line omen hinting at what's inside (“It smells of salt and machine oil”). Higher PER tells you more. Scouting from the Veil tells you everything.
  • Built before you arrive. The inside is generated in the background when the gate appears, so walking in is instant.
  • Inside. Three to six floors made of handbuilt rooms joined by a layout grammar. Each floor ends at a Rest Shrine (extract here) and a Seal (go deeper). The last floor is the Gate Lord.
  • Push or extract. Kill XP lands as you go. Floor loot rides in your Satchel and only becomes yours when you extract at a Shrine or kill the boss. Die and you lose what's in the Satchel, keep your gear, and get Fracture, a short debuff that resting at home clears. The simulator in Section 5 shows this stays a real decision.
  • The gate wakes up. There's no hard death timer inside. Stay on one floor too long and the gate notices you, sending stronger waves and eventually its Warden. That's pressure without a cliff, borrowed from Spelunky's ghost and Risk of Rain's rising difficulty. Research
  • Failure still pays. If you die, kill XP you already earned stays, and you take home Insight: the boss's tells and weak points go in your Codex. The next attempt is smarter even when it isn't stronger.
  • Bosses have phases and clear tells. They have parts you can break off for materials, the Monster Hunter way. Each one has a signature move you can sometimes take as a Power Core.

Your Path (the Second Awakening at C-rank)

The Signal reads how you've actually played: kills by power family, time in the Veil, lucidity, blocks built, Echoes raised, machines built. It offers your top three Paths, each with the evidence in your own numbers (“You struck 1,204 foes bare-handed”). A hidden fourth appears if you met a secret condition. You choose. It can be changed later through a costly ritual.

Launch six: Shadowbinder (Echo army) · Redline (speedster) · Prism (beams and flight) · Architect (build-combat) · Seer (Veil and precognition) · Warden (protector, shields others). Later six: Stormbreaker · Dreamwalker (dream powers leak into the waking world) · Engineer (Frames and drones) · and three hidden ones: Monk (unarmed, Stillpoint mastery), Hollow (the dark Path, powered by the Deep) and Wanderer (you refused the Signal).

Truths, Titles and Latents: the layer you find by accident

Truths

At your awakening and at every rank, the Signal offers Truths, short statements about who you're becoming. Declare up to three. Each shows NOT TRUE YET until your actions match it, then it becomes true and grants a permanent perk. The Signal also notices patterns and offers new ones: “You have never fled a fight. A new Truth is available.”

Titles

Earned by feats, often surprising ones. Equip one for its perk. Fell Further (survive a 200-block fall). Unwitnessed (clear a gate without taking a hit). Wolf Friend (tame ten wolves). Gatebreaker (clear a gate before its omen reveals itself).

Latents

Hidden abilities with no hint at all. Sprint for two minutes on an empty stomach and get Second Wind. Meditate on a peak above the clouds at dawn and get Clarity. Stand in a thunderstorm holding copper and get Stormcall. Sleep in the Deep and become a Deep Dreamer.

The Lucidium: dreams you can wake up inside

  • Dreaming. Sleep and there's a chance you dream. It's higher with more SPI, a better bed and a Sanctuary room. Hold sneak while you sleep for a dreamless night. Your time stays yours.
  • Built from your day. All day the mod quietly snapshots about ten memorable moments: where you nearly died, a boss arena, your house, a view you stopped to look at, the mob you killed most. The dream stitches them together, wrongly. Your house floats next to the boss room. The colours are off. Doors lead somewhere else. You literally dream of your own world.
  • Becoming lucid. Lucidity runs from 0 to 100. These are real lucid-dreaming techniques, turned into controls: Research
    • Reality check (one key: look at your hands). In a dream your hand flickers, sign text scrambles and clocks spin. Every check raises lucidity.
    • Dream signs. Recurring oddities, like a mob with no shadow. Spotting one raises lucidity.
    • Set an intention at the bed before sleeping (“I will find the door”). This is MILD.
    • Wake and go back to sleep. Set an alarm block to wake you mid-night, then sleep again for a big lucidity bonus. This is WBTB.
    • Recall. Writing in your Dream Journal raises your Recall stat, and Recall predicts lucidity.
    In a 2017 study, people who combined these went lucid in 17% of attempts within a week, and about 46% when they fell asleep within five minutes. The game is far more generous, but the verbs are the real ones.
  • Perceive and create. Rooms you aren't looking at can change. Once lucid, hold intent on a blank wall and a door, stair or bridge forms from the places you've been. At mastery you can fly and summon.
  • What it's for. Training (skills used in dreams level twice as fast, and your Path's mentor duels you). Visions (a dream shows a real place in your world with a secret, and you go find it awake). Nightmares (picked from a set of authored nightmares by what you've died to most this week; beat one and that fear is gone for good). Truth progress. Dream Residue for crafting.
  • Waking up. A dream lasts three to eight minutes, or ends when you die in it. Dying there costs nothing but leaves you a little shaken. Then a clean morning.
  • Later. Re-enter a dream from your journal. Start lucid at high SPI. Share dreams with friends who sleep in the same Sanctuary.

The Veil: stepping out of your body

  • The first time is an accident. On your first near-death in a gate, you briefly see yourself from outside. After that, meditate (sneak plus Focus, still for ten seconds) somewhere quiet or in the Stillpoint.
  • Leaving. A ripple, a low hum, and you rise. Your body stays behind, meditating, and it's vulnerable. If it gets hit you get a Warning and can snap back. That makes a safe meditation room at home matter.
  • Spirit form. Fly freely and pass through blocks, up to your cord's length (SPI: 32 blocks at first, 512+ later) and for as long as your Focus holds.
  • One readable rule: you can pass through anything, and you can only touch what glows blue. Critics found the ghost in Beyond: Two Souls arbitrary because its limits were never explained. This rule can be learned in five minutes and never breaks. Research
  • Awareness is your render distance. At low Awareness the spirit world past a few chunks is fog. It opens up as PER and SPI grow.
  • What you see. Ores and hidden rooms glowing faintly. The spirits of fallen Awakened, with their stories and hints. Ley lines leading to ruins. Every mob's rank. And gate interiors: drift into a gate's mouth as a spirit and you preview its boss and modifiers.
  • What you can do. Talk to spirits. Work ghost levers in Veil puzzles. Possess animals (C-rank), then mobs (A-rank; yes, you can possess a creeper). Enter Veil Gates, dungeons only a spirit can walk.
  • The danger. Cord-Eaters hunt spirits. If they cut your cord, you wake with Spirit Shock.
  • With friends, spirit sight never reveals other players' bases, and the server checks it, so it can't become an x-ray cheat.
The Stillpoint. Your meditation hub: a calm void above a sea of clouds at dawn, with a temple platform. Time runs ten times slower there. Spend points, declare Truths, read the Codex. One door leads Out into the Veil. If you're asleep, another leads In to the Lucidium.

The Above: the machines that remember

  • Precursor ruins: sealed vaults on the surface, sky platforms above Y 250 you can see from the ground, ocean labs, desert monoliths.
  • Salvage, scan, build. Find a broken machine, read it with the Scanner, and it goes in your Codex. Build it at home from three to five parts the build screen shows you. There's no tech tree screen and no wiki.
  • One kind of power. Essence, stored in cells. Machines draw from the Hearth's reactor within range. No pipe puzzles.

The Hearth, the town and your Echoes

  • The Anchor. Craft it from your first boss's essence. It defines your base.
  • Rooms are detected from what's in them. No menus. Bed, meditation mat and quiet make a Sanctuary (better dreams). Anvil, forge and rack make an Armory (gear upgrades). Boss trophies make a Trophy Hall (a passive bonus per trophy, and something to show off). Machines make a Workshop. Role pads make Barracks.
  • Costs that fall. Vault Hunters' sharpest complaint was base recipes priced in dungeon currency that never got cheaper. Here, Hearth upgrades mostly cost overworld materials plus a little essence, and every rank-up makes older upgrades cheaper. Home is never a tax on adventuring. Research
  • The town grows by itself as you rank up. A Blacksmith, a Scout, an Archivist, a Healer and your Rival arrive. They react to your rank and offer services and rumours. You never assign anyone to anything.
  • Echoes do your chores. Fallen elites can be bound as Echoes. They fight beside you, and at home each one stands on a role pad: Guard, Haul (sorts loot into chests), Farm, Mine, Train. The shadow army does the boring parts so you can do the fun ones.
  • Colour comes back. A gate greys the land around it. Clearing it brings the colour back. That's your HALFTONE “districts regain colour with Hope” idea, given a new home.

Daily Regimen

Each in-game morning, never each real day, the Signal offers three quick drills tuned to your Path: sprint 1,000 blocks, two minutes of Flow, a lap of the Training Grounds, a short meditation. Do them for a stat point. Skip them and nothing happens, unless you turn on Hardline.

Training Grounds

Movement time trials, combat drills and flight rings at home, with personal bests and a ghost of your best run. Your Fortnite routine, given a place to live.

  • Priority when things conflict: server safety rules, then your explicit choices (settings, declared Truths, accepted Directives), then story state, then the Director's suggestions.
  • Frozen when it should be: a gate's contents are fixed at creation (seed plus content version), so what the Veil showed you is what's inside. Path offers are frozen once shown.
  • Fresh when it should be: the Director re-reads your state every tick it runs, and Directives re-check their conditions live.
  • Late results: a dream plan for a night you don't sleep is thrown away. Gate builds are bound to their gate id, so a build finishing after a break can't reopen the gate. A content update rebuilds gates nobody has entered and leaves entered ones on their old version.

Invariants

  • A reward is granted at most once per claim id.
  • Loot moves from the Satchel and is never copied.
  • A spirit never changes a real block except blue-tagged Veil objects.
  • No cell is cleaned up while a player or Echo is inside.
  • What the Veil showed you about a gate is what's in it.
  • Never more than three Directives. Nothing runs on real-world time.
  • Every number the Signal shows is the server's number.
  • Path and Truth explanations quote only real counters.
  • Something you do to one enemy never changes another enemy's stats (the patent rule below).
  • Your world is backed up before any mod version change.

Patent-safe rules. WB's Nemesis patent (US 10,926,179, granted 2021, estimated to expire in 2036) may cover more than Nemesis. In plain terms: an event between you and one hostile NPC changes a different hostile NPC's stats, and the game shows you the change. Something like “you died, so other monsters got stronger” could fall inside it. So:
  • Something you do to one enemy never changes another enemy's stats.
  • Consequences go into world state instead: a gate's rank, grey land, timers, loot, the Director's spawn budget.
  • The Rival is authored and moves on story beats tied to your rank.
  • Nightmares are picked from authored templates using your own death counts.
  • No ranked factions that reshuffle, and no cross-player vendettas.

AI, privacy and ownership

No language model in the game

That keeps it offline, free and consistent. Two systems make judgement calls: the Director and the Path and Truth offers. Both are fixed rules with seeded randomness, and both explain themselves only from real counters. /awaken why prints the last ten decisions and what they were based on. The only “learning” is novelty memory plus which gates you enter or skip, with a variety floor so it can't trap you in a rut. You can reset it. Rules and content are versioned.

Privacy and ownership

Everything stays on your Mac. The mod makes no network calls. The playtest log is opt-in and local. In multiplayer the server enforces spirit rules (the client is never trusted), and Day Journal snapshots never capture another player's base. There are no credentials anywhere.
