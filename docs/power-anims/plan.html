What you saw, and why

Beam · Energy · hold

It comes out of the wrist

  • First person: your hand is lifted by a fixed nudge, but the beam starts at a fixed spot below it. At the default view it begins about 0.7 of the half-screen under your fist and sweeps up past its left side.
  • Third person: your body holds the charging pose, hands cupped at the hip, for the whole beam. The beam starts half a block behind your palm.
  • No recoil, no tremble, no light on your hands.

Should be: light gathers at your chest, runs down the arm, leaves from the open palm. A braced stance, the off hand steadying the wrist, a small recoil.

Shockwave · Kinetic · stomp

There is no stomp

  • Third person: the clip is Kinetic’s punch. Without the animation library it is a crouch.
  • First person: your hand dips a hand’s width. No leg, no foot.
  • The wave leaves the instant you press, before a foot could land. The sound is a gust of wind. The camera rumbles but never thumps.

Should be: the knee comes up, hangs, the foot plants and the wave leaves from the foot. The view dips, your hands drop, dust kicks up at the heel, and a heavy thud lands on the frame.

BeforeStomp: what Shockwave plays today (the Kinetic cast clip)

A coil, a hip turn and a straight fist. The feet barely move. Each frame is 50 ms. This is the shipped clip, drawn by the same rig tool the game’s clips come from.

AfterStomp: proposed (a proof made with the rig tool, not in the game)

Coil on the off leg at 100 ms, knee to hip height at 200 ms, a one-tick hang, plant at 300 ms with the weight dropping into a deep knee, rebound, settle. The wave starts at the red ring, the planted foot, about 0.2 block ahead of you. No part of the body clips through another.

BeforeBeam: the shipped Energy clip, with where the game starts the beam

Gold is the palm. The ring is where the beam starts today: a fixed offset from the eyes, 0.46 to 0.63 block from the palm. While it fires, the game plays the hip-cupped hold clip, not this one.

AfterBeam: proposed, through the core (a proof, not in the game)

Cyan is the chest core, gold the palm. Hands gather at the sternum (150 ms), the arm lays along the aim, the off hand steadies the wrist, braced stance. The beam starts at the gold dot, and the light runs from the cyan one down the arm.

One fault under all of them

  1. One pose per family, not per powerNine families share 42 clips. A stomp, a throw, a clap and a guard all fall back to “the family’s cast”: Shockwave, Brace and Thunderclap play Kinetic’s punch, Spirit Palm plays a prayer, every dash plays Velocity’s sprint.
  2. Effects start at a fixed spot, not at a body partThe game places “the hand” at a fixed offset from the eyes. Nothing reads where the drawn hand is, so beams, orbs, cables and bolts start in the air near it.
  3. First-person casts are outside the hands systemYour hands system (Hands) has fists, legs, glow and keyed events, but powers never use it. Casts just nudge the vanilla arm.
  4. The effect leaves before the body doesEffect, sound and damage start on the key press. The body’s release lands 3 to 10 ticks later.
  5. Channels skip their releaseThe beam and Prism Lance never trigger the release pose, so the charging pose is the whole cast.
  6. Three shake systems that do not talkPowers use their own shake, their own kick and Arms’ feedback only for the victim. Your own hands and view never hold on a hit.
  7. Sound is 50 vanilla remixes played onceA mace smash, a wind gust, a beacon hum. No foley, no layers, no tail, not placed at the hand.
  8. No light and no ground contactNothing uses Lens for a cast, so time-slow powers tell your screen nothing. Heavy casts start at your position, not at a planted foot.

The plan: one system

Every cast has four beats

Gather, release, follow through, recover. The release is the one frame the effect, the sound, the camera and the hit-stop all land on. That frame is written down once per power and used by both the first-person and the third-person animation.

gatherrelease: effect, sound, camera, hit-stopfollow throughrecover

Named body points, the same for you and for everyone watching

Effects start at one of these, found on the body you can see:

Palm
beams, bolts, palm prints, cables
Both palms
barriers, novas, claps
Core
charge light, Prism Lance, overload
Brow
every Mind ripple
Foot, plant
the stomp’s wave, dust, cracks
Feet
auras, rings, domes

In first person the point is the hand’s place that the animation sets on screen, so it is exact. For you in the shoulder view and for other players it is read from the posed body each frame, the way Arms already does for sword blades. If a player is too far to draw, a stored track puts the effect at their body.

First person

  • Casts join the hands system: each hand is placed on screen by the animation (so a palm can sit at the chest, then go out along the aim), and the animation’s own frames fire the effect, the sound and the camera.
  • For the stomp, the boot rises into the bottom edge of the frame, hangs and drives down, with the view dipping. Looking down shows the real leg.
  • For the beam, your off hand rises under the main wrist, and the hands glow with the charge.

Third person, the shoulder view, and other players

  • Every power gets its own clip, made from its family’s base pose with the animation rig we already use for casts. Bent elbows and knees stay.
  • Channels get a real hold and release. The beam’s hold is a braced stance that trembles more as it drains.
  • Other players see the anticipation. A stomp’s knee comes up four ticks before the wave, a tell that helps in PvP.
  • Everyone puts the effect at their own drawn view of the caster, from the same seed. Positions never cross the network, and a cast stays one small message.

The impact

  • The thump: the view dips 2.4° in two ticks and rebounds, the field of view narrows a little, your hands and view hold for the hit-stop, dust and a crack appear at the planted foot. In the shoulder view the camera drops with it, as it does on a landing.
  • Sound, in four layers on the frame: cloth and boot on the gather, a thud and a crack on the release, the family’s own tone, a tail of air or rumble. Played on each listener’s game, at the spot.
  • Light: a bloom pulse on every cast; a handful of Lens looks for ultimates, the beam hold, Flow and Foresight.

Decisions

Each has my recommended answer. “Yes to all” is a fine reply.

  1. D1

    Do the whole system for all 59, or only the beam and the stomp?

    Recommended The system, proof first: beam and stomp end to end, then your look, then the families.

    The two you named share every cause with the other 57. Fixing only them leaves the rest as they are.

  2. D2

    Where does the beam come out?

    Recommended The palm of the aimed arm, fed from the chest core. Prism Lance is the one that leaves the chest core with both arms wide.

    “The core place” is the sternum, but a beam still has to visibly leave a hand to read as thrown. Two beams, two bodies.

  3. D3

    A short real wind-up for heavy powers?

    Recommended Yes: 3 to 6 ticks (at most 250 ms) for 14 powers: Shockwave, Spire, Frost Nova, Terrain Rip, Thunderclap, Brace, Gravity Flip, Collapse, Rampart, Legion, Citadel, Drone Swarm, Void Maw, Storm Call. None for movement, toggles or quick casts. Your key press starts the pose at once.

    The foot has to land before the wave leaves. Energy and cooldown still start on the press. Arms’ heavy weapons already work this way.

  4. D4

    For every other power, should the effect wait for the body’s release frame?

    Recommended Yes, on your screen only: 1 to 4 ticks, no change to damage or balance. Hit-stop and camera move with it.

    Closes the gap where the effect leaves before the hands do.

  5. D5

    A boot in first person for the stomp?

    Recommended Yes, stylised: the boot rises into the bottom edge of the frame, hangs, and drives down. Off with reduced motion.

    A real stomp is out of frame. Without the boot it is only a camera dip and a hand drop; with it you see your foot do it.

  6. D6

    One camera director for powers?

    Recommended Yes. One class over Arms’ combat feedback, the Feel Director and the screen flash. Same settings you have.

    Today there are three shake systems that do not know each other.

  7. D7

    Authored, layered sound in place of vanilla remixes?

    Recommended Yes. Signature moments first (stomp, slam, beam loop, nova, lightning, blink), then a kit per family. Played on each client at the muzzle, on the release frame.

    Sound is half of a thump, and it is where “vanilla add-on” shows most. Fewer network packets too.

  8. D8

    Lens light for casts?

    Recommended Yes, small: a bloom pulse for every cast and about 8 looks (ultimates, Beam hold, Flow, Foresight). Lens off falls back to today’s flash.

    Light on the hands and a cue for the time-slow powers are what is missing. It touches the Lens module, so I will brief its owner.

  9. D9

    A signature per power, or one pose per family?

    Recommended A signature for all 59: a hand, a stance and one beat each, generated from the family’s base pose.

    Every power gets its own look. About 3 hours more than family-only.

  10. D10

    Keep Player Animation Library and Bendable Cuboids as the third-person path?

    Recommended Yes. Vanilla poses stay as an improved fallback.

    Already in the pack; the bent versions fix elbows and knees.

  11. D11

    Left-handed players

    Recommended Mirror everything, as now.

    Already how the pack works.

  12. D12

    Can other players see the anticipation?

    Recommended Yes, at least 3 ticks for heavy casts.

    A tell makes PvP readable and costs nothing in PvE.

  13. D13

    Comfort setting

    Recommended One “Cast animation: Full / Reduced / Off” that halves or removes the dip, the boot and the tremble.

    Fits the body’s reduced-motion setting.

  14. D14

    Build gate

    Recommended Your OK on the proof’s shots before the families start.

    The look is the risk, not the code.

What I did not check

  • I did not run the game or a tour. Scores come from reading the code. The first-person numbers assume the default field of view and a 16:9 screen.
  • Whether the pose I read from the body is ready before the effect draws in the same frame. Arms’ blade ribbon shows the method works; the worst case is a one-frame lag.
  • The boot’s fit inside the first-person frame comes from the Hands spec (“down to about 0.7 of the distance”); the first shots must confirm it.
  • D3 changes feel: a short wait on 14 powers. If you dislike it the fallback is the screen-only release (D4) for them.
  • Lens changes live in another module and need its owner.
