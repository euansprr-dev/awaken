What you asked for

In your words: push the power animations to the next level, especially the variety, the animations themselves and the sounds, and get creative about new powers and combos. You also said you don't want to scrap everything.

So this is a polish plan with a creative layer on top. Yesterday's plan, Powers in Full Voice, stays as the map of what each power does. You said yes to all 43 parts of it and I'm not reopening them. This plan changes how every power is made from here on: what your hands do, what you hear, how two casts differ, and how powers meet your weapon and your movement.

How you'll know it worked

  • Hide the HUD. From one second of your hands you can tell which power it is. The same from the sound with your eyes shut.
  • Cast one power three times. It doesn't look or sound the same three times.
  • With a sword out, a one-handed cast never hides the sword and never waves it about.
  • Nothing that matters happens below the bottom of the screen.
  • No sound makes you say "synthetic".

What doesn't change

The Beam, Charged Shot's ball of lightning, Storm Call, the ground wave, how powers level, and the server deciding all damage. Every rule you've given stands: no timing layer, no dings, blocky fists with no fingers, nothing fights for you. This plan doesn't touch the K menu or rebalance damage.

Why it's like this

  1. The arm can't act. It's a rigid block, and the only thing an animation can say is where its end is. That's why a palm, a punch and a reach all look alike.
  2. The animations were written from numbers and never watched. The spec says so itself: "authored from the numbers, not yet seen". That's how wind-ups ended up off screen.
  3. A cast and a weapon can't share the screen. The system has two choices, hide the weapon or move the weapon hand.
  4. The sounds are built, with almost nothing recorded to build from. The whole library behind the power sounds is seven field recordings and two free packs. Yesterday took the ring out of four powers and kept the method. It's the same method as the first pick sounds, which you called "super synthetic". When those were remade from Minecraft's own recordings you said "WAYYYY better".
  5. A power's identity is mostly its colour. Thirty effects, forty shared sounds and eight screen kicks are spread across 63 powers.
  6. Nothing changes between two casts.
  7. Combos are tables. A Weave is one of four shapes in your family's colour, whatever weapon you hold and whatever power you cast.

Two things I haven't proved. I don't know that a blocky arm with an elbow will look right. And I can't hear, so I don't know that layered game recordings will sound right to you. The first stage exists to answer both.

Hands

The left hand casts and your weapon stays

One-handed casts move to your left hand. Your weapon drops into a low guard in your right and never leaves the screen, so you can swing, cast and swing again. Two-handed casts lower the weapon properly and bring it back, using the same motion the game already uses when you climb. This is the same Spirit Palm, today and proposed.

An elbow and a wrist

The arm stays blocky, with no fingers, as you ruled. What changes is that the forearm can point anywhere and the wrist can turn. It can lie across your chest, stand straight up or drive forward. Every animation made so far keeps working unchanged.

Your arm shows the power

Light runs from your elbow to your fist while you wind up, leaves through your hand on the release and fades as the arm comes home. Each family has its own pattern, drawn in pixels on the skin's own grid so it looks like it belongs to the arm. Barrier, Mend and the other powers that sit on you show here, so you can finally see them in first person.

Rules every animation has to pass

  • Every pose you're meant to see is on screen, above the hotbar.
  • No hand sits over the middle of the view for more than a blink.
  • A big strike pulls back first, goes past where it stops, and settles.
  • Two hands never start on the same tick. One leads.
  • No two powers share an animation.
  • You can cut the recovery short with your next action.
  • Each power has its own screen kick, in place of the eight shared ones.

The first five are checked by the build on every animation, so they can't slip back. And nothing is called finished until its film has been looked at frame by frame.

What it keeps
Blocky fists, your skin, left-handed play, the comfort settings, and what other players see: every first-person animation gets its third-person twin in the same step.
The trade-off
The hands are shared with weapons. The weapon session and I have to agree the arm's new fields before either of us builds. A two-handed weapon needs its left hand to let go for a moment.
How we test it
The same seventeen casts filmed again, shot for shot against today's. Then you, with the HUD off.

Sound

A score for every power, from Minecraft's own recordings

Each power gets a score. A cast has beats: the gather, the peak, the release, the hit and what's left afterwards. Each beat is a few layers, and each layer is one of the game's own recordings at a pitch and a volume. A Haymaker's charge becomes a crossbow being drawn slowly, leather tightening, and the Warden's heartbeat getting faster. The game already has these files, so nothing of Mojang's goes into our project, and the sound belongs in Minecraft because it is Minecraft.

  • The world joins in. The hit adds the sound of whatever you hit: stone, grass, a zombie.
  • Never the same twice. Most recordings come in several takes. Each cast picks different ones and shifts the pitch a little.
  • Nothing that sings. Amethyst, note blocks, bells, the beacon and the level-up sound are off the list. The ring checker still runs on every score.
  • Where the game has nothing, a real recording of the real thing. No synthesis.

Variety

Takes

Every cast gets two or three takes: the same move with a slightly different path and timing. You never see the same one twice running. Everyone watching you sees the take you see.

Ways

About twenty of the physical powers read what your body is doing when you press. Standing, sprinting and in the air each get their own animation and change the shape of the power. They don't change its damage and there's nothing to time. It's the situation, which is how you asked powers to reward you.

First to get ways: Shockwave, Ground Slam, Haymaker, Spirit Palm, Nova, Frost Nova, Thunderclap, Terrain Rip, Beam and Charged Shot.

A look of its own for every power

One rule: no power may end as only a ring, a flash and a number. Each gets one effect that only it has, made of its own material and tied to your hands or the ground. Yesterday's plan already describes many of these, such as real frost crawling over ground and water for Frost Nova, the air breaking in a wedge for Thunderclap, and loose things flying into the Well. This plan makes it a rule for all 63 and adds three smaller ones: powers that sit on you show on your arms, the plain squares go, and burning gets our own fire in place of the game's.

Combos, new powers and other ideas

With your weapon

Today a Weave is a ring or an echo in your family's colour. I'd have the weapon perform it: when a cast lands in a Weave's moment, your blade carries the power through a swing of its own.

On top of that, twelve Arms Duets. Each pairs one power with one weapon. You find them by trying things, and they're counted with yesterday's Duets in the Journal.

With your movement and with each other

Ways are the movement combos. Yesterday's plan already makes casts count in the Ascent's chain, and its 24 Duets stand. The one thing I'd add is that each Duet gets its own animation, effect and score, so finding one is something you see and hear.

Break finishers

When you break a boss's stance and cast into it, the power plays a short finisher in first person, one for each family. A Kinetic punch goes right through and the boss folds over your arm. With Velocity you're past it and back before it falls. With Shadow, your shadow finishes it a beat after you do. You get a couple of seconds to do it, so it rewards the moment and needs no timing.

New powers

I looked for gaps before ideas. Heat has one power, and it's a dash. Frost has one. Yet your weapons can be attuned to both, and yesterday's Burning and Chilled States have almost nothing to cause them. Nothing in powers answers the forge, and Flight has no power that helps you move. These five each fill one of those. Yesterday's eight new powers aren't built yet either, so these would queue behind them.

Heat · from the War Anvil

Brand

Press your palm on a creature or a block. It holds the heat and glows from inside. Your next weapon blow on it sets it off. A branded wall bursts outward, and a branded creature that dies spreads fire to what's near.

In your hands: a flat left palm, ember cracks running down your arm into the thing, and a glowing handprint left behind. You'd hear: a flame catching, lava popping, iron cooling. Why it earns a place: it makes your weapon the trigger, and it comes from forging.

Frost · from a frost gate's Lord

Hoarfrost Lances

Hold the key and up to five lances of ice grow over your shoulders. You see their tips at the top corners of your view. Every swing of your weapon looses one at what you hit. Press again to loose the rest together.

You'd hear: the game's own freezing sound, a Creaking locking still, a trident thrown. Why it earns a place: it works through your weapon and leaves things Chilled for the Duets.

Flight · from one long glide

Updraft

Plant a column of rising air. Ride it up, glide off the top or dive out of it. Creatures, arrows and loose things caught in it go up too.

You'd hear: a Breeze, the elytra's rush, a wind charge. Why it earns a place: it's ground you make for your own movement, and the first Flight power that meets the Ascent.

Heat · from an ember gate

Backdraft

Breathe in. Every fire near you goes out, every burning creature stops burning, and all of it comes into your chest. Breathe out and it leaves as one cone. The more you took in, the bigger it is.

Why it earns a place: it feeds on what your other powers set alight, and it puts out a fire at home.

Spirit · from the Wild Lands

Thornwake

Point at the ground and the plants that are really there answer. In a meadow, grass snares their feet. In a forest, roots come up and hold. On bare stone you only get a stumble.

Why it earns a place: it ties powers to the wild, and it gives the Held State a source.

Each one would show its first step on the Tasks page and keep its Reading in the Journal, like yesterday's.

One for later, if the rest lands well: your beam meeting a boss's beam and pushing against it.
