The Way Forward

Out of Carillon's gate, the world should already be pulling you somewhere: smoke from a camp up the road, a gate's pillar past the trees, a vault that stirs as you pass and tells you what it waits for. No guidebook. Every lock you meet says what it wants, the world remembers it for you, and when you hold the key it calls you back.

Already in · Phase 0 · branch pack/progression

The vault answers your rank

The line is no longer given in the forecourt. It now needs C (or the vault's own door rank, if higher) and the sealed door open. So you open the door, go in, and the vault calls as you come back out under its pylons. The thrown line, the pull onto the pylon and the mast are unchanged.

Short of that, the vault stirs: its light lifts off the paving in the rank's colour, stalls and settles back. "The vault stirs, and stills." then "Its line waits for a C-rank hunter." If you have the rank but the door is still shut, the light drifts toward the door instead: "The vault stirs. Its door will know you."

The sealed door now reads anyone short of its rank who walks up to it, touches it or strikes it: the ward's light holds on the core, a refusing tone plays, and it says "Sealed. It reads you and stays shut. Come back at C-rank." Before, a strike did nothing at all, which read as a dead block.

A second hole the audit found: a Hearth Anchor beside a vault switched its ward off, so an E-rank player could dig round the door. A vault's walls now hold even inside a Hearth.

The guidance design

The world pulls you forward

  1. Every lock is seen before it opens, and says what it waits for, in the world's own words: a rank, a level, or the ability's in-world name ("a line", "wings"). It names the key and never explains how to play it.
  2. The world remembers what you saw. A lock you met becomes a promise, kept in the Journal under "Waiting" and pinned on the Atlas.
  3. When you get the key, the world calls you back, once. "The spire you passed answers you now · ↗ 640 m."
  4. Three goals at all times, never nagging: Now (minutes, with a compass), Next (an evening, a place), Far (the next rank and what it opens).
  5. The world pulls, the HUD confirms. Smoke, light, sound, people and pillars come first; text is the receipt.
  6. Teach once, by doing, in the place. The Vow's thrown line is the model. Then never again.
  7. Building round a designed lock is answered by the place, at the first block, not by a wall of text.

The promise loop

The first thread

One short errand, led by the compass, that ends at a hub. BotW's first layout tied places together with routes, and about 80% of testers felt railroaded. Points with pull fixed it. So the thread stops at the camp, and after that you choose.

  • The clerk starts Kestrel's errand: the Roost, then the High Gate, then the camp.
  • Leaving the walls is a moment: "THE COMMONS · outside the walls".
  • The camp gets a fire and a smoke column, two people with level-1 asks, and a view of a gate's pillar and a vault.
  • The first gate is placed on purpose (registered, level 3, 300-500 blocks out along your road) and announced.
  • The Registry's first Directive fits the moment ("clear a floor") and shows its progress on the Now line.

Building up to a lock

At a Tether Spire, a sky platform or a hero tree's crown, your first block won't hold. The place says what it answers to, worded by what you already have.

  • No rope yet: "The spire sheds what you build. Its platforms answer to a line." Once: "A vault keeps a line for a hunter it opens to."
  • Rope in hand: a tip instead. "Climb it by rope: hold [key] at a ring."
  • The first time only, Show me opens your band's Ascent page: the rungs you hold, the next as a silhouette, the rest "???".
  • The crown, the core and a cache's best loot answer only the line. A clever climb with pearls still earns the view.
  • Terrain that's too dangerous stays gated by danger, never by a "higher level" message.

People who point, tips that show

  • The crier and the gate guards name real places. Named people get idle lines. The clerk answers "Anything for me?" with the one next step that fits you.
  • A level-1 flare can rise in the first 15 minutes. The E-rank pillar gets a look of its own.
  • Three "?" marks seeded on the Atlas at the Registry: the camp, the first gate, the spawn vault.
  • The tip system, finally switched on, through discovery-ux's director: K, M, your power's key, how XP comes, the first gate, the first night, each rung's how-to. Each shows once, in its place.
