The Shape of Danger

You walked out of Carillon, died to an elite skeleton at the edge of the Commons, and twenty blocks further north the land jumps to level 72, with the first hamlet inside it. This is why that happens, what a level should mean, and the fourteen changes I recommend.

The short answer. Distance should set how bad a place can get. The land should decide how bad it is, and say so before a creature does. Near the city the ground rises smoothly, like a starter zone. Further out you keep the patchwork you asked for, under a ceiling that grows with distance.

What happened to you

  • You died at (-1287, -1444), 708 blocks north of the city, on the Commons' north edge. That is level 8 land, so an elite skeleton there is level 10 with 4.4 times a skeleton's health. You were level 1 with a stone sword. Nothing warned you: the Commons never raises a danger warning.
  • On the camp road the level reads 8, then 72 twenty blocks later. That is Black Glades. Wickton, the first hamlet and its 16 people, stands inside it, and the road from the camp leads straight there.
  • The toast "Danger · Black Glades" waited 19 seconds in the queue before it showed, and said "be careful". Nobody could act on that.
  • This is not bad luck. In 60% of worlds there is level 20+ land within 1,200 blocks of the city, and in 85% there is level 45+ within 1,800.
One correction to the story so far. You died in the Commons, not in Black Glades. Both happened on the same road, and both come from the same rules.

Why a level-72 land touches the Commons

  • The safety rule measures to the wrong place. "Neighbours of the Commons stay mild" counts the distance to a region's middle. Black Glades' middle is 1,703 blocks out, 103 past the limit, though its edge touches the wall.
  • The odds round up. A 5% chance of a level 45 to 80 region becomes one region in every block of nine, every time. That is 11%.
  • Nothing eases a border. 8 on one side, 72 on the other. Inside 1,800 blocks of the city the biggest jump over 100 blocks is, in a typical world, 59 levels.
  • Nothing placed on purpose asks the land. The first hamlet, the first town, the first gate and the roads' hubs never check the level.
  • Elites roam at every level. One in twenty hostile spawns is an elite, even on level 1 to 8 ground. At low levels an elite is a bigger step than the whole ladder.

What a level changes today

Only a monster's body. Nothing else follows a level: not the look, the sound, which creatures live there, or what they drop (AWAKEN's own creatures drop a few more shards, that is all). A pig gets a number on its plate and is otherwise a pig, which is why it died as easily at "level 68".

A level is faint at the start (level 1 to 8 is half again the health), so you can't feel one. An elite is bigger than that whole climb. Against a level 72 skeleton a stone sword needs about 26 hits, and its arrows hit nearly four times as hard.

What I recommend

A ceiling by distance, a temper by region. Distance decides the highest a land can be: 8 at the wall, 12 at 1,000 blocks, 20 at 1,500, 30 at 2,000, 52 at 3,000, 74 at 4,000. Each region has a temper, from calm to savage, that decides how close to the ceiling it gets. Same distance, different lands.

Borders are slopes. Levels ease across about 450 blocks, so you can see a land rising before you are in it.

The land speaks first, and a land is a rank. The ranks you already know, E to S, become the rank of the land. Each rank looks, sounds and pays differently:

Fourteen decisions

Each has the answer I'd pick. Say "go with your recommendations", or change any of them.

  1. Distance sets a ceiling; the land sets the levelYes

    Replace the dealt level bands with the ceiling above and a temper per region under it.

    Removes the cause of the 72 and keeps the patchwork: a dangerous valley two kilometres out, a calm one further.

  2. Borders become slopesYes

    Levels ease across about 450 blocks. Inside 5 km no 100 blocks change by more than about 15 levels.

    Today, inside 1.8 km, a typical world has a 59-level jump.

  3. A safe startYes

    The Commons stays 1 to 8, and the ground rises gently to about 12 by 1,000 blocks. The first hamlet must be level 12 or less, the first town 22 or less, the first gate your level plus 5 or less.

    The first three hours stay survivable, and nothing you are sent to is a trap.

  4. Quiet valesYes

    One region in eight beyond 2 km is calm, however far out.

    Somewhere to settle or retreat to after a bad walk. It keeps your "a calm one can wait five kilometres out".

  5. Roads and homes are calmerYes

    Within 24 blocks of a road creatures are 6 levels lower, fading out by 60. Around a hamlet the land is at most level 10, a village 14, a town 18.

    Safe corridors in, and the Hearth's calm for anyone's home.

  6. One set of ranksYes

    E 1-9, D 10-24, C 25-44, B 45-69, A 70-99, S 100+ for the plate, the Atlas and the land. The Atlas legend becomes 1, 10, 25, 45, 70, 100.

    Today the land and the plate use two different sets of edges.

  7. Elites live in places, not on the roadYes

    Roaming elites: none in E, 2% in D, 4% in C, 5% from B. A den keeps an elite guard you can see and walk round.

    It is what killed you. The pacing sim doesn't notice (below).

  8. A pig is a pigYes

    Animals that can't hurt you show no level. In higher lands each gives one more of its ordinary drop per rank above E (a pig in B land, four chops).

    Your pig. What changes is the game, not the pig.

  9. Each land owns the hunt's partsYes

    Beast trophies and hide come from the land of the tier's rank, and Hale's job names the land. Metal stays in lodes by depth in any region and light stays in gates. Nothing is "only here": the existing named beasts stay, and "Terror of <region>" joins them.

    Going somewhere harder pays, and no save or tier hits a wall. Agreed in principle with the Arms planner, whose own plan you haven't approved yet. This is Valheim's rule and Monster Hunter's.

  10. Going above your level pays in findsYes

    The kill-XP bonus stays at +40%. The first step into each higher rank is a small discovery, and wonders and secrets rank with their land.

    A bigger XP bonus would pay people to walk into death.

  11. The land speaks firstYes

    When a land is 5 or more levels above you the screen cools and a low sound comes in; at 10 or more it gets an ember edge. On roads a post marks where a higher land begins and its levels.

    Danger only works as a gate if it looks like danger. It is relative to you, so friends each see their own.

  12. Warn early, in plain wordsYes

    150 blocks ahead, one toast that jumps the queue. For your old Black Glades it would have said:

    Black Glades ahead. Creatures there are about six times as tough as you and hit nearly four times as hard. Turn back, or go in only to look.

    The Commons' wall warns a level-1 player too. Today's toast was 19 seconds late.

  13. Existing worlds switch on at the next loadYes

    No migration: levels are worked out from the seed each load. Names stay as they are. Your Black Glades becomes level 9 to 24 and Wickton level 10. Creatures already alive finish their lives.

    One cost: calm land beside the wall, like your Amberwood (1 to 7), becomes 9 or 10.

  14. Build orderYes

    Phase 1 now (decisions 1-8, 12, 13). Phase 2 straight after (11). Phase 3 with the Arms and deep builds (9, 10).

    Phase 1 is the fix.

Do the hours hold?

Building it

One builder at a time, no sub-agents, at the pace git history shows: about 9 to 12 hours of builder time. Phases 1 and 2 back to back are roughly one working day.

Phase 1 · the fix
4 to 5 hours

The new level rules and their data file, one set of ranks, placed things checked against the land, calm roads and homes, elites by rank, no chip on animals, the early plain warning, the Atlas shading, existing worlds.

Phase 2 · the land speaks
3 to 4 hours

The cool tint and low sound by how far above you a land is, road posts, a client setting, one look tour on CI and a listening pass on the Mac.

Phase 3 · finds by land
2 to 3 hours

Hunt parts and trophies by land with the Arms builder, surface signs for lodes, richer game, discoveries for first steps into a rank. Mostly in their files.

What I'm not sure of

  • The ceiling table is a design number, not a measurement. I set it so level 20 is about 1,600 blocks out, 45 is 2,900 and 70 is 4,100. It lives in a data file, so if it feels too tame or too far it is a five-minute change.
  • Out to about 1,000 blocks the level follows distance exactly. That is the one place distance is the whole answer.
  • With no roaming elites in E land the first hours may feel quieter. Dens keep a visible fight in reach from the wall.
  • Decision 9 follows the Arms plan, which you haven't approved either. If they change a tier's part, this changes with it. C tiers and up open three levels before their land, so the locked card and "What waits" do the pulling there.
