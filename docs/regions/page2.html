The Wild Grows Teeth

Walk out of the city and the world should change shape under your feet. A pig grows tusks. A zombie puts on a helmet and then a crown. The trees fill with silk, the birds stop singing, and the Living Wilds finally have the wonders, secrets and legends to make you want to see it all.

The short answer. Make rank something you see, hear and fight. Every common creature gets five forms (ranks D to S): a new body, a new move and a new voice at each step, not a recolour. The land around them is dressed and sounds the part. The rest of the Living Wilds goes in as the reasons to walk into it.

Three layers, one idea

What you see
Bodies

Tusks, horns, plates, spines and crowns hung on the vanilla creatures, plus scars and a glow. Rank sets the glow colour. The biome sets the material: moss and bark in forests, sun-baked bone in deserts, rime in the snow.

What they do
Moves

One new move per rung, always wound up where you can see it, always something you can answer with your dodge, block or parry. A pig lowers its head. A zombie raises a shield. A spider goes quiet.

What the world shows
Land

Signs dressed into new ground by rank, drifting particles and a sound bed, fewer and shyer animals the further you go, and the set pieces: wonders, secrets and legends.

Walk outward from the city

Drag the slider. The level is the highest a land can be at that distance (many lands are milder), from the ceiling you approved. The sketches are concept drawings of what a pig and a zombie would look like there. They are not final art.

The plate chip is the colour you would see on a creature at the ceiling, from the rule you approved: grey ten or more levels below you, blue up to four above, orange from five above, red from fifteen above.

The ladders

Twelve species in the first drop. Land E, the first 850 blocks or so, stays exactly as it is, so the first hour is untouched. From D up, each rung changes the shape, the move and the voice. Names are working names. Pick a species.

Zombies also cover husks, drowned and villagers; skeletons also strays, bogged and parched. Within a land the mix is about 70% the land's own rung, 25% the rung below and 5% the rung above (a taste of what is further on, never in E land). Across a rank border it is half and half over three levels.

The land looks the part

  • Signs, in new ground. A worldgen feature reads the same land rule and dresses it: scratched trunks and mud wallows in D, skulls on fence posts and webs strung between trees in C, bare snags and the ribs of something huge in B, gravefields in A, weeping stones in S. Vanilla blocks and a handful of soft blocks of our own, with no loot. Never on a road, in a settlement or in a Hearth. Rust-stained outcrops in D land are the surface sign of iron (a pull only; iron stays in lodes by depth).
  • An ambient layer for each rank. Dust, then ash, then embers or cold motes, then drifting violet specks. A low sound bed, and a gentle colour grade through Lens. It sits under the screen cooling you already get when a land is far above your level.
  • A quieter land. Passive animals thin out and bolt from far away, birds and insects thin with them and fall silent in A and S. Hunting pays more per animal, and alphas still carry hide, so hide stays in supply.

The Atlas, smooth

Today the Atlas paints each region one flat colour. The server will send a level for every explored 16 by 16 cell, and the map will blend between them, so you can see a land rising toward the red before you reach it. A switch on the legend flips between the land's rank and how hard it is for you (the plate's blue, orange and red). Old worlds shade what they already explored at the next login.

The last four are new, built from the forms. Rank flavours the same kind: the Statue Field in B land has cracks that glow.

Secrets and maps. Three surface secrets per region, from eight kinds (a cairn over a buried cache, a spirit's grave with a lore log, a three-stone puzzle shrine, a hollow behind a waterfall, an old camp with a journal, a standing stone that glows on one moon, a den with a gift, a ruined shrine). The region card says "Secrets found 1 / ??". Treasure maps from camps, ruins and merchants are clues, never a pin.

Legends. Eight in all, three first: Old Greymane (a white stag who walks with the migrations and can be calmed with the Herdcaller Horn), Hush (a silent hound that hunts on new-moon nights) and the Glass Wyrm. One of each per world, rumours first, and when one falls it is gone for good and everyone hears.

The rest of C and D. Eleven chains by rank (D 4, C 3, B 2, A 1, S 1), each a reason to meet a form, such as "The Tusker of Black Glades" and "Silk Road". Nineteen happenings, ten of them tied to ranks: Tusker Moot, Silk Bloom, Ashfall, Thunderhead Herd, Crown Hunt, Revenant Night, Gravebell, Weeping, the Hush, and the migration across your fields.

What other games teach

The lesson that shaped this: colour alone reads as a reskin, and stat-only evolution disappoints. Change the silhouette, the move and the voice.

Sixteen decisions

They continue the regions plan's numbering (the first fifteen are approved and built). Each has the answer I'd pick. Say "go with all recommendations", or change any of them.

  1. Every common creature gets five formsYes

    D to S, each with a new body, one new move and a new voice. Land E stays vanilla.

    Nobody has to reach a gate to see the world change.

  2. The first drop's speciesYes

    Zombie, skeleton, spider, creeper, enderman, slime, witch, pig, cow, sheep, chicken, mooshroom. Wolf, fox, goat and horse come later.

    These are what you meet most. Sheep, chicken and mooshroom get D to B first and A and S on a second pass.

  3. One kit on the vanilla bodiesYes

    Overlay, glow and attached pieces, with no mixin. Rank sets the glow colour, the biome sets the material. A setting offers Full, Lite (no attached pieces) or Off. The plate chip stays relative to you; the glow is absolute so a party sees the same thing.

    Two colour systems, so the Almanac carries a key and the Atlas gets a toggle.

  4. One telegraphed move per rungYes

    A second on alphas from C up. Tells run from 1.2 seconds in D down to 0.7 in S. A move hits for at most 1.3 times a normal blow at that level, and is delivered with its class named, so guard, deflect and dodge answer it.

    Danger you can read, not danger you guess at.

  5. Voices drop and layer by rungYes

    A creature's own sounds drop in pitch and gain a growl, and each move has its own tell sound.

    You should hear a Boarlord before you see it.

  6. The first sight of a form is a momentYes

    An Almanac page, a tenth of a level, and one line in the corner. No toast stack.

    The discovery feeds the same "new thing" the Director already watches.

  7. The mix inside a landYes

    About 70% the land's rung, 25% the rung below, 5% the rung above (never in E land, never in the first hour). Half and half across a rank border.

    Two zombies are rarely the same, and a new form shows up before its land does.

  8. A quieter landYes

    Passive animals at 80%, 55%, 35%, 20% and 10% of today's by rank from D to S, bolting from far away, never at a settlement or Hearth. Birds and insects thin and go silent in A and S.

    Hunting pays more per animal and alphas still carry hide, so supply holds.

  9. The land is dressed by rankYes

    Signs in new chunks, a handful of our own soft blocks, rust-stained outcrops as the surface sign of iron. The Arms planner is told, since it is a pull and not a source.

    Old worlds get signs only in ground not yet generated.

  10. An ambient layer per rankYes

    Particles, a sound bed, a gentle grade. It turns off with the existing dread setting.

    Atmosphere, not a plate, so it stays on in pure survival.

  11. The Atlas shades smoothlyYes

    A level per explored cell from the server, a blended colour, and a switch between rank and "how hard for me".

    About a thousand bytes more per map tile.

  12. WondersYes

    One per region, sixteen kinds first, warded, flavoured by rank. A discovery of 0.15 of a level, rising to 0.5 at S, a Codex page and an Atlas icon.

    The reason to go somewhere dangerous.

  13. Surface secrets and treasure mapsYes

    Three secrets per region from eight kinds. Maps are clues with terrain words, never a pin.

    Underground secrets stay Between the Gates'.

  14. Eight legends, three firstYes

    Old Greymane, Hush and the Glass Wyrm. One of each per world, rumours first, they fall for good.

    Something every player in a world shares.

  15. Finish chains and happeningsYes

    The other 11 chains by rank and 19 more happenings, ten tied to forms and ranks.

    Every rank gets stories and events, not only levels 1 to 20.

  16. Order and who buildsYes

    Six builds below, one builder each at extra-high effort, no sub-agents. The mobs owner is asked first for one small change (making the telegraph helpers public), and Arms is told about the dressing.

    Each build ends with its tests green on CI and one tour.

Order and estimate

About 25 to 30 builder hours, and 12 to 14 hours on the clock with three builders side by side. For scale, the Living Wilds' phases B to E took about six hours on 25 October. This is bigger because it is art and worldgen. The least certain parts are the bodies (art needs review rounds) and the wonders (structures). You would see the first Tusker after the first build.

What could go wrong, and what I have not seen

  • I have not seen any of it on screen. That includes last turn's wild animals fighting and the blue, orange and red plate colours: they are tested by rules and goals, not by eye. Art is the schedule here, so every build gets a parade tour and contact sheets before it is called done.
  • Texture packs. A pack like Fresh Animations changes vanilla models. If it renames a part, the attached pieces fall back to Lite automatically. A pack that moves a part without renaming it could float a tusk, which is a known limit.
  • Fairness. Every move is wound up and capped, a rallying pack is capped by the existing summon budget, and the first hour never meets a form.
  • Not claimed. That these names are final, that the moves are balanced (the pacing sim checks the long marks and a playtest checks the feel), or that old worlds get signs and wonders in ground that is already generated.
