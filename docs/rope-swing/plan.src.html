The rope, made to swing

You asked for a rope that swings you through the world the way Spider-Man does, instead of dragging you at whatever the crosshair touches. This page shows why the rope does that today, what I want to build instead, and a live model of both that you can play below.

Click the view, then hold Space (or press and hold the view) for the rope. A D turn, S coasts. You run and hop on your own, and you drift back to the middle of the way when you don't turn. With today's rope each press throws once, and the slider sets where you look (it aims today's rope). The assisted swing keeps looking while you hold, so one long hold chains swing after swing. This is a model, not the game. It's 3D blocks and ticks (20 a second), today's rope ported line for line from AscentRules, AscentAnchors and AscentMoves, with simplified collision and no network. The same code drew every plot and number on this page (docs/rope-swing/proof/swing.js, run with node docs/rope-swing/proof/run.js).

What you asked for

Three things, the rope by far the biggest:

  1. The rope has to swing, not pull. It should pick a good place to hang from on its own, carry you under it in an arc, keep your speed, and throw you on, so chaining swings feels great and you don't smash into what you roped.
  2. Rope and dash were on the same key (Left Option). Fixed already, see below.
  3. Skyborn sprint stops working once the bar has run dry, until you tap Space. Fixed already, see below.

What good looks like

  • Strong: hold the rope key and keep looking where you want to go, and you swing on and on through a forest or a city without touching a trunk or a wall, faster than running, and timing the let-go gives a felt boost.
  • Weak: it swings, but you still clip walls now and then, or chains stall into a dead hang.
  • Not acceptable: being pulled into the block you aimed at, stopping dead against a wall, the rope deciding to drag you somewhere you didn't look, or a swing that the server and your screen disagree about.

Kept as it is: the rope stays a power you earn (the Tether), it still bites only real blocks, ziplines, rappel and the Tether Vow keep working, and no setting is needed to make it good.

Why it pulls you in today

The code already has a pendulum on paper. Five things in how it starts and runs a swing turn it into a pull. Each one was checked in the code and then reproduced in the model.

1

It bites where the crosshair is. AscentAnchors.findAimed takes the block under the crosshair first whenever it's 6 or more blocks up. A pendulum's lowest point is right under its anchor, so a rope on a wall in front of you swings you into the foot of that wall. Looking at a spire 46 blocks away ends in a hit at 2.4 blocks a tick in every one of 40 runs.

2

The rope bites long, then reels in hard. It catches at the full distance, then shortens at 1.2 blocks a tick so your feet clear the ground (AscentRules.reel). Each tick of reeling also pushes you toward the anchor. On a cliff the aimed rope is 36 long and fits to 9, so it pulls you 27 blocks straight at the face. That is the "brings you straight forward" you felt.

3

A yank toward the look on the bite (0.22 blocks a tick) adds to the pull.

4

The catch throws speed away. When a slack rope goes taut, all the speed along the rope is lost, so a slow entry hangs dead and a fast one stops short.

5

Nothing looks ahead. No check that the arc is clear, no let-go at the top (you swing back toward the anchor), and the best let-go adds only 6% speed, too little to feel.

Not the cause: the server. It only checks that the anchor is a real block in reach and caps the speed. Nothing it does pulls you in.

Where it falls short. On the sandbox lane, today's rope at its best (a bot letting go at the perfect moment every time) goes a little farther in 15 seconds, 199 blocks against 177, because its pulls are fast when they don't hit anything. It also hits hard in 27 of 40 runs. Matching that distance with the assist is a gate in stage 2. Of the assisted runs' hard hits, 7 happen on the rope and 13 after letting go, while flying free into a trunk. Those 13 are why stage 2 adds the hand-off to a wall-run, as Spider-Man does.

How the assisted swing works

You hold the rope key and look roughly where you want to go. Everything else is the rope's job.

Choosing where to hang from

While you're in the air, the rope looks ahead every other tick with a fan of rays from your hand (66 of them, 22° to 72° up, either side of your heading, 44 blocks out). A good anchor is ahead and above, about 56° up, about 18 blocks of rope, and 3 to 12 blocks off to one side, so the arc passes beside it, never into it. A face near its top edge moves the anchor up to the edge, so roof lines and cliff lips work. Before it commits, it plays the first part of that swing forward and drops any anchor whose arc would meet something. A small marker shows where a press would bite now.

The swing itself

  • An arc, never a pull. The rope's length is fitted on the bite so your feet clear the ground at the bottom, and it never yanks or reels you in. If it has to shorten, it does so slowly on the way down (0.4 a tick) and your speed is kept.
  • You keep heading where you look. The body swings in the upright plane of your heading, under the point of the rope's anchor. A tower beside you holds the rope, and you swing past it. Turning your look turns the swing, as Spider-Man straightens his.
  • Speed is kept. The catch keeps 65% of the speed a real rope would throw away, the bottom of each swing adds drive, and holding forward pumps a little more. A swing too slow to reach its top gets more drive. It never hangs dead.
  • Walls. The rope looks 10 ticks ahead along the arc. If something is in the way it bends the swing 8°, 16° or 24° to go round it. If it can't, it lets go early with a hop. Stage 2 also adds the hand-off: a swing into a wall starts a wall-run instead of a hit.
  • Letting go. Let go on the way up, best at 62° past the bottom, and you get a boost (up to 14% speed and a lift). The rope also lets go at the top on its own, so you never swing back toward the anchor. Keep holding and the next rope bites as soon as there's a good one: one hold, a chain of swings.

When there's nothing good to swing from

  • A cliff or a wall dead ahead: a swing would end in the wall, so the rope zips you up to its lip and over the top instead (today's zip to a top, SPEC §17.6). In the model: 0 hits in 40 runs, 123 blocks covered, against today's hit and full stop every time.
  • Open land with nothing above you: the rope doesn't fire, and the marker doesn't show. No rope bites the sky. You keep your speed and carry on (decision 6).
  • Low ceilings and caves: the same rule as walls. If no arc is clear, no swing, and the zip takes over when the look is on a ledge.

What it feels and looks like

  • Camera. Field of view and a little distance grow with speed, a short kick on each bite, and the view tilts gently along the arc and settles at the top. Every amount is a Swing Studio tune and respects the game's FOV effect slider.
  • Rope. The rope stays our own woven line with its glow, now taut and humming at the bottom of a swing and slack and whipping as it lets go. The bite flash grows with speed.
  • Sound. Wind that rises with speed, a creak of load at the bottom, a sharp snap on a perfect let-go, all made with the project's own sound tools, nothing vanilla.
  • Hands and body. First person: the fist is pulled up and back at the bottom and thrown forward on the let-go, reusing the Swing Studio's tuning. Third person: the body leans into the arc and tucks at the top.

What other games do, and what we take

SourceWhat they foundWhat we take
Marvel's Spider-Man (2018), Game Developer design analysis The swing "cheats physics a lot": it straightens your arc to keep you going straight, shortens the web near the ground, and starts a wall-run when you meet a wall, instead of a crash. Straightening (the heading plane), the fitted length over the floor, the wall-run hand-off.
Marvel's Spider-Man 2 (2023), Destructoid, MP1st A ten-step Swing Steering Assistance slider, maxed by default. Insomniac said full assist is how the earlier games played, and that lowering it means hitting the ground and walls more. Ship full assist as the one tune. A slider waits until someone wants less help (decision 4).
Insomniac's swing camera, J.-B. Siraudin Field of view and follow distance rise with speed. Each web shot gives a short FOV bump, which reads as a dolly zoom. The camera's pitch follows the arc. The camera plan above.
GDC talks by Doug Sheahan: Concrete Jungle Gym (2019), Higher, Faster, Farther (2024) The talks exist, but their public pages don't give the swing-point maths. Our anchor rules are our own, and the model is what tests them.
Bionic Commando (2009), Destructoid, Worthplaying interview A physics swing with an on-screen guide for the best swing, and skill in when you let go. The team reworked it heavily after focus tests. Reward the let-go's timing, never punish missing it. The marker plays the guide's part.
Grappling Hook Mod for Minecraft (yyon), CurseForge Real rope physics, you steer with WASD and jump to let go, and a motorised hook that pulls you, based on Titanfall 2's. Hard to master. Shows what an unassisted rope feels like in blocks. We keep its honesty (real blocks only) and add the help.
Just Cause The grapple is mainly a pull to a point, saved by a parachute. My search found no design notes to cite. That is today's rope, the thing you don't want. The zip stays only as the fallback for walls.

Already fixed: keys and Skyborn sprint

These were small and safe, so they're in commit 225dbdf2 on my branch, with GameTests green on CI (run 40670175169). Not merged yet; the orchestrator merges.

The rope has its own key

"Rope (hold)" is a new key under AWAKEN in Controls, on Left Option. Your movement power (dash and friends) moved to mouse button 4. A world you already play gets the change once, with a tip naming both keys. Both can be rebound.

Other clashes between AWAKEN's defaults and vanilla's (Controls marks them red): G Focus and vanilla's quick actions, O Look and vanilla's friends list, X C Powers 2 and 3 and the creative toolbar keys (creative only). Every letter near WASD is taken, and Caps Lock can't be held as a key on a Mac.

Sprint takes a Skyborn glide back into flight

The cause: boosting empties the bar, and an empty bar folds flight into a glide. The bar refills in a glide, but only a jump turned a glide back into flight, so sprint did nothing until you tapped Space. Now a sprint press in a Skyborn glide takes flight whenever the bar allows, and sprint held through the glide takes off again by itself once the bar is a quarter full (about 5 seconds in the sun). A plain glide, from running off a cliff, stays a glide. GameTest ascent_sprint_flies_again checks all of this.

Multiplayer and keeping client and server in step

  • The swing's maths goes in AscentRules as plain functions, the same on the client and the server, as the rope's maths is today. The client plays it and the server checks it.
  • The anchor is still a real block in reach (the server's check doesn't change). The swing's plane is worked out from the anchor and your heading, both of which the server already has, so no new packet is needed.
  • The anchor search runs only on your own client, at most 66 short rays every other tick while you're in the air with a rope. It allocates nothing per tick. Other players see your rope as they do now.
  • Speed caps stay as they are. A let-go boost is within the cap, so a modified client can't fling itself.

How we'll know it's good

  • The model's six places become a GameTest harness that drives the real AscentRules, so the numbers above become the baseline and each stage must beat it.
  • Gates: no hard hits on the rope in the city, forest, cliff and spire runs; no run stops dead; the lane at least as far as today's best (199 blocks in 15 s); hard hits after letting go halved.
  • Then you, in the game. The model can't tell us if it feels great. Only you playing it can.

How it gets built

All by me, in this session, on this branch. Each stage is its own commit with GameTests on CI, and the old rope stays behind a switch until stage 2 passes its gates.

  1. The harness. The six places as GameTests driving the real rules, today's numbers as the baseline.
  2. Choosing the anchor. The ray fan, the scoring, the fitted length with no yank or reel, the marker, and hold to chain.
  3. The swing. The heading plane, keeping speed, the momentum floor, letting go at the top, bending round walls, and the wall-run hand-off. Old rope switched off once the gates pass.
  4. The feel. The let-go window and its boost, camera, sound, the rope's look, first- and third-person animation, and a city lane in the Ascent sandbox to try it.
  5. Tuning. Every number on a live-reload tune file, so we can adjust it from your play notes without a rebuild.

Stages 0 to 2 are the core. The rest can follow on the same branch. Splitting the feel (stage 3) to another builder wouldn't save much, since it leans on the swing's own numbers, so I'd keep it here.

Other ways I considered

  • A small repair: drop the crosshair bite, the yank and the reel, keep the rest. Cheap, and it stops most of the pulls, but the swing still sways side to side, stalls, and swings back. It wouldn't feel like Spider-Man.
  • The recommendation: the assisted swing above. It reuses the rope's code, rules, rendering and server check, and changes how anchors are chosen and how the swing runs.
  • Further: invented anchors in the sky, like the web wings of Spider-Man 2, so you can swing anywhere. It breaks the rule that a rope bites a real block, and Skyborn already covers open land.
