What you asked for

"gradually you get more and more tasks that lead to a natural progression… a maximum of a certain amount of tasks, like 1 or 2, or maybe 3 per thing… you always know what you can add or what you can build"

So the job is one list that opens up as you play, and the test is simple. At any moment, a glance at K answers "what can I do next that makes my home or my kit better?" The same list keeps your rule that bosses and secrets stay yours to find.

It works if Now fits one screen, finishing a step puts the next in the same place, and every unlock has a way to be learned. It fails if the list still runs past the screen, a row can never be finished, or the page names a boss.

Why three

One Do this next, one row per line of work, three rows per tab. Now holds at most 7: the next step, one row for each of the five tabs, and one from Out there.

I looked for the number rather than picking it. Your Hearth tab has exactly three kinds of work: grow the home, add to it, bring beasts. Across all the tabs the real lines of work run from one to four. Two would hide one of the Hearth's three. Five would only be filled with padding.

To be plain about the evidence: no study names three for a quest list. Memory research puts what people hold at about four things. The games below sit between one and five. The real count of work lines is what settles it, and it's still a guess until you've played it, so Stage 2 stops for you to try it and the cap is one number to change.

Telling and finding

You've asked for "direction without telling" and for always knowing what's next. They don't actually fight, because they apply to different things. Your own rule in the spec already draws the line: what to do next and where each piece comes from is said plainly, and what the world holds beyond it is still found. So every step gets one of three voices.

PlainAnything you build or make at home. Every Hearth, Forge, armour and weapon step. Says the name, what it takes and where."An anvil, the Forge and an armour stand in the same room."
LeadRare parts, beasts, bells, new ways of moving. Says what kind of thing and what kind of place. Never coordinates until the game has given you a bearing."Every town's granary keeps one against a ground-floor wall."
HiddenBosses, Provings, events, gate rooms, story, Truths and Titles. They never appear on the list at all.(nothing, on purpose)

The one place this needs your call is rare parts. "Every town's granary keeps one" tells you where to look, which is more than the world tells you today. I think it's right, because that silence is how the Storehouse got lost. That's decision 4.

How one task leads to the next

  • Lines of work. Finish a step and the next in that line takes the same place, marked NEW. Nothing else on the page moves.
  • Then: a quiet line under a plain row names the one step after it.
  • Add to your home is one row with up to three options. Pick one and the row becomes it; Change goes back.
  • READY shows when you've got everything. After the main path runs out, what's closest to done goes first.
  • A footer says what the next rank opens for you: "At B-rank your Forge can go to Tier III."
  • Later at the foot of each tab shows what's behind the rows, read-only, for when you want to look ahead. Set aside takes a row off the list for now, with Undo.
  • A tab shows up the first time it has something on it, then stays. Trails only fill empty places, marked WHEN YOU WANT, and never sit on Now.

The one rule for every other plan

Forged Pick, Gates as Worlds, Powers in Full Voice, Riding Along, Made by Hand and Storage all add steps. Each adds one line, and the book holds the cap for them. No plan can go over it even by accident.

Goals.Step.of("hearth/storehouse", Goal.HEARTH, "Build a Storehouse")
    .thread("hearth.build", 40)     // one row per line of work; lowest number first
    .lead()                         // Plain unless you say otherwise
    .teaches("room:storehouse", "block:awaken:storehouse_drop")
    .build();

And one test, modelled on the Journey's journey_ledger, walks every room, work plan, Hearth and Forge tier, beast, armour rank, way of moving and level-road unlock. Each needs a step that teaches it or a written reason it stays quiet. If a new plan adds something and forgets, the build says exactly what:

tasks_ledger: 1 thing can be unlocked but nothing teaches it:
  room:storehouse
Add .teaches("room:storehouse") to the step that leads to it,
or Teaches.quiet("room:storehouse", "why the player finds this alone").

What could go wrong

  • A system throws an error. That system's rows drop out for the moment, the rest stand.
  • Your pinned step disappears. It unpins itself.
  • A row can never be finished. The test catches it before it ships.
  • An old save. Loads as it is and fills its places the first time you open K.
  • A friend's server. The list stays per player, and Hearth rows read the shared home, so it holds up for your guild idea.
  • Three turns out wrong. It's one number. A config switch brings the old list back at any time.
