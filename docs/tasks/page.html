What you're after

The job: at any moment, know what you're working toward and what to do next for it, and trust that nothing on the list is done already. The pain: the game told you to do things you'd done; its offers are scattered over eleven sections, half of them menus; the Hearth page is walls of text; and when you got home with a bed built, nothing said whether to go mining, build a kitchen, make armour or take a gate. Asked for amethyst, it listed creatures you'd never seen instead of "mine a geode".

What it should feel like: a few big goals (your home, your armour, your weapon, your beasts, the bells, your rank), each showing exactly what it needs and where each need comes from, so "I need iron, so I'll go mining" follows on its own. One clear next step on top, the other options under it with why they matter, optional upgrades marked optional.

Strong: back home with a bed, the Tasks page says "Build your Armoury: a Fitting Stand and a crafting table in one room", and under it your first armour, your first gate and its Lord's Heart with the distance, the humming bell and the Burrowbuns, each with one line of why. Weak: the same lists regrouped into tabs. Unacceptable: a done thing anywhere, the same goal twice, a wall of text, a basic need hidden behind a room without saying so, or a reward the game used to give lost.

Keep: the top-right card and the top-left place (one each), Status first on K, Powers and Map as they are, E armour makeable in the first hour, the Mat's amethyst recipe, and "found, not told": no daily lists, nothing naming a boss before you meet it. Not in this plan: new quests or content, the Powers and Map pages, the toasts' look, and the bell frame's own design (bells-early's; the Bells tab is where its first step goes).

Three ways to do it

A · Tidy the pages

Move sections around

Put the to-do sections on a new page and leave each list as it is.

Loses: no goals, so still no "why"; the same goal shows two or three times; every list still asks you to choose.

B · Recommended

Goals, with needs and sources

A few standing goals, each a tab. Each system says what its goal needs; every need carries where it comes from, the easy way first. The server merges the same need from two systems, drops anything done, and picks one next step.

Why: it answers "what am I doing and why", reuses the Armoury's have/need cards and part 1's sweep, and keeps each system's rules where they are.

C · A quest log

Rewrite as quests

Turn every system into scripted quest chains.

Loses: weeks of rework, a fixed order in a sandbox, and the "list of chores" feel you turned down on 29 October.

Core goals

Six standing goals, each a tab on Tasks. A goal always shows its current step: what it needs, how many you have, and where each need comes from. Finishing a step shows the next; nothing past what you've seen is named.

Hearth

Grow your home

Fire, a bed, the Armoury, the Anchor (a Lord's Heart), then each tier's rooms. Optional rooms are marked optional.

Armour

Wear better armour

Your first E set at a Fitting Stand, then the Forge for D and up.

Weapons

Wield a better weapon

Hale's weapon, then patterns from gates and raises at the Forge (the Armoury's cards).

Beasts

Win the kin

Rumours to follow, wants you've read, nooks to fill.

Bells

Find the Hundred

The leads you have, the hum nearby, your tower. The bell frame's first step comes from bells-early.

Gates and rank

Rise in rank

The next gate, the level for your trial, the trial when it's ready.

Out there

What's near you

A star fell, a rift, a lair: things you can do now that belong to no goal. Shown on Now only.

Now

The first tab

One next step on top, the other goals' steps under it, each with one line of why.

The next step on top follows the main path: your fire, a bed, the Armoury and your first armour, your first gate and its Lord's Heart for the Anchor, the Forge, then your rank's trial. A step you pin wins over it. Where it comes from leads with what your rank can do where you are: mine it (with the depth and the land), gather it, craft or smelt it; a creature drop comes after, saying where that creature lives and whether that's near you.

The Armoury, your first workshop

Today E armour needs only a Fitting Stand with a crafting table within 6 blocks, D and up need an anvil and the Forge, and the Forge room (anvil, Forge, Fitting Stand) only lets the Forge temper higher. Nothing walks you there. The plan:

  • The Armoury is a new room kind: a Fitting Stand and a crafting table in one closed room, all copper and wood, the first hour. The Hearth asks for it right after your first room. It opens the Armour tab's "Choose your first armour".
  • It grows into the Forge: add an anvil and the Forge to the same room and it's both (one room can be several kinds). That opens D-rank armour, weapon raises and tempering, and the Weapons tab's raise step.
  • Nothing basic hides behind it. Any Fitting Stand with a table near it still makes E armour, and the Armour tab says so. What the room itself gives is decision 3.

How it behaves

A goal

Each tab is one standing goal with its current step: what it needs, have/need, and where each need comes from. A step that waits on something says what ("opens at D-rank: level 10"). Nothing past the next step is named.

Where it comes from

One service for every item a step needs, ordered by what you can do now: mine or gather it (depth, land), craft or smelt it, then creature drops with where the creature lives and "near you" when it's in your land. It grows from the Armoury's arms/sources.json plus a short hand list of the world's easy ways (geodes, ores, trees, plants).

One step per need

Every step carries a key (hearth/armoury, armour/first, kin/lampwing, bell/12). Two systems giving the same key make one row, with the most specific words, the place and the count kept.

Never done

Built from live state on the server and dropped before it's sent once met, through part 1's done checks. The sweep test covers every source.

The next step

Now's top step follows the main path; a pinned step wins. A pinned step that's done unpins itself. Track on the Hearth, Journal and Armoury pins the same way. The card and the corner show it.

At a glance

No paragraph longer than two lines on any page. Pieces and needs are icons with counts; rooms and slots are tiles; readings are bars. Rows never reorder while a page is open. Nothing expires daily.

Optional

An upgrade that isn't on the main path (a Sanctuary over a Bedroom) is labelled optional, dimmed, and never the top step.

Unknown slots

Categories with a fixed set (creatures, kin, bells, gate worlds, cave regions, happenings, ores) show numbered "?" slots. Open-ended ones (landmarks, lairs, settlements) show a count only. A "?" never names or hints.

Sent when it changes

At most once a second while a page is open, every 5 seconds otherwise, only when something changed. Per player, so multiplayer-safe.

Old saves

A followed trail becomes the pin. Declared Truths already met come true on first login in one line. Directives, the Regimen and request offers drop quietly; anything handed in comes back. A new small save (awaken:tasks, versioned) holds the pin and what you've seen. Discoveries already made fill their slots from the counters you have.

Tests and building

One GameTest per mechanism, on CI: tasks_one_row_per_need (the Ember from three sources is one step), tasks_done_never_shows (part 1's sweep over every source), tasks_next_step_follows_path (your moment: bed built, the Armoury on top, armour and the gate under it, Sanctuary last and optional), tasks_sources_easy_first (amethyst leads with a geode; a drop names its land), hearth_armoury_room (stand and table make an Armoury; add an anvil and the Forge and it's a Forge too), kin_want_on_tasks, truths_by_themselves, journal_slots (a find fills its slot; a "?" carries no name), and the band layout check with six pages. One CI tour shoots Tasks (Now and Armour), Hearth and Journal.
