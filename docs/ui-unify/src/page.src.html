2 · The idea

The band shows you things, and speaks only about the one you point at.

  1. One stage. K fills your view with the band's glass, laid out on the same sheet the Hall uses, instead of a slab in the corner.
  2. One layout everywhere. The thing in the middle, its neighbours beside it, one panel of words about it, one bar of keys under it. On K, in the Hall, in the bag.
  3. Pictures before sentences. The real item with what you have and need. A recipe drawn as a recipe. A needle and a distance. What you haven't found is a black shape.
  4. No tabs inside tabs. The six goals become six lanes on one view. The Journal's twelve categories become shelves on one wall.
  5. One word and one key for each thing. Track. Esc goes back, Tab moves to the next thing, hold to commit.
  6. A word budget, checked by a test, so the wall of text can't grow back when the next system is added.

5 · The rest of the system

The same parts everywhere

Every screen is built from one small kit. This is what makes the band, the bag and the workshops feel like one thing.

The Tab glance

You said "the tab menu". I took that to mean the panel you get while holding Tab. It keeps every fact and its width, and gets a third shorter: the labels become marks, and your step is one line with what it takes as items.

The Hall, pushed further

The Hall stays its own place. It takes three things from the system: "What makes it different" becomes marks with numbers instead of two paragraphs, the foot becomes the shared key bar, and Track gets its key. The War Anvil's "At most it can take Temper VI…" becomes six pips. The Inscribing Table's "6 levels and 2 lapis" becomes the two items with counts.

Two doors to every place. The Hall already works this way: from K you look and track, at the anvil you forge. The Hearth gets the same rule. Your Anchor opens the same Hearth place the band does, with building the next tier added, and the separate guide window goes away.

Two materials, one layout. Blue glass is what you wear: the band, the bag, the glance, the cards. Firelight is where you stand: the Hall, the anvil, the table. The material tells you which you're in; everything else is the same.

7 · Three ways to do it

I looked at three directions

I recommend B, the one stage you clicked through above. It's the only one that fixes the cause: the slab. It reuses what the Hall already proved, and it can be built in stages with today's band one setting away.

What you give up with B. Today the band leaves the right half of your view clear. The stage covers the view, with the world dim behind it, like the map's card. So the map's rule comes with it: if you're hit, or something hostile comes within 8 blocks, it folds at once. Also, Tab and Q/E change what they do inside K.

C is worth one experiment later: Status as your real character, standing where you stand.

  • If the list hasn't arrived yet, the stage opens and the panel says "Reading…". You can still change place or shut it.
  • If the server says no (too far, a stale choice), the line under the button says why, in the server's words.
  • If a step finishes while you look at it, its tile ticks and the next step takes its place. Nothing moves under your pointer.
  • Keys alone work for everything. The narrator reads the panel's first line when you land on a thing, as it does now.
  • Words are capped. Names and numbers on the stage. One sentence of 14 words or fewer in the panel. Longer help is a tip on the thing it explains. A test fails any step, room or power that breaks this.

9 · How it gets built

In stages, with today's band one setting away

  1. The proof, then you play it. The shared sheet, the kit, the new K shell, and the Tasks place. Nothing else moves until you've played this.
  2. Status and Powers.
  3. Hearth and the Journal wall. Your Anchor becomes the Hearth's second door.
  4. The bag's item names, the Tab glance, the cards.
  5. The workshops. The Hall's panel and key bar, the anvil's pips, the table's costs, then the boards and books.
  6. Clean up. The old band and its sections are deleted one build after you've played the new one.

What stays true. The server still decides everything. Every progression system still puts its steps on Tasks. No boss is named, shown or shaped before you meet it. Other players still see only a glow. No save data changes, so going back is one setting.

The risk I can't answer from here. A glass panel that covers the view costs more to draw than a slab. It must cost no more than the Hall's wall does today on Cinematic at 4K. That needs one timed run on your Mac (decision 7).
