Waypoints and Kin Plates

Two pieces of UI you asked for while playing. Your own waypoints on the Atlas, named and coloured, shown on the table, on the wrist and in the world. And a plate of their own for the beasts that live with you, in place of the hearts they show now.

"Ability to set waypoints on maps (AAA UI and same design language as now)"  ·  "kins like lampwing don't have a card? They just have the vanilla hearts. So let's add their custom plate."

Part one

Waypoints on the Atlas

Double-click the table where you want one. It drops in with the same pop as a pin today and its card opens beside it: type a name, pick a colour and a mark, and say whether your party sees it. Click any waypoint later to make it your destination, change it or remove it. The legend lists your nearest ones.

What the Atlas has today

Party pins: a double-click drops one, twelve each, no name or colour, always shared, and the oldest goes without a word when you drop a thirteenth. One destination at a time, which the Now line points to. Nothing marks a place in the world.

The wrist

The old wrist glance (a live mini map) isn't drawn anywhere since the band's pages came in. The wrist today is the Tab glance. A waypoint reaches it two ways: set as your destination it is the glance's "where you're heading" row with its needle, and while you hold Tab every waypoint in view is named in the world.

1

Pins become waypoints

One kind of marker instead of two. Today's pins carry over as shared waypoints named "Pin", in your colour, so nothing your party dropped is lost.

yes, one thing called a waypoint
2

Making and changing one

Double-click ground you know (as pins today), or press N on the table for one where you stand. Its card: a name (32 letters at most; it starts as "A spot in the Greenfold", already selected so you can just type), eight colours, eight marks (flag, diamond, star, house, pick, cave, ring, cross), share with my party, remove. Click a waypoint later for Set as destination, Change it (E) and Remove. V hides or shows them all on the table.

as described
3

Sharing with the party

Each waypoint has its own switch, off by default. A shared one shows to your party members online in the same dimension, in its own colour with your name under it. They can head for it but only you can change or remove it. If someone leaves the party, their waypoints leave with them.

per waypoint, off by default
4

How many

64 each, across every dimension together. Nothing is ever removed for you: at 64 the card says "You have 64 waypoints. Remove one to make room."

64, and no silent removal
5

Where they're kept

On the server, in your Atlas record (the one that already holds what you explored and your pins). So it's per world, each waypoint knows its dimension, it's kept through death, it works the same on a server and in single player, and a new computer has them all. The server checks every one: the name's length, a colour and mark from the set, ground you have explored, the limit.

server, per world, per dimension
6

The mark in the world

Only a waypoint you are looking toward shows: within 25° of the middle of your view it's a small mark in its colour, and within 6° it adds its name and distance. Never more than four at once, none in a fight, and each fades out once you're within 12 blocks (you've arrived). Your destination's mark always shows. Holding Tab shows and names every waypoint in view. Classic screen: the same rules.

quiet by default, Tab shows all
7

Where you fell

A grey waypoint at the spot of your last death (not inside gates), so you can walk back for your things. It goes away when you reach it or die again. It doesn't count toward the 64.

Part two

A plate for your kin

The creature plate you know, made warm: the lantern's rim instead of the band's blue, the name you gave it, what it is and who it lives with, its health as a soft line with no level and no red, how close you and its kind are, and what it's doing. It reads as yours at a glance, never as something to fight.

Left to right: your Lampwing at home, a wild one you've helped twice, and for comparison a wild beast's plate as it is today. Your own kin's plate never carries a level or the fight bar's segments.

Why kin show hearts today

A creature gets a plate only when it carries a level tag. The Living Wilds tags vanilla creatures, and our own creatures tag themselves, but the kin never do. So they have no plate, and Jade (the "what am I looking at" card in the pack) fills the gap with its hearts. Tamed wolves and cats are left out of plates on purpose, so they show Jade's hearts too.

Why riding shows hearts

The clear screen hides vanilla's hearts, armour, food and XP, but not the mount's own row of hearts. So riding a Frost Stag, a Dune Ray or a horse shows vanilla hearts above the hotbar.

8

Who wears it

Every kin, at home or wild, and every creature that is yours: a mount that trusts you, a tamed wolf, cat or parrot. A Gnawed kin (the Deep has got into it) wears it too, but dark with an ember rim and "Gnawed" on it, so you know it's one of them and that it can be freed.

kin, your mounts and your pets
9

What it shows

Its name (the one you gave it, else its kind). Under it, its kind and who it lives with: "Lampwing · lives with you", "lives with Ana", or for a wild one "Could live with you". Its health as one soft line in its kind's colour, amber when low, never red, no level. How close you and its kind are as four marks (Wary, Curious, Friendly, Kin); a wild one you're helping says how far off its trust is ("One more help and it will trust you"). What it's doing, in the Hearth page's words ("lighting the yard", "hiding, hurt. It'll come back when it's well").

as in the mockup
10

Naming them

A name tag already works on kin. Better: on the Hearth page, each of your beasts gets "Give it a name". A small field, checked by the server (24 letters at most), and the plate and the page use it from then on.

yes, from the Hearth page
11

When it shows

When you look at it, with the same reach and fade as every other plate. One of yours that gets hurt near you shows its plate for 4 seconds. At most two at once. While plates are on, Jade's card goes quiet for these creatures, as it already does for every wild creature ("only the plate shows").

look, or hurt near you
12

Riding

On the clear screen, the mount's hearts give way to a ride line just over your health line, with its name: shown for 3 seconds when you climb on, and again while it's hurt, at a third of its health or less, or in a fight. The jump bar stays as it is. Classic keeps vanilla's hearts.

the ride line

Neighbours and size

The Boss Plate and the plate rules stay as they are (the boss-fights session is working there). The kin plate is its own code in the kin module. Three one-line touches outside it: Jade's "this creature has a plate" check, the wild-plate picker skipping your trusted mounts so they don't wear two plates, and the clear screen's list of vanilla HUD pieces it hides (the mount's hearts). No new Atlas hooks, so the bells session's marks are unaffected.

Cost: a kin plate is one more of the plates you already have, at most two. The world marks are at most four small quads a frame, worked out once a tick from at most a few hundred waypoints. Each mechanism gets one test (the waypoint rules and their save, who wears the kin plate, when the ride line shows), then one CI tour at the MacBook's scale with the Atlas, a kin plate and the ride line in shot.
