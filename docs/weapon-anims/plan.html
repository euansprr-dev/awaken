Swing Studio

A plan to make every weapon's swing feel right in first person, our weapons and vanilla's, and a loop that turns your notes into a better swing you can feel in game within minutes, round after round.

In short
  • "Too far up" is real, and I can measure it. Every cut is built as a lever turning about a point below the bottom-right corner of the screen, so the blade sweeps over the top half before it lands. 34 of our 48 cuts do most of their fast moving above your eye line, and the tip meets the target level with the crosshair, pointing up.
  • The floaty katana has several causes stacked. After each cut the blade drifts back across the screen for about 0.3 s. The swoosh plays before the blade arrives. The katana is swung with one visible hand. And every combo link snaps the blade back to rest.
  • The fix is a system, not one tweak. Each weapon gets a feel spec: its feel written as numbers. Every swing is checked against it with numbers and pictures, and you feel each change live in your game, without a restart, with an A/B switch against the last version.
  • First build: the loop, the fixes every weapon needs, and a re-shaped katana. After that: you send a note and have the change in your hands in about 15 minutes.
  • Third person follows each weapon once you sign off its first person, re-derived from the first-person blade the way the katana's and greatblade's third person already are.

1 · What you asked for

The job. You fight in first person most of the time. You want every swing, our 16 styles and the vanilla swords, axes and mace, to feel like a top action game: sharp, heavy where it should be, connected to what it hits.

The pain. It's decent but wonky in places. Some swings feel like they hit too far up instead of naturally. The katana feels floaty, like you're not cutting with a sharp blade and real strength. Third person needs a lot of work, but first person comes first.

What you asked for. A systematic way to test and improve, over and over, from your notes per weapon, until each one is perfect.

A strong result

You say "the katana is floaty"; within 15 minutes your game has a new katana and you can flip between it and the old one on the same swing. Each weapon reaches "that's it" in a few rounds, and nothing you already liked gets worse.

A weak result

Each change needs a rebuild and a restart. The numbers get better but you can't feel the difference. One weapon gets fixed while another quietly gets worse.

Unacceptable

Blows landing at a different time than the server judges them. Blocking, the crafting-table hands or the climb stow breaking. Tuning files written into your play copy without you switching them on.

Must not break: the guard and its jolt when a shot hits it (projectile blocking), the station scenes (crafting table, furnace), climbing putting the weapon away, the charge and tap rules, the edge-first and working-part audits, the blade ribbon, and third person as it is today until its own stage.

Not in this plan: new moves or combos, combat balance, creature animation (the boss plan's Creature Kit), power casts (the power-levels plan).

OptionWhat it isWhy not / why
A. Targeted repairChange the baker's shared numbers (lower target, quicker way home), add blending, two hands and sound timing. B. Swing Studio recommendedA feel spec per weapon (data), a feel report that checks every swing, live reload in your game with an A/B switch, a log of every round, plus the fixes from A built into the runtime once.Your notes map to named knobs. Most rounds need no build. Each weapon is tuned alone, and the report shows any other weapon that moved.
C. An in-game animation editorDrag key poses in game, scrub a timeline, save.A big build, and it makes you the animator. You want to describe what feels wrong, not pose arms. Parked, and B doesn't rule it out later.

What would change my mind: if you'd rather tune by hand yourself than describe it, C wins. If after the first build the live loop doesn't make rounds faster than a rebuild would, B shrinks back to A.

Splitting the work: I don't recommend it. The loop needs one owner who keeps the context of every round. Third person could become its own session later, once first person is signed off.

4 · How it works

The loop, from your side

  1. You swing, you say what's off. Tell the orchestrator or me, in your words: "katana second swing is floaty". If you want it pinned to the exact swing, type /awaken swing note katana second swing floaty right after it. That saves the weapon, the move, the round and whether it hit.
  2. I find it in the numbers. The note becomes measures (the word list below). I reproduce it in the feel report before I change anything. If I can't find it in the numbers, I say so and send you two candidate versions to pick from instead of guessing.
  3. I change the feel spec, never the code if I can help it, rebake (a few seconds), and check the report: this move improves, its class rules pass, no other weapon moved.
  4. Your game picks it up live. A chat line says "Katana: round 4 loaded". No restart.
  5. You compare. /awaken swing ab flips between this round and the last on the same swing. Say which is better.
  6. I log it and keep it. The round, your words, what I changed, the numbers before and after, and your verdict go into the rounds log, and the change is committed so the next build carries it for good. A worse round is simply rolled back.
Commands you'll have in game (after the first build)
/awaken swing tune on | off
Your game starts or stops reading tuning rounds from the tuning folder. Remembered between launches. Off by default.
/awaken swing ab
Flip between this round and the last one. The chat says which one is live.
/awaken swing blind
The same flip, labelled only 1 and 2, for close calls.
/awaken swing note <words>
Pins your note to the last swing you made.
/awaken swing last
Says what the last swing was: "Katana, strike 2, rising cut, round 4, hit".
/tick rate 5
Slow motion (vanilla's command; cheats on). Today it would break the swing, because the hands keep real time. I make the hands follow the game's tick rate, so the whole swing, hit-stop and sound slow down together. /tick rate 20 to go back.

How I check a swing without you

The feel report (one command, every move or one weapon) gives the numbers above per move, each with its class range, pass or fail, and the change since the last round. It also gives three pictures per move:

  • A frame strip at 60 fps. The lab draws the weapon as the game composes it, frame by frame at real spacing, so slow parts show as crowded frames and fast parts as gaps. Animators read motion this way.
  • The tip on the screen with the eye line and crosshair, like the chart above, this round over the last one.
  • The speed curve with its phases marked (load, pause, drive, hit, bite, carry, home) and the swoosh's peak on it.

Rules that stop a bad round. A GameTest, arms_feel_rules, reads the baked swings and holds every move to rules that are true for every weapon. The fastest moment is the hit. Cuts cross below the eye line. No combo link jumps. The swoosh peaks within 25 ms of the hit. Two-handed weapons keep both hands on. Plus each class's ranges. The existing working-part audit and arms_fp_swings stay. CI runs them on every push.

What the lab can't tell: how it feels at your frame rate with sound and hit-stop. That's your verdict, and it wins over the numbers. When you approve something the rules call wrong, the rule moves, and the log says so.

Rules for the tuning folder. Your game reads it only while tune on. A file from a newer schema than your build is refused with a chat line ("Round 6 needs the next build"). A round number lower than the one built into your jar is ignored, so an old round never overrides a newer build. The reload parses off the render thread and swaps between moves, never mid-swing. Only first-person visuals, the view's lean and the swoosh's timing come from it. The server never reads it, so nothing about damage or timing can change through it, and other players are unaffected.

Rounds that need code (a new sound file, a new kind of knob) ride the next build. I hand those back as usual and say which notes they answer.

Failure cases

CaseWhat happens
A tuning file is broken or half-writtenYour game keeps the swings it has and says why in chat. I write files whole, then move them into place.

Tuning left on after we're doneOn joining a world the chat says "Swing tuning is on: round N". Accepted rounds are in the jar anyway.
Your note is vague ("feels off")I ask one short question, or send two versions as A and B.
The numbers pass, but you say it's worseYour verdict wins. The round is rolled back and the rule is adjusted, logged.
A class change moves another weaponThe report lists every move that changed. Only the weapon in the note may change, unless the round says the whole class.
First and third person drift apart while tuningExpected until a weapon's third-person stage. Third person stays exactly as today until then.

Performance: no allocation per frame. Polling the folder once a second is a file-time check. Parsing a 1.2 MB file takes about 50 ms, off the render thread. Nothing is sent over the network. Notes stay on your disk.

5 · Before and after

The katana, the case you named

Today

You click. The swoosh starts and the blade rises up and out of the right edge. About 50 ms after the swoosh peaks, the blade sweeps back in over the top third and meets the target level with the crosshair, pointing up. The view leans 2° with the blade. The blade glides back across the bottom of the screen for 0.3 s. Click again early and it jumps to rest before the next cut.

After (planned; the motion numbers are the lab prototype's)

You click. Both hands bring the katana back to the right shoulder and it holds there, visibly loaded, for about 40 ms. It drives through on a flat line, crossing at chest height (−0.23) as the swoosh peaks. On a hit it bites for a beat, then tears through and leaves the frame low left. The view snaps and settles. It comes back up from below the frame and is still by 375 ms, so a quick second click flows on with no jump.

"This swing goes too far up" on the greatblade

Your note pins Greatblade, strike 1. The report shows all of its fast motion above the eye line and the tip at the hit at +0.10. I lower its crossing and lay the blade more across; the report shows the new height, every other greatblade move unchanged, and the class rules passing. Your game loads round N+1, and you A/B it on the next swing.

A note the numbers can't see

"The axe feels cheap." Nothing in the report is out of range. I don't guess silently: I offer two directions as A and B (a bigger bite and a lower thump sound, or a heavier wind-up), and you pick by feel. Your pick becomes the axe's spec.

Something fails

A round's file arrives cut off on the external drive. Your game says "Katana round 5 couldn't be read, keeping round 4" and plays on. I rewrite it.
