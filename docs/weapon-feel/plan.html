AWAKEN · weapon motion and fight feel · plan for your approval · 10 Nov 2026 · nothing is built

Weight in the Hand

The swings are decent, as you said. What keeps them from the top level is not the swings. It is what happens around them: whether a hit you see really landed, whether you can hear it, whether the blade and the world agree on which way it went, and whether the weapon has any weight when it stops. This plan keeps every swing and fixes those.

The short version
  • Keep all 179 moves as they are. You haven't played the merged round yet, so no weapon's motion is changed on a guess.
  • Make hits honest first. In your last 15 fight sessions, 3 in every 10 hits you saw and heard did no damage on the server.
  • Then sound. The hit sounds are almost all bass that a laptop can't play, and the parry is the kind of synthetic ding you turned down in the powers.
  • Then weight. A shake in the freeze, a settle after the cut, a draw for every weapon, a blade that bounces off stone.

2 · What I found

Six things, each checked against the real game

I read the hands code, the whole weapons section of the spec, your rules from 9 Nov, and your own play logs. I measured all 179 baked moves frame by frame, all 102 weapon sound files, and the 447 seconds of your play that the black box kept on 9 Nov (16,384 frames at 75 a second, 200 presses).

1

Three in ten of your hits are ghosts from your logs

The game plays a hit the moment your blade arrives, then the server confirms it. When the server doesn't, you saw the sparks, heard the hit and felt the freeze, and nothing happened to the creature. The game counts these when you leave a world. Across your last 15 fight sessions:

One session had 19 ghosts in 25 hits. On 8 Nov you said hits feel random. This is the number behind that.

What I don't know: the cause. Three fixes went in after these logs (only the hands decide the combo, a boss's body arrives on time, a hit waits for the blade). Nobody has measured since. And the server never tells the game why it disagreed, so the logs can't say. I have two guesses and neither is proven. One: the game draws other bodies about three ticks behind where the server has them, so a creature your last strike knocked back is already out of reach on the server when your next strike "hits" it on screen. (In today's film, against a husk that can't move, all 9 hits shown were confirmed. So the ghosts come with moving creatures and fast chains, not with every hit.) Two: when you click faster than the chain, the server can land a blow under one press's number while your game files it under the next, so the blow is played twice and one copy is counted as a ghost.

3 · The lesson from the sixteen rounds

Why they made it worse, and what this plan does instead

  • They tuned to scores. One score, "drift", counted any slow motion after the hit as a fault. Round 2 took it from 172 moves to 2, and round 3 made each strike stop in view and hold for the next press. Between them they left the dead stop in finding 5. Round 3 even listed "the settle is now seen" as a cost.
  • No capture had a real click. Films ran at a tenth of speed and test clicks lasted ticks. Yours last about a millisecond.
  • They changed motion you hadn't asked about, one complaint at a time, each change moving something else.

So this plan sets itself four rules. No script score is ever a target. No weapon's motion changes until your hands have flagged it. Every new feel layer is tried on two weapons first, and you pick between it and today's in the game before it goes anywhere else. Your play log is read before any theory.

4 · Options

Three ways to do it, and the one I recommend

A · Only fix what is wrong

Ghost hits, the wrong-way kicks, the hit sounds. Small and safe. It gets the fights to "correct" and stops there. No weight, no draws, no world.

B · Keep every swing, add truth, sound and weight around it recommended

Everything in A, plus layers that sit on top of the same keys: the shake, the settle, the draw, the glance off a wall. Each has its own switch. Each is tried small before it spreads.

C · Re-make the twelve rule-made weapons by hand

Bring every weapon to the Daggers' hand-keyed standard, or drive weapons by physics. This is the rewrite you said you don't want, and it repeats the rounds' mistake: changing motion you haven't judged.

The aim in one sentence. Every blow you throw is answered by the creature, the view, your ears and the weapon's own weight, and the answer always matches what the blade did.

Why B. The measurements say the swings are not where the gap is. The gap is in the 250 ms around each hit and in everything that isn't a swing. B closes that without touching a key.

What would change my mind. If your play of the merged round flags many of the swings themselves, the weapon-by-weapon pass (stage 6) grows and comes sooner. If your A/B says you prefer today's dead stop, the settle is dropped, not tuned.

4Weight in the freeze and after it

Evidence
In the freeze the weapon is still. After the cut it stops dead within 60 ms and holds at speed 0.00.
Change
Three small layers drawn over the same keys, in your own game only:
The shake. While a landed blow is frozen, the weapon shudders along the cut and dies away by the end of the freeze. About 5 pixels for daggers, 9 for a blade, 13 for the Maul on a 1080p screen.
The settle. Where the blade stops after a cut, it carries a little past and comes back once, with no bounce. Quick and small for daggers (it peaks in 0.05 s and is gone by 0.2), slower and bigger for the Maul (0.1 s, gone by 0.5). Only along the way the blade was already going.
The living hold. While a strike waits for your next press, and at rest, the hands breathe: under 3 mm, once every four or five seconds.
You get
The hit feels like it met something, and each weapon stops like its own weight.
Keeps
Every key and every timing. A press still shows nothing for four ticks. The server knows nothing about it.
Risk
This is the one that could go wrong. You once called the katana floaty, and on 9 Nov a dip on the way home read as a glitch. So it is built for the Longsword and the Maul only, behind a key that flips between today's and the new one, and you choose. If you choose today's, the layer is removed.
Test
Your A/B. Pure-maths tests hold each layer to its size, to nothing at all when switched off, and to no movement on a hand that is already at rest.

8 · How it is kept safe

The rules every change is held to

  • One side decides. Every new layer is drawn by your game from what the hands already decided. No message from the server changes what is on screen. The blow's direction is baked once and both sides read the same number.
  • No float clocks. The layers run on the hands' own double clock, the one fixed on 9 Nov.
  • A press still shows nothing for four ticks. Not touched.
  • A hand at rest stays at rest. The settle acts only where the blade was moving. The breath is under 3 mm and slower than one in four seconds; the 8 Hz tremble you called a glitch is the line it stays far from.
  • Every layer has a switch in the arms settings. Off is today's picture, frame for frame, and a test holds that. Reduced motion halves or removes each one.
  • Nothing is told by sound alone. Every new sound comes with something you see.
  • Each stage is its own merge. If one reads wrong, it comes out without the others.
