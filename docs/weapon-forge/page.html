Awaken · weapons plan · waiting for your yes

The War Anvil

Weapons stop coming off the crafting grid. You forge each one yourself at a new anvil at home, in first person, with your own hands: the tongs in one hand, the hammer in the other. Then you draw the finished blade out of the stone and raise it.

  1. The anvil. Soot-black iron with a long horn. The hot blank lies on its face.
  2. The slot. Lit orange from inside. Your finished weapon stands in it, hilt up, glowing, until you draw it out.
  3. The trough. Cut into the stone's side for the quench. It steams while the work is hot.
  4. The peg. The hammer and tongs hang here, and they leave the peg while they're in your hands.
  5. The stone. A carved dark block. At night the slot and the steam light the room.

What you asked for

"The weapons system needs it's own cinematic crafting progression similar to what the armour has now."

So the bar is the armour's: a played scene, quality worth chasing, a place in the Hearth. But the station, the camera, the acts and the sound are the weapon's own, not a copy of the Forge Song.

Why weapons feel flat today

  • Making one takes half a second. You lay the blade, a part, a binding and the Blueprint in a crafting grid.
  • Improving one is a click. Tempering and attuning are notice windows at the Forge. Raise is a button in the Armoury.
  • Weapons have no place at home. The Armoury room is built around the Fitting Stand.
  • The Weapons tab on the Tasks page is empty. Nothing gives it steps.

What already works stays: Hale's first weapon, patterns from gates, stands, villages and people, mastery, Marks, weaves, and the Lord-forged drop with its pillar and ring.

The War Anvil

  • Made from three stone, a copper ingot and a log, at level 3. It needs no iron, so it's yours from the start.
  • What it needs near it. E weapons are made by hand on the anvil alone. From D, your Hearth's Forge must be within six blocks. From C, a grindstone too. The anvil says plainly what's missing, and nothing is taken until you start.
  • Use it with an empty hand for the list of what you can make. Holding a weapon, to temper, attune or reforge it. Sneak to draw out whatever stands in the slot.
  • It counts as an anvil for the Fitting Stand, so one smithy serves both your armour and your weapons.

The scene, through your eyes

The view stays at your eyes and turns to frame the work. When the work goes to the Forge, the view glides there and back; you never move. Your hands stay on screen the whole time. Anyone watching sees you hammering at the anvil.

HeatThe blank goes into the Forge in your tongs. The glow runs from the tip toward the tongs, dull red to yellow. Pull it out when the glow reaches the tongs.Too early and your strikes count a little less. Too late and the tip comes out burnt.
Draw outStrike on the beat, aiming at the glowing mark. Sparks fly, and the blade lengthens strike by strike, from a squat glowing bar to the weapon's shape. Halfway the tongs flip it over.8 to 14 strikes, on the weapon's own rhythm (below).
QuenchHold to plunge it in the trough. Steam roars, then hisses. A dark line climbs the blade toward a gold notch near the guard: let go when it gets there.Early, the steel is soft. Late, it's brittle.
HiltThree taps on the beat. The guard slides on, the grip is wrapped, the pommel is peened.
The drawYou stand it in the stone, then hold to pull it out. Steel scrapes on stone and rises into the blade's own note. You raise it before your eyes, its sheen runs from hilt to tip, and a card names it: "Beastbound Longsword · Masterwork · Forged by Euan".
First cutIf you like, strike once. With a Target Dummy nearby the view turns to it and the dummy rocks. It deals no damage.

Nothing can fail. Skill only sets how good the weapon is. A first forge takes 25 to 45 seconds, and a weapon you've made at Masterwork can be forged again from memory in about 8.

Every weapon forges to its own fighting rhythm

The hammer keeps the beat of the style's attack chain, so the anvil teaches you a weapon's tempo before you fight with it. And each clean strike rings the blade a step nearer its own note: a Maul low, Daggers high. Pick one and press play.

A rough preview with a plain tone. The game's sound is real struck steel.

Each rank adds one act

So the forging grows with you instead of repeating.

EKnap and bind. By hand, no Forge: strike flakes off a bone or flint edge, then wind the cord round the grip in even circles. About 20 seconds.
DThe whole scene above, in iron.
CGrind. The view glides to the grindstone. Turn the wheel and keep the edge on the line as it drifts, while sparks shower toward you.
BBalance. The blade rests on the anvil's horn and tips. Slide the Deepglass pommel weight until it settles level; it hums pure when the blade is true.
ATwist. The netherite bar is clamped in the anvil. Turn it three even circles, and a well-twisted blade shows ripples in its steel.
SStarlight. No water: a column of white light comes down onto the anvil. Hold the blade up into it as its ring closes on the point, and the runes wake.

The smithy grows with you (new, decision 15)

You said the forge should upgrade over time. So the War Anvil is rebuilt as you climb, and the room around it fills in. Each rebuild is what lets you make that rank's weapons, so it takes the place of a gate we already have; it isn't a second ladder. Each one is a step on the Weapons tab.

EThe War Anvil, as it is now: carved stone and a copper-banded horn. E weapons by hand.
DBellows go up by your Forge when you fire it to Tier II, as now. A kin beast can work them for you: then the heat climbs evenly and the gold holds a moment longer.
CIronbound. The anvil's face is re-laid in iron and a grindstone is set beside it, for the grind. Iron blocks and diamonds. This is the C gate we already have (a grindstone near), made part of the anvil.
BThe Deepglass horn. The horn is re-forged in Deepglass, and it hums. The balance happens on it. A weapon rack goes up for what you've forged.
AThe Lord's anvil. Rebuilt on a Lord's Heart and netherite, in that Lord's colour. The twist is clamped in it, and at S the starlight comes down onto it.

Tiers still open at the same levels, and your Forge's tier still sets how far a weapon can be tempered. There's no ceiling on quality: a perfect song is always Flawless, whatever the anvil looks like.

What skill gives you

  • Plain, Fine, Masterwork or Flawless, the same as armour. It sets the weapon's starting temper, from none up to III, but never above what your Forge could temper it to. Skill saves you materials; it never makes a weapon stronger than you could buy.
  • Flaws show. A burnt tip is darker, a brittle blade has a hairline. They only change the look, and a flawed weapon can't be Flawless.
  • A friend helps. Anyone else at your anvil can strike the off-beats. Three helpers make it twin-struck.
  • Every weapon has a story in its tooltip: "Masterwork, twin-struck. Forged by Euan."

Where it all comes from

  • Patterns come from the same places as now: gates, stands, villages, people and Hale. A Blueprint teaches its style when you use it; it no longer goes into the recipe.
  • Materials are exactly what each recipe costs today, minus the Blueprint. A tier blade you're carrying (a Fang, a Cleaver...) stands in for its parts, so Hale's spare Fang still counts.
  • Tiers open at the same levels. What you hammer follows the tier: hide and bone, iron, diamond, Deepglass, netherite, starmetal. The sound and the glow follow the metal.
  • Lord-forged weapons stay drops, exactly as rare as now, with their reveal. At home the anvil reforges them in their Lord's colour, with its sigil glowing in the steel.
  • At home, the War Anvil belongs in the Armoury room, and a weapon made there lasts a quarter longer. The Weapons tab gets real steps, starting with "Set up a War Anvil in your Armoury".

Vanilla stays vanilla (new, decision 14)

"Maybe we keep the vanilla ones normal, since those are kind of the quick tools... if you want the stronger and more interesting ones, you need to go through this path."

So vanilla swords, axes, tools and armour stay as they are, crafted on the grid. For that to read clearly, a forged weapon has to beat the vanilla one at every point. I checked every style at every tier against the real numbers. Today it doesn't:

  • Tempering makes it worse. Today a vanilla sword tempers up to VI exactly like ours, so a tempered netherite sword (14) out-hits an untempered A blade.
  • The fix. Every forged weapon gets 1 more damage at every tier. The eight slower or longer ones (axe, maul, greatblade, scythe, glaive, spear, warstaff, chainblade) get 2. C and B last longer than diamond and netherite (1,700 and 2,200 uses, up from 1,400 and 1,900). Vanilla gear no longer takes Temper, and what's already tempered keeps it. With that, all 16 styles beat the vanilla sword or axe of their own metal, and match or beat the next metal you could get early. The three close calls left are at E, and each is a trade: daggers hit lighter but far faster, and the warstaff swings faster than an axe.
  • Enchanting already agrees. Vanilla gear holds one enchantment; a forged weapon holds one to four, by how well you forged it.
  • Fights stay tuned. The fights were tuned against a player with a diamond sword at D, which is what a D weapon becomes. The combat simulator checks every Lord again before it lands.
  • Said once, in plain words, and never nagged: Hale: "A crafting table makes quick tools. For a real weapon, set up a War Anvil at home." The Weapons tab's first step: "The War Anvil makes weapons stronger than anything from a crafting table." The anvil's tooltip: "Forges weapons stronger than any crafted sword or axe." Nothing comments on the vanilla gear you carry.

Your saves

Every weapon you already have keeps working. Its tooltip gains a Plain story the first time you hold it. Blueprints you carry still teach their style, and nothing is charged twice. The weapon recipes leave the crafting grid.

What else it touches

  • Enchanting. That's the enchanting session's, and we've agreed the line. The forge makes the metal: tiers, quality, tempering, attunement, Marks. Enchanting writes into a finished weapon or armour piece at its own station. There's no enchanting step inside the forge scene. Enchanting reads how well something was forged, so a Masterwork blade has more room for enchantments, and it may add one line to the forge's result card, like "Room for 3 inscriptions".
  • Armour. One line, so the War Anvil counts as the Fitting Stand's anvil. The armour's forge camera may be moved into a shared class both forges use, with the armour's behaviour unchanged and tested.
  • Fights. No moves or combat rules change. Damage numbers change only if you say yes to decision 14.
