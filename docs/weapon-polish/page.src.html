What you asked for

Keep the language, lose the fat

"Some of them are a bit too chunky and don't look that good in the hand. Some of the blades to me look a bit fat. Most are still really good, like the Maul... the Katana is decent, but I think can still be slightly improved... I don't want to completely scrap everything."
"It has to kind of stay that voxel style. Just like extremely well polished voxel style."

How I'll know it worked

  • In first person a blade reads slim: a thin edge, a clear line down the middle, a point that steps down finely.
  • Every piece is a box. It looks like something Minecraft could have made, only better finished.
  • Each weapon is plainly still the same weapon, and the Maul is untouched.

What would be a failure

  • Any shape Minecraft doesn't have: a slanted face, a wedge, a smooth taper. That was the first version, and you turned it down.
  • Blades so thin they lose their weight.
  • A change you can't see in the hand.
What I found

Every blade has a hammer's body

The weapons' look was built from one reference picture, and that picture is a hammer. The Maul is that hammer, which is why it is the best one. Every blade was then built by the same rules, so every blade got a hammer's mass. A Longsword's blade today is 5 wide with a square post 3 wide and 3 deep down its middle. That post is what reads as fat.

Why you see it more now

On 9 November you asked for swords to be held like a vanilla sword, edge ahead. That was right, and it is in the build. It also means the eye now looks straight at the blade's thickness. Before that, the flat was turned toward you and hid it.

Two of your weapons already have the right cut

The Rapier and the Gatewrought Daggers are built as a thin slab with a one-texel ridge. They are the blades that already read slim in the hand. The plan gives every other blade that same cut.

Two things I checked and ruled out. The weapons are not too big: at mid-blade a Longsword is pixels across on a 1280 by 720 screen and a vanilla diamond sword is . And the hilts are not the problem any more: the 2 November pass already brought them down to 5 deep.

Why B

  • It takes out the post and keeps a spine, so the blade is slim without looking like paper. C is slimmer still, and it is a fair choice if you want blades as thin as vanilla's.
  • It is already in your game on the Rapier, so it is your language, not a new one.
  • The point steps down over more texels and ends one texel square, as a pixel-art sword does.
  • No new tools. The model toolkit builds it today.

What it costs

  • It is a quieter change than the one you rejected. A blade gets slimmer and cleaner. It does not become a different object.
  • From the front, in the Hall and in the icon, most weapons look almost the same. The change is in the hand and from the edge.
  • Blades show more of their flat, so their patterns (flint flakes, scales, the oil sheen) are seen more.

What stays the same

The promises

Never changes

  • The voxel style. Boxes at whole-texel sizes, and the turned boxes the weapons already use for stones, antlers and curved blades. Nothing else.
  • Every weapon's name, id, damage, speed, uses, recipe and where you find it.
  • Its length, its grip and which side its edge is on. I measured every changed weapon: each end is within half a texel of today's.
  • How it is held and every swing. The weapon-feel plan needs nothing re-done because of this one.
  • Hilts, guards, stones, hafts, chains, glows and the light that lifts off the high ranks.

Changes

  • Straight blades: the post becomes a slab one texel thick with a one-texel ridge (three wide on the Greatblades).
  • Points: longer, in finer steps, ending one texel square.
  • Curved blades (Glaive, Scythe): a thin flat with a thick back along the spine.
  • The Katana: an even curve, one texel thick, a stepped point.
  • The Shieldblade's sword slims from 6 wide to the Longsword's 5, its lit groove becoming its ridge.

Left alone on purpose: the Maul, War-staff, Precursor Arm and Gauntlets (block-shaped, and good), the Rapier (it already has the cut), the Axes (thinning the bit set its teeth and caps fighting, and the head is good), and the Chainblades' tusk hooks.

How each stage is checked

  • The model checks that exist today. They already pass on every draft here: nothing outside the item box, no two faces fighting over one plane, every glow on the weapon.
  • The full test suite on CI.
  • A look tour in the real game, every shot read for clipping, floating parts and wrong scale before you see it.
  • Every weapon's ends measured against today's.

Undo, cost and risk

  • Only generated files change (models, textures, icons). One commit per kind, so any kind can be put back alone.
  • About 1 hour for Stage 0 and 3 for the rest. There is no new tooling this time: it is the weapon file's own numbers.
  • Main risk: it is too quiet a change for you, or a blade reads too thin in play. Stage 0 finds that out on two weapons.
  • The build is about to get the new K menu and Hall. I merge that in first.
