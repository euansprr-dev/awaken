What you asked for

Keep the language, lose the fat

"Some of them are a bit too chunky and don't look that good in the hand. Some of the blades to me look a bit fat. Most are still really good, like the Maul... the Katana is decent, but I think can still be slightly improved... I don't want to completely scrap everything."

How I'll know it worked

  • In first person a blade reads as a blade at a glance: thin at the edge, a clear point, one clean line of light down it.
  • Each weapon is plainly still the same weapon. Same outline, same materials, same hilt.
  • The Maul and the other block-shaped weapons are untouched.

What would be a failure

  • Thinner, but still a stack of boxes.
  • A new look that loses the chunky painted feel. You have turned down two looks before: plain cubes ("still very, very blocky") and smooth sculpted meshes ("too thin and noisy"). This plan stays between them.
What I found

Every blade has a hammer's body

The weapons' look was built from one reference picture, and that picture is a hammer. The Maul is that hammer, which is why it is the best one. Every blade was then built by the same rules, so every blade got a hammer's mass. A Longsword's blade today is 5 wide with a square post 3 wide and 3 deep down its middle. That post is what reads as fat.

Why you see it more now

On 9 November you asked for swords to be held like a vanilla sword, edge ahead. That was right, and it is in the build. It also means the eye now looks straight at the blade's thickness. Before that, the flat was turned toward you and hid it.

The second cause: steps

Points, axe bits and spear heads are stacks of boxes, so they end in stairs. A stepped tip reads blunt however thin the blade is.

Two things I checked and ruled out. The weapons are not too big: at mid-blade a Longsword is pixels across on a 1280 by 720 screen and a vanilla diamond sword is . And the hilts are not the problem any more: the 2 November pass already brought them down to 5 deep.

Three ways to fix it

Grind the blades

The same Gatewrought Longsword four ways, at your first-person view, then upright from the front and from the edge.

Today

Three stacked planks and a stepped tip.

A. Thinner boxes

The post made 2 deep. Cheapest. You can hardly tell.

B. Ground blades

A flat, a real bevel each side to a knife edge, a true point. My recommendation.

C. Flat, like vanilla

One texel thick. Loses all its weight and still has stairs.

Why B

  • It is the only one that removes the stacked planks and gives a real point.
  • It keeps the same pixel size, the same paint, the same hilts. Only the blade's cut changes.
  • The blade becomes one clean plane that catches the light, so it reads sharp when it moves.
  • The models get lighter, not heavier: the Longsword goes from 248 triangles to 188.

What it costs, and the guard rails

  • One new piece in the model toolkit: a slanted face. Everything else is the box toolkit you have now.
  • The risk is drifting toward the sculpted look you rejected. So: flat faces only, never smoothed; one bevel a side; no new kinds of detail; hilts, stones, hafts and chains untouched.
  • A fourth way, re-sculpting the weapons, would scrap the language. I did not draft it.

What stays the same

The promises

Never changes

  • Every weapon's name, id, damage, speed, uses, recipe and where you find it.
  • Its length, its grip and which side its edge is on. I measured all 82: every end is within half a texel of today's.
  • How it is held and every swing. The weapon-feel plan needs nothing re-done because of this one.
  • Hilts, guards, stones, hafts, chains, glows and the light that lifts off the high ranks.

Changes

  • The blade's cut: a flat, a bevel, a knife edge, a point.
  • Axe bits become wedges. Spear heads become real leaves and spires.
  • Curved blades (Katana, Glaive, Scythe, tusks) become one smooth curve with no joints.
  • The Shieldblade's sword slims from 6 wide to the Longsword's 5, with its lit groove painted into the flat.
  • Icons are redrawn from the new models, so they gain the points too.

How each stage is checked

  • The model checks that exist today, plus new ones for slanted faces (no gaps, no faces fighting over one plane, no stretched pixels).
  • The full test suite on CI.
  • A look tour in the real game, every shot read for clipping, floating parts and wrong scale before you see it.
  • Every weapon's ends measured against today's.

Undo, cost and risk

  • Only generated files change (models, textures, icons). One commit per kind, so any kind can be put back alone.
  • About 2 hours for Stage 0 and 4 for the rest. The drafts on this page already exist.
  • Main risk: it reads like the sculpted look in play. Stage 0 is there to find that out on two weapons, not eighty.
  • The build is about to get the new K menu and Hall. I merge that in first.
