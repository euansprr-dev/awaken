## Scope
1. **Roads** between Carillon, the Wayfarer camp and the settlements: paths in each region's own blocks (dirt and gravel
   in the Commons, cobble and stone near towns, sand and sandstone in Sunscorch, planks over the Mire), following the land
   (no road over a cliff, bridges over rivers, cuttings through ridges kept small), waymarkers at forks with the next
   place's name, lanterns near settlements; planned per region pair from the region map and painted per chunk at world
   generation like every structure (new chunks only).
2. **Inns** about a day's walk apart on long roads: a bed (respawn once), a meal, the night's rumours, a stable.
3. **Caravans on roads**: the World Pulse's Caravan happening walks a real road between two settlements instead of open
   ground; a village's trade and news arrive with it; one on your road is worth defending. At **Tier IV**, roads to your
   Hearth's village get caravans too (W6's hook, if merged; otherwise leave one clearly marked call site).
4. **Towns** (25-45 homes, 60-120 people): about one region in five, never in the Commons; walled with a gate and the
   Watch, a market square and stalls, an inn, a scribe, a bell tower, several districts' worth of styles; the first town a
   player meets seen from a pass. W3's planner scales up; W0's engine runs it within its budgets.
5. **Mining towns**: at a mountain's foot over their own mine (a real cave set piece: a shaft head with a winch, ladders,
   rope, galleries down to a lode), selling ore and lode maps (read with lapis), the miners' day down the shaft.
6. **The Deep Mine** trouble (PLAN 6.3), on W4's trouble framework: the town's mine broke into a seep or a Hollows nest; the
   mine bell; miners at the shaft head; go down the town's mine (lifts, ladders, rope, a cave-in on the way out: W2's
   cave-ins); bring the miners up; the mine reopens (ore and lode maps for sale), or it's sealed for good if nobody came.
7. **The Atlas** shows roads you've walked and the settlements you've found (through its hook).
